# Answers 2

## 1. Does the process also start when someone other than the person who had the device reports the loss, for example a colleague, or the company itself finding a device in the equipment register that nobody can account for and no employee has reported?

Yes, in all those ways. The process starts whenever anyone cannot account for a device: the person who had it, a colleague who notices it is gone, Marius Tangen finding a device in the equipment register that nobody can account for, and a report from outside the company such as a hotel, a train company, the police or a finder. Whoever notices tells the head of IT the same hour. It is not the employee's admission that starts the process but the fact that the device cannot be accounted for, and a device nobody has reported missing is treated exactly like one an employee reported.

## 2. Where the lost device is the employee's own, used for work, does the process go beyond what the company does in its own systems, so that the insurer is told and the company gives the employee a replacement device, or does it stop there?

It stops at the company's own systems, with two additions. The company does not locate, lock or wipe a device it does not own, does not claim on its insurer for it and does not replace it; the employee claims on their own insurance and reports the theft to the police themselves, and Marius Tangen helps them with what the company knows. The two additions: the employee's sessions, password and tokens are handled exactly as for a company device, and where the employee needs a device to work, Marius Tangen gives them a company laptop within 2 working days, which is the company equipping them and not a replacement of their own device. The record says that the device was private, what the employee says was on it and that the company could not reach it.

## 3. Where a lost device is locked and encrypted, had its screen locked, was signed in to no company system and was only mislaid, so that none of the grounds for a wipe is met, but nobody is looking for it any more: is it wiped, and how long after the report?

Yes, it is wiped, 72 hours after the report, and that is an outer limit and not a question to be reopened. A device that has not been recovered within 72 hours of the report is wiped whether or not a ground for an earlier wipe was met, and Marius Tangen does it without asking again. Where it is recovered inside those 72 hours, it is not wiped, and Marius Tangen checks the device and says it is safe before the employee uses it again.

## 4. What is done for a lost company device that device management cannot reach, such as a memory stick or an external disk with company data on it, which can be neither located nor locked nor wiped from there?

The steps on the device fall away, because there is nothing to reach, and the process says so instead of leaving the reader stuck: write in the record that the device cannot be reached from device management and why. Everything else is done, and two things are done differently. The data on it is treated as exposed from the first minute, not as possibly exposed, unless the person who had it says the disk was encrypted and Marius Tangen can confirm from the equipment register or the purchase that it was an encrypted device; where that cannot be confirmed, it counts as unencrypted. And where any password, key or token was stored on the device, Marius Tangen changes or revokes it in every system it could reach, within 2 hours of the report, beyond the employee's own sessions and tokens. Kari Nordmann decides the breach question knowing the data is treated as exposed.

## 5. Where neither Kari Nordmann nor Henrik Aas can be reached within 4 hours of the report and the device held personal data, is the device wiped without the data protection officer being asked, or does the wipe wait until one of them can be reached?

The device is wiped. Marius Tangen decides it alone where a ground for a wipe is met, and he does not wait: protecting the data is the point of the wipe, and asking the data protection officer is advice on it, not permission for it. He writes in the record that he could not reach either of them, what he tried and when, and tells Kari Nordmann, or Henrik Aas, at the first hour one of them is reachable. She may then say in the record that she would have advised otherwise, and that stands as a finding for the next version of this process rather than as something to undo.

## 6. Where there is no employee behind the lost device, for example a laptop that someone who has left the company never handed back, whose sessions are ended, who gives the facts that only the employee can give, who is told before the wipe, who reports a theft to the police and who gets a replacement?

Marius Tangen answers for the device in the employee's place, and the steps that need a person are filled in like this. The sessions, password and tokens of the account the device last used are ended by Marius Tangen, and where the person has left the company and their account was already closed, he writes in the record that it was closed and when. Nora Lind gives what the company knows in place of the employee: who had the device, when they left, what they handed back and what they said about it then. Nobody is told before the wipe, because there is nobody to tell, so the device is wiped at once rather than after any waiting time. Marius Tangen reports a theft to the police himself with the serial number from the equipment register. No replacement is given, and the register records the device as lost. Where the person who had it has not left the company and simply cannot be reached, the device is still handled this way, and Marius Tangen asks them for the facts as soon as they can be reached and adds what they say to the record.

## 7. On what legal ground does the company process the employee's own personal data in this process, namely where and when their device last contacted the company and the entries in the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud that show what their account reached: the company's legitimate interest in protecting its systems and the data it holds, a legal obligation it must meet, or something else?

On Article 6(1)(f), the company's legitimate interest in protecting its systems and the personal data of pupils, teachers, employees and job applicants that it holds, and, from the moment Kari Nordmann decides that the loss is a personal data breach, also on Article 6(1)(c), the legal obligation of Articles 33 and 34 to establish what happened. The processing is limited to what the loss needs: the device's own entries in device management, and the entries of that one account in the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud, in the window from when the device went missing to when its access was blocked. It is not used to see how the employee works, what they worked on or where they were at other times, and no conclusion about their performance or conduct may be drawn from it. Where more than that window is needed, Kari Nordmann decides what is looked at and why, and writes it in the record.

## 8. Is the employee who lost the device told what the company reads about them while it handles the loss, namely where and when the device last contacted the company and the log entries of their account in the office suite and in Nordsky Cloud, and why it is read, and at what point in the process are they told?

Yes, and they are told early, not afterwards. Marius Tangen tells them within 4 hours of the report, in the same answer in which he asks them for the facts, and says in plain language: that he looks at where and when the device last contacted the company and at the entries of their account in the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud, for the window from when the device went missing until its access was blocked; that this is to find out what could have been reached and nothing else; that it is not used to judge how they work; that what he finds goes into the record of the case, which is kept 3 years, or 5 where the loss was also a personal data breach; and that Kari Nordmann is the data protection officer they can ask about it. The routine for lost or stolen equipment in the staff handbook says the same, so that no employee meets it for the first time on the day they lose a laptop.

## 9. Who starts the process `Handle a personal data breach` once Kari Nordmann has decided that the loss is a personal data breach?

Kari Nordmann starts it, in the same hour she decides it, by telling Marius Tangen and Ingrid Solheim that it is a personal data breach and that the breach process now runs. Marius Tangen then holds both processes, as he is the one who takes every report in the breach process, and he keeps one record and one timeline for the two. Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold starts it when she is away. The moment the loss reached the company is the moment the company became aware, so the 72 hours of the breach process count from it and not from her decision, and the record says both times so that nobody has to reconstruct them later.

## 10. How long is the entry in the equipment register kept that says which employee had the lost device, which replacement they were given and the date the lost device was marked as lost?

3 years from the day the case is closed, which is as long as the record of the loss, and 5 years where the loss was also a personal data breach, so that the register and the record never disagree. Marius Tangen then deletes the entry's personal part, which is which employee had the device, and keeps the device, its serial number and the dates without a name for as long as the company needs them for its accounts and its insurance. The entry for a device that was returned rather than lost is kept 3 years after the return.
