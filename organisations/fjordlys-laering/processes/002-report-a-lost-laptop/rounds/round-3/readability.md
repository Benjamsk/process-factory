# readability review of draft-4.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Steps 10 and 13: step 13 says the telling goes out "in the same answer in which you ask them for the facts", but the asking is step 10, so the reader who does the steps in their order has already sent the question before they reach step 13 and cannot comply; the telling must stand in step 10 where the person is asked, or step 10 must say that the question goes out only together with what step 13 lists.
- [high] Step 15: the wipe grounds "it was not encrypted" and "the screen was not locked when it went missing" cannot be decided where step 10 recorded that fact as `not established`, and the draft does not say which way to decide then; it must say which way an unestablished fact counts, as the fifth paragraph of `When this applies` does for a device device management cannot reach. spec.md does not say this either.
- [medium] Steps 10 and 11: the limit on what may be read belongs to the reading in step 10 but comes only after it in step 11, so the reader has already opened device management and the logs before they are told to read only the device's own entries, that one account's entries and only the window from when the device went missing until its access was blocked; the limit must stand with the action in step 10.
- [medium] Step 15: the step does not say what to do where neither Kari Nordmann nor Henrik Aas can be reached within the 4 hours; the answer stands only under `When to hand over` ("Marius Tangen decides the wipe alone where a ground for a wipe is met"), while the same case for the breach question has its own step 19. Step 15 must say it.
- [medium] Step 2, and the on-call developer's duty under `When to hand over`: the reader is told to "open the record of the case" and to write into it without being told what the record is or where it is kept; that stands only under `What to record` ("Marius Tangen keeps it in the office suite, in the folder he makes for the case"), which the reader who is not Marius Tangen reaches too late. Step 2 must say what the record is and where it is made.
- [medium] Steps 4 to 6, step 15, step 20, step 23, `When this applies` and `Who must be told`: the support desk, the reading app, the equipment register and Datatilsynet are used without being explained where the reader first meets them, although the draft does explain the office suite ("which is Microsoft 365") and device management ("which is Microsoft Intune"); each must be explained the same way at first use.
- [medium] Steps 12, 18 and 20: the step starts with one role and name and then gives the action to someone else (head of people Nora Lind gives what the company knows in step 12; Marius Tangen holds both processes and keeps one record in step 18; Marius Tangen reports the theft and the person reports their own device in step 20), so the reader cannot tell from the start of the step whether it is theirs; each action needs its own step starting with the role and the name of the person who does it.
- [medium] Step 12: "answer for the device in their place" asks the reader for no action they can do, only for a result; the step must name the actions, as it does for Nora Lind ("who had the device, when they left, what they handed back and what they said about it then").
- [medium] Step 20: the step does not say who writes the police case number into the record ("the case number is written into the record"); it must name the person who writes it.
- [medium] Steps 3, 10, 13, 16, 22 and 23: each step asks for two or more actions, so the reader cannot work through them one at a time: step 3 locate the device and lock it; step 10 get the facts from device management, get them from the person who had the device, get them from the logs, and write each into the record; step 13 tell the person the five things and ask them for the facts; step 16 reach the person, tell them the device will be wiped, ask them what exists only on it and write their answer into the record; step 22 give the replacement laptop, give the access back and tell Nora Lind; step 23 enter the replacement in the equipment register and mark the lost device as lost. Each action needs its own step.
- [medium] Step 16: "Do not hold the wipe back longer than 1 hour because they cannot be reached" does not say from what the hour is counted, from the decision in step 15 or from the first attempt to reach the person; it must say. spec.md does not say this either.
- [low] Step 19: "where you cannot establish that the loss is not a personal data breach, decide that it is one" negates twice and has to be read again; it can say that the loss counts as a personal data breach unless you can establish that it is not one.
- [low] Step 19: "She looks at it again then and may decide that it is not a personal data breach after all" comes after "tell Kari Nordmann, or Henrik Aas", so "She" can point to either, and the reader cannot tell whether Henrik Aas may also decide it again; the sentence must name who looks at it again.
- [low] Step 24: "the person does not use it before you have" tells the reader what to bring about but not as an instruction to them; it can say to tell the person not to use the device until you have said it is safe to use again.
- [low] `When this applies` and `When to hand over`: sentences give the reader background they do not need in order to act, among them "that is the company equipping them and not a replacement of their own device", "Finding the device again does not end it", "neither waits for the other" and "that stands as a finding for the next version of this document rather than as something to undo".
