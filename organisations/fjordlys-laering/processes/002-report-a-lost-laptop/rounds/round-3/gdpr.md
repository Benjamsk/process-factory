# gdpr review of draft-4.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] `When this applies` (the paragraph on times), step 18, step 19 and `Who must be told` (the last bullet): the draft never says when the 72 hours of GDPR Articles 33 and 34 start. It says only that "every time in this process counts from the moment the loss reached the company", and the only 72 hours it names is the outer limit for the wipe in steps 17 and 24, while step 18 and the bullet on Datatilsynet say that `Handle a personal data breach` "runs from" the moment Kari Nordmann decides. A reader therefore counts the 72 hours for notifying Datatilsynet from her decision, which can be 4 hours or more after the loss reached the company, and in a case where Marius Tangen or Henrik Aas decides late, later still. The draft must say what `spec.md` says in its `Deadlines` section: that the 72 hours of `Handle a personal data breach` count from the moment the loss reached the company and not from the decision that the loss was a personal data breach, because that moment is the moment the company became aware.
- [low] Step 13: the draft drops the sentence of `spec.md` step 13 that the routine for lost or stolen equipment in the staff handbook says the same thing, so that no employee meets it for the first time on the day they lose a device. GDPR Article 13 wants the person informed when the data is collected, not only afterwards; with that sentence the step rests on information the employee already has, and the draft should say it.
