# readability review of draft-9.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [medium] Steps 18, 23 and 24: the condition that these steps fall away stands only under `When this applies` ("The steps on the device fall away because there is nothing to reach") and at steps 4 and 6, not at the steps themselves. Step 18 excludes only the person's own device, and steps 23 and 24 exclude nothing, so the reader with a memory stick or an external disk is told to decide the wipe and to wipe a device device management cannot reach, and the reader at step 24 is told to wipe a device that has not been recovered within 72 hours "whether or not a ground for an earlier wipe was met" even where that device is the person's own, which `When this applies` and step 18 forbid. Steps 18, 23 and 24 must each carry the same two exclusions as steps 4 and 6.
- [medium] Step 22: the step asks for two writes at two different times, and the second one can only be done after the next step. Name the actions: keep "write into the record what the person said exists only on the device" in step 22, and put "write into the record what work existed only on the device and is lost" in its own step after step 23.
- [medium] Step 30: "The police case number goes into the record" does not say who puts it there. The step is the person who had the device's, and the draft gives the record to head of IT Marius Tangen (step 2 and `What to record`), so the reader cannot tell whether they write it themselves or give the number to Marius Tangen. The step must say who does it. `spec.md` does not say who either.
- [medium] Step 8, second bullet: "the contract archive" is used without being explained where the reader first meets it, unlike device management, the office suite, Nordsky Cloud, the support desk and the equipment register, which the draft does explain. The step must say what the contract archive is.
- [medium] `Who does what` (on-call developer and data protection officer), `When to hand over` (second, third and fourth bullets) and `If something does not fit`: the reader is told to telephone Marius Tangen "on his mobile" and Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold "on the firm's emergency number", but the draft never says where either number is to be found, while it does say that the on-call telephone number is in the staff handbook. The draft must say where the reader finds these two numbers. Neither `spec.md` nor the organisation files say where they are.
- [medium] `Who does what`, on-call developer, and `When to hand over`, third bullet: Lise Berntsen is named as the stand-in for the week beginning 23 November 2026, but no step and no hand-over rule says when she is telephoned, and `When to hand over` sends the reader who cannot reach Erik Moen straight to Marius Tangen. The draft must say whether the reader tries Lise Berntsen and at what point, or not name her. `spec.md` names her as the stand-in but gives her no duty either.
- [medium] Step 19: the reader cannot tell whether the device "is being looked for", because no step of the draft asks anyone to look for the device and the draft does not say who would. The same step then sends the reader whose device is not being looked for to `If something does not fit`, which sends anything about the devices to head of IT Marius Tangen, who is the reader of step 19 himself, so the reader is sent to himself and still does not know what to do before step 24. Step 19 must say what the reader does in that outcome. `spec.md` neither says who looks for the device nor what happens where it is not being looked for.
- [medium] `When this applies`, fifth paragraph, and step 28: "personal data breach" is used throughout the draft and step 28 asks head of IT Marius Tangen to decide whether the loss is one, but the term is never explained where the reader first meets it. The draft must explain it. `spec.md` does not explain it either.
- [medium] `When this applies`, last paragraph, step 2 and steps 3 to 32: the moment every deadline counts from has three names: "the moment the loss reached the company", "the time the report reached the company" and "the report". The reader has to work out that they are the same moment before they can count 1 hour, 2 hours or 4 hours. Use one name throughout, or say in `When this applies` that the report is the moment the loss reached the company.
- [low] Step 17: "answer for it in their place" makes the reader reread, because "it" is the device and "their" is the person's, and the step does not say which facts are answered for. Write it as the action the reader does, naming what they have to supply.
