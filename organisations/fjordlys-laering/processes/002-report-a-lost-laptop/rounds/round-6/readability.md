# readability review of draft-8.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Step 10, second source: it says "Ask them for this in the same message in which you tell them what step 13 lists", and step 13 says "in the same answer in which you ask them for the facts of step 10", so the reader cannot finish step 10 without first carrying out step 13 and the two steps point at each other; one step must hold both the telling and the asking, or step 10 must itself say what the message tells the person.
- [high] Steps 21 to 23: step 21 ends "Wipe the device one hour after that first attempt whether or not they have answered", step 22 asks for "after the wipe, what work existed only on the device and is lost", and step 23 says "Wipe the device in device management", so the reader cannot tell which step wipes the device and has to do step 23 before they can finish step 22; one step must order the wipe, and the record entry about the lost work must stand after it.
- [high] Steps 18 and 19: step 19 covers only the device that "is locked, encrypted and being looked for", so a device for which none of step 18's grounds is true but which nobody is looking for leaves the reader with no instruction at all until the 72 hours of step 24, and the draft nowhere says who looks for a lost device or how the reader can tell in the moment that it is being looked for.
- [medium] Steps 4, 6, 18 and 31: the exception "Not where the device is the person's own, and not where device management cannot reach the device", and in step 31 "Not where the device is the person's own", stands as a fragment after the action, so the reader locks, blocks, decides the wipe or tells the insurer before learning that the step does not apply to the case in front of them; the condition must come before the action and as a full sentence.
- [medium] Steps 5, 8, 21 and 34: each step asks for several actions at once, and each action needs its own step: step 5 ends the sessions, changes the password and revokes the tokens, in the office suite, in Nordsky Cloud and in the support desk; step 8 writes in the record, treats the data as exposed and changes or revokes every password, key and token; step 21 reaches the person, tells them, asks them, writes the time and wipes; step 34 writes in the record, undoes nothing, does not wipe, and checks the device and says it is safe.
- [medium] Step 8: "Treat the data on the device as exposed from the first minute, not as possibly exposed. Treat it otherwise only where the person who had the device says the disk was encrypted" asks for no action the reader can do, and no step of the draft says what the reader does differently when the data counts as exposed; the step must name the action that follows from it.
- [medium] Step 8: "you can confirm from the equipment register or the purchase that it was an encrypted device" never says what "the purchase" is, so the reader does not know what to look at; the organisation files call it the purchase invoice and keep it in the contract archive.
- [medium] Step 1, and "When to hand over" second point: whether the reader telephones head of IT Marius Tangen or on-call developer Erik Moen turns on "Outside working hours", but the draft never says what the working hours are; spec.md does not say either, and the organisation files give 08:00 to 16:00 Monday to Friday only for two roles that this document does not use.
- [medium] Steps 26 and 29: step 26 says Marius Tangen "keeps one record and one timeline for the two", and step 29 says the breach process "stops with that line in its own record", so the reader does not know whether to write that line in the one shared record or in a second record of the other process; the same process is also called `Handle a personal data breach` in step 26 and "the breach process" in steps 26 and 29.
- [medium] Step 28: the reader has to "decide that the loss is a personal data breach, unless you can establish from the facts that it is not", and steps 25 and 27 turn on the same term, but the draft never says what makes a loss a personal data breach; neither spec.md nor the organisation files say what it is either.
- [low] Step 12: it repeats word for word the limits already given in the third source of step 10, "Read only the entries of that one account, and only in the window from when the device went missing until its access was blocked" and "Do not use them to see how the person works, what they worked on or where they were at other times", so the reader stops to look for the action that is new in it, and only its last sentence, about Kari Nordmann deciding, is new.
- [low] "When this applies", fourth paragraph, and step 20 last sentence: "which is the company equipping them and not a replacement of their own device" and "that stands as a finding for the next version of this document rather than as something to undo" give the reader a reason and a consequence they do not need in order to act.
- [low] "When this applies" last paragraph but one, step 26, step 28 and the end of "Who must be told": the sentence that the 72 hours of `Handle a personal data breach` count from the moment the loss reached the company stands four times, and no deadline of this document depends on it.
- [low] "Who does what", data protection officer: "decides what is looked at where more than the window from when the device went missing until its access was blocked is needed" has to be read twice before the reader sees what she decides.
