# readability review of draft-10.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Step 13: the telling has to go out `in the same answer in which you ask them for the facts of step 10`, so the reader who does the steps in their order has already sent the step 10 request without it; step 10 must carry the telling, by saying there that the message asking the person for the facts also says what step 13 lists, or step 13 must come before step 10.
- [high] When this applies (the paragraph on a device device management cannot reach) and steps 4, 6, 8, 18, 23 and 25: `where device management cannot reach the device` can be read as a device that device management never held, such as a memory stick, and as a company laptop that is enrolled but is switched off or offline right now, and the two readings make the reader do different things, because on the second reading the lock, the block and the wipe all fall away for a stolen laptop while step 18 at the same time makes `it cannot be located` a ground for wiping it; the draft must say which of the two it means, for instance that the step falls away only for a device that is not in device management at all, and say what the reader does where an enrolled device does not answer. `spec.md` does not settle this either.
- [high] Steps 18 to 25 and step 35: the draft does not say what the reader does where the device is found again after a wipe was decided but before it is carried out, because step 23 tells them to wipe it and step 35 tells them `do not wipe a device recovered inside the 72 hours`; the draft must say which one wins. `spec.md` does not settle this either.
- [high] Step 19: the reader cannot decide in the moment whether the device `is being looked for`, because no step of the draft gives anyone the job of looking for a device and the draft never says what counts as looking for it, and the condition decides whether the wipe waits; the draft must put the condition in words the reader can answer from what they have. Neither `spec.md` nor the organisation files say who looks for a lost device or what counts as looking for it.
- [medium] Step 12: the limits on the reading, the window from when the device went missing until its access was blocked and that Kari Nordmann decides anything beyond it, come two steps after step 10, which is where the reader reads the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud; the limits must stand in step 10, with the reading they belong to.
- [medium] Steps 4 and 5: that outside working hours the on-call developer, Erik Moen, is the one who locates and locks the device and locks the account in Nordsky Cloud stands only under `Who does what` and `When to hand over`, and both steps name only head of IT Marius Tangen, so the on-call developer who takes a report at night finds no step of their own and the Nordsky Cloud account lock they are to do is named in no step; steps 4 and 5 must say who does them outside working hours, and the record they are to write into must be opened before they write in it, which only step 2 does.
- [medium] Step 1, `Who does what` (on-call developer) and `When to hand over`: the draft turns on whether the moment is inside or outside working hours and never says what the working hours are, so the reader who notices the loss at five in the afternoon cannot tell whether to telephone Marius Tangen or Erik Moen; the draft must give the hours. `spec.md` does not give them, and the organisation files give Monday to Friday 08:00 to 16:00 only for when the head of customer success and the support lead are reachable.
- [medium] Step 1: the reader is sent to the staff handbook for the on-call telephone number, and the draft never says what the staff handbook is or where it is to be found, which stops a reader whose laptop is the lost device; the draft must say where the number is to be found. The organisation files say the staff handbook is a document in the office suite that every employee can open, and that every employee is given the on-call telephone number on a card when they start so that it can be reached without signing in to anything.
- [medium] Steps 8, 33 and 34: each step asks for several actions, so one of them can be done and the others forgotten; step 8 asks to write in the record that the device cannot be reached and why, to confirm the encryption from the equipment register or the purchase invoice in the contract archive, to write in the record whether the encryption was confirmed and that the data counts as exposed, and to change or revoke within 2 hours of the report every password, key or token that was stored on the device; step 33 asks to give the replacement laptop, to give the access back and to tell head of people Nora Lind; step 34 asks to put the new device into the equipment register and to mark the lost device as lost there with the date. Each action must be its own step.
- [medium] Step 35: `check the device and say it is safe before the person uses it again` and `undo nothing that was already done until you say the device is safe to use again` ask for a result and name no action the reader can do, so the reader does not know what to check or what makes the device safe; the step must name the check. Neither `spec.md` nor the organisation files say what the check is.
- [medium] Step 7, `Who does what` (head of people), `Who must be told` and `When to hand over`: what step 5 calls ending the sessions of the account, changing its password and revoking its access tokens is called `the accounts and access are blocked` in those four places, so the reader cannot tell whether blocking the accounts is a measure they still have to do; the draft must use one name, for instance by saying in step 5 that this is what blocking a person's accounts and access means.
- [medium] Steps 17 and 31 and `Who does what` (head of IT): `where there is no person behind the device` is never explained, and the reader cannot tell whether it covers a device whose holder has left the company, which steps 15, 16 and 17 treat as a case with a person; the draft must say what it means. `spec.md` uses the same words without explaining them.
- [low] When this applies (the paragraph on a device the person owns themselves), step 19 and step 21: `the sessions of their account are ended, its password changed and its tokens revoked`, `step 25 then wipes it`, `The device is wiped at step 23` and `the device is wiped at once` tell the reader what to do but not as an instruction to them.
- [low] Steps 4, 6, 18, 23, 25 and 32 and step 25: the two conditions `This step does not apply where the device is the person's own, and it does not apply where device management cannot reach the device` are repeated in front of six steps, and step 25 repeats the whole of step 21 in one long sentence, which the reader has to read past each time to reach the action.
- [low] When this applies: the paragraph on what `Handle a personal data breach` does and does not cover and the sentence that the start is the fact that the device cannot be accounted for `not an admission by the person who had it` give the reader background they do not need in order to act, and stand between them and the first step.
