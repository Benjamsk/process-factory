# gdpr review of draft-2.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Step 17 and `When to hand over`: nobody is named to decide whether the loss is a personal data breach when neither Kari Nordmann nor Henrik Aas can be reached within 4 hours, a case the draft itself foresees in step 16 for the wipe. Where it happens the decision that starts `Handle a personal data breach` is never made, while the 72 hours of Article 33 and the `without undue delay` of Article 34 run from the moment the loss reached the company, as step 18 says. The draft must say who decides the breach question in that case and by when; `spec.md` names no one for it either and lets Marius Tangen decide only the wipe alone.
- [medium] Step 9, third bullet: the step tells Marius Tangen to read the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud without the limit that Article 5(1)(c) needs; the limit appears only inside the text step 10 reads out to the employee, so the person doing the reading is never told to keep to it. Step 9 must say that only the entries of that one account are read, only for the window from when the device went missing until its access was blocked, that no conclusion about the person's performance or conduct is drawn from them, and that where more than that window is needed Kari Nordmann decides what is looked at and why and it is written into the record.
- [medium] Steps 10 and 11: where the person who had the device has left the company, they are never told what the company reads about them and why, although step 4 blocks their account and step 9 reads the entries of that account in the logs and step 11 collects what the company knows about them from Nora Lind. Step 10 ties the telling to the same answer in which the person is asked for the facts, which for someone who has left is never sent, so the duty of Articles 12 and 14 is not met for them. The draft must say that the person who has left the company is also told, by which route and within which time; `spec.md` does not say it either.
- [low] `When this applies`, fifth paragraph: the draft adds that the moment the loss reached the company is "the hour someone answered the report". `spec.md` says only that every deadline runs from the moment the loss reached the company, and step 18 makes that moment the moment the company became aware for the 72 hours of Article 33. Reading it as the hour the call was answered can place the start later than the moment the company became aware, for instance where the caller works down the chain of `When this applies` before anyone answers. Drop the addition and keep the wording of `spec.md`.
