# readability review of draft-2.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] `When this applies`, the paragraph "Every time in this document counts from the moment the loss reached the company ... and runs around the clock: through every evening, every weekend and every public holiday", against step 20 ("within 3 working days"), step 21 ("within 2 working days"), the private-device paragraph ("within 2 working days") and the same two times in `Who must be told`: the reader cannot tell whether those three deadlines are counted in calendar time like every other time in the document or in working days, so a loss reported on a Friday evening gives the replacement laptop on Sunday under one reading and on Tuesday under the other; the paragraph must say which deadlines run around the clock and that the insurer and the replacement are counted in working days, or the steps must give those two deadlines in hours like the rest. `spec.md` has the same conflict: its `Deadlines` section says every deadline runs around the clock and its table then gives "Within 3 working days" and "Within 2 working days".
- [high] Step 12: the step tells the reader to ask Kari Nordmann first where the device held personal data, but never says what her answer does, so the reader who reaches her and is advised not to wipe although a ground in the list is met does not know whether to wipe; the step must say whether her answer binds the decision or is advice the reader may act against, and what to write into the record when it does. `spec.md` does not say either: its step 12 and step 16 only cover the case where she cannot be reached.
- [medium] Steps 3, 4, 6, 12, 13, 20 and 21: the two situations that change them, a private device and a company device that device management cannot reach, stand only under `When this applies`, so the reader who works down `Steps` in an incident locates, locks and wipes a device the company does not own and promises a replacement for it; each of these steps must carry its own condition, such as step 3 "not where the device is the employee's own" and step 21 "where the device was private, give a company laptop only where the employee needs one to work", with the detail staying under `When this applies`.
- [medium] Step 4 asks for three actions in three systems at once, end the sessions, change the password and revoke the access tokens, in the office suite, in Nordsky Cloud and in the support desk; split it into one step per action so that the reader cannot carry out one and believe the step is done.
- [medium] Step 13 asks for four actions, reach the person who had the device, tell them that it will be wiped, ask them what exists only on it and write their answer into the record, and adds two exceptions on top; split the telling and asking from the writing, and put the exception for a device with no person behind it in its own step.
- [low] Step 10 is one sentence of five semicolon clauses that the reader has to hold in their head while speaking to the employee; give the five things to tell them as a short list under the step.
- [low] Step 9 says "establish six facts" but its three bullets name eleven, so the reader cannot tell which six they must mark `not established`, and steps 17 and `What to record` repeat the number; either write the number that matches the list or drop the number and refer to "the established facts". `spec.md` has the same mismatch in its steps 9 and 17 and in `Records`.
- [low] Step 13, last sentence, and the private-device paragraph of `When this applies` say what the record says rather than telling the reader to write it; write "write into the record what work was lost" and "write into the record that the device was private, what the employee says was on it and that the company could not reach it", as steps 2, 14 and 15 do.
- [low] Step 8, "tell Kari Nordmann about the lost device, at the same time as you start establishing the facts and whatever you think her answer will be": the last clause can be read as a second thing to tell her; write "tell her even when you are sure what her answer will be".
- [low] `When this applies`, second sentence, "It is not an admission that starts it: a device nobody has reported missing is treated exactly like one an employee reported": the reader does not need the reason in order to act, and "it" in this sentence and in "Start it when" points to the document while "it" in the sentences around them points to the device; drop the first half of the sentence and keep "A device nobody has reported missing is treated exactly like one an employee reported."
