# Answers 4

## 1. Where the person who had the device cannot be reached before a wipe, when does the one hour that the wipe may be held back start to count: when the wipe is decided, at the first attempt to reach them, or at some other moment?

At the first attempt to reach them. Marius Tangen telephones or writes to them as soon as the wipe is decided, writes the time of that first attempt in the record, and wipes the device one hour after it whether or not they have answered. The hour is not a waiting period he has to use: where they answer in ten minutes, he asks what exists only on the device and wipes it then. Where the wipe was decided before anyone tried to reach them, the hour starts at the first attempt and the record says why the attempt came later.

## 2. On what legal ground does the company keep the name of whoever reported the loss, and what they said, in the record of the case, including a reporter from outside the company such as a hotel, a train company, the police or a finder?

On Article 6(1)(f), the same legitimate interest in protecting the company's systems and the personal data it holds: the company must be able to show who told it about the loss and when, because the whole timeline of the case, and the 72 hours of `Handle a personal data breach` where it becomes one, counts from that moment. Only the name, the way the person can be reached and what they said about the loss are kept, nothing else about them, and they are kept no longer than the record of the case: 3 years from the day it is closed, or 5 where the loss was also a personal data breach. Where the reporter asks not to be named, Kari Nordmann decides what of their identity the record holds and writes that decision in the record, as she does in `Handle a personal data breach`.

## 3. Is whoever reported the loss told that their name and what they said are written into the record of the case and why, who tells them and by when, including a reporter from outside the company such as a hotel, a train company or a finder?

Yes. Marius Tangen tells them in the same answer he already sends them within 1 hour of the report, and Amina Farah or the on-call developer where they answer the report instead. It says four things in plain language: that their name, how they can be reached and what they said are written in the record of the case; that this is so that the company can show how it handled the loss and from what moment; that the record is kept 3 years from the day the case is closed, or 5 where the loss was also a personal data breach, and is then deleted; and that Kari Nordmann is the data protection officer they can ask, with Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold when she is away. A reporter who is an employee is told the same thing, and this does not replace what the person who had the device is told about the logs.

## 4. Must the message that tells the person what the company reads about them also say that Nora Lind gives what the company knows about them where they have left the company or cannot be reached, and that the entry in the equipment register naming them is kept as long as the record of the case?

Yes, both. The message says that where they cannot be reached or have left the company, head of people Nora Lind gives what the company already knows about them for the case, which is who had the device, when they left, what they handed back and what they said about it then, and that this comes from the employment file she keeps. And it says that the entry in the equipment register naming them as the person who had the device is kept as long as the record of the case, 3 years from the day it is closed or 5 where the loss was also a personal data breach, after which the name is removed and the device, its serial number and the dates are kept without it.

## 5. Whom does a person turn to when this process does not cover the situation they meet?

Data protection officer Kari Nordmann, on the three days a week she works, and lawyer Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold on the other days and at weekends, on the firm's emergency number. Two kinds of question go elsewhere first: anything about the devices, the accounts or the systems is decided by head of IT Marius Tangen, and by lead developer Amina Farah when he is away; and a question nobody above can answer goes to managing director Ingrid Solheim, and to head of finance Tomas Berg when she is away. Every such question and the answer given go into the record of the case, so that the next version of this process covers it.

## 6. What does the record hold about the telling of the person what the company reads about them: that the telling was sent, to which address and at what time?

All three, for every telling the process requires: that it was sent, to which address or telephone number, and at what time. That holds for what the person who had the device is told about the logs, for what Nora Lind sends to a person who has left, and for what whoever reported the loss is told about the record. Where a telling could not be sent, the record says that, what was tried and at what times, and the telling is sent at the first address the company later has. The record also says who sent each telling, so that no telling rests on everyone assuming someone else sent it.
