# Answers 1

## 1. What exactly starts the process: a laptop the employee is sure was lost or stolen, or also one that is only missing for now, for example left behind on a train and perhaps recoverable?

Both, and a device that is only missing is treated as lost from the first minute. The employee does not wait to see whether it turns up: a laptop left on a train, a laptop that may be in the other jacket at home, and a laptop taken in a burglary all start the process the same way. Where the device is found again, the case is closed with a line saying it was found, when, where and whether anyone else could have used it in the meantime, and whatever was already done is not undone until Marius Tangen says the device is safe to use again.

## 2. Does the process cover only company laptops, or also work phones, tablets, USB drives and a private device the employee uses for work?

It covers every device that held company data or could sign in to a company system: company laptops, work phones and tablets in the equipment register, and any memory stick or external disk with company data on it. A private device the employee used for work is covered too, but only for what the company can do to its own systems: the employee's sessions are ended, their password changed and their tokens revoked, and the company does not lock or wipe a device it does not own. The record says that the device was private and what the employee says was on it.

## 3. What must be done at once when a loss is reported, and in which order: locking or wiping the laptop remotely, changing the employee's password, ending their sessions and revoking their access tokens, blocking the device in the systems, something else?

In this order, because each step narrows what someone holding the device can still reach:

1. Locate and lock the device in device management. This is first because it is the only step that can stop someone who has the device in their hands right now.
2. End the employee's sessions, change their password and revoke their access tokens in the office suite, Nordsky Cloud and the support desk.
3. Block the device itself in device management, so that it cannot sign in again even with a new password.
4. Establish the facts of step 4 below.
5. Decide about wiping, which comes after the facts because a wipe cannot be undone.

Marius Tangen does all of it, Amina Farah when he is away, and outside working hours the on-call developer does steps 1 and 2 in what the role can reach and tells Marius Tangen at once.

## 4. What must be established about the lost laptop before those measures are decided, and how: what was stored on it locally, whether it was encrypted, whether it was logged in to the reading app or Nordsky Cloud, and where and when it went missing?

Six things, within 4 hours of the report. From device management: the model and serial number, whether the disk was encrypted, whether the screen was locked, and when and from where the device last contacted the company. From the employee: where and when it went missing, whether it was taken or mislaid, whether anyone else could have seen the screen or the password, and what was stored on it that exists nowhere else. From the sign-in logs of the office suite and the Nordsky Cloud access logs: which systems the device was signed in to, with which account, and whether anything was reached from it after the time it went missing. Marius Tangen writes all six into the record, and writes `not established` with the reason where something cannot be established rather than leaving it blank.

## 5. How quickly must each step happen after the loss is reported, and are those times counted in working hours or around the clock, including evenings and weekends?

Around the clock, including every evening, every weekend and every public holiday, because a laptop is most often lost on the way home or on a trip. Counted from the moment the loss reached the company:

- The employee tells the head of IT: the same hour they notice it.
- The device is located and locked: within 1 hour.
- Sessions ended, password changed, tokens revoked, device blocked: within 2 hours.
- The six facts established: within 4 hours.
- The decision whether to wipe: within 4 hours.
- Kari Nordmann's decision whether this is a personal data breach: within 4 hours.
- A theft reported to the police: within 24 hours.
- The insurer told where the equipment was worth more than 10,000 kroner: within 3 working days.
- A replacement laptop and the employee's access back: within 2 working days of the loss.

## 6. How is a loss reported and acted on when the employee notices it outside working hours, in the evening or at the weekend, given that the duty is to report a security incident the same day?

The employee telephones the on-call developer on the on-call telephone number in the staff handbook, and does not wait for Monday or send an email that nobody reads. The on-call developer locates and locks the device at once, revokes what the role can revoke in Nordsky Cloud, writes the time of the call and what was done into the record, and telephones Marius Tangen on his mobile, who can be reached around the clock. Marius Tangen does the rest himself or decides what waits until working hours, and says so in the record. Where the on-call developer cannot be reached, the employee telephones Marius Tangen directly, and Ingrid Solheim where he cannot be reached either.

## 7. On what basis is a laptop wiped or locked remotely, and is the employee asked first, given that wiping destroys work that exists only on the device?

Locking needs no basis and no asking: every lost device is locked at once. A wipe is decided by Marius Tangen within 4 hours of the report, and Kari Nordmann is asked first where the device held personal data. The device is wiped where any of these is true: it cannot be located; it was not encrypted; the screen was not locked when it went missing; it was signed in to the reading app, Nordsky Cloud or the support desk; or it was taken rather than mislaid. Where none of them is true and the device is locked, encrypted and being looked for, the wipe can wait up to 72 hours from the report, and the record says who decided to wait and why. The employee is told before the wipe where they can be reached and is asked what exists only on the device, and that goes into the record; the wipe does not wait more than an hour because they cannot be reached. Work that existed only on the device is lost, and the record says what it was.

## 8. At which point in this process is it decided whether the loss is a personal data breach, and does the breach process then take over, run alongside this one, or is this process only its first step?

Kari Nordmann decides it, within 4 hours of the report, from the six established facts; Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold decides when she is away. Marius Tangen tells her about every lost device at the same time he starts establishing the facts, whatever he thinks the answer will be.

This process is the first step and keeps running. Where she decides it is a personal data breach, `Handle a personal data breach` starts and the two run alongside each other, with one record and one timeline: the moment the loss reached the company is the moment the company became aware, so the 72 hours of that process count from it and not from her decision. This process keeps the device, the accounts, the police, the insurer and the replacement; that process takes the fact page, Datatilsynet, the school owners and the people affected. Neither waits for the other.

Where she decides it is not a personal data breach, she writes the line in the record with her name, the reason and the time, and this process runs on to its end on its own.

## 9. Does the process also cover getting the employee working again, with a replacement laptop and their access restored, or does it end once the security measures are done?

It covers it. Marius Tangen gives the employee a replacement laptop and their access back within 2 working days of the loss, and Nora Lind is told so that she knows the employee could not work and for how long. The new device goes into the equipment register and the lost one is marked as lost there with the date. The process ends when the employee is working again, the record is complete and Kari Nordmann has said whether it was a breach.

## 10. What is recorded about each lost laptop, including the losses that turn out to be harmless, where is that record kept and for how long?

One record per lost device, kept for every loss including the harmless ones, because the pattern across losses is what shows whether the routine works. Marius Tangen keeps it in the office suite, in the folder he makes for the case, and it holds: who reported the loss and when they noticed it; the time it reached the company; the device, its model and its serial number from the equipment register; where and when it went missing and whether it was taken or mislaid; the six established facts, with `not established` and the reason where one could not be established; what was done to the device and the accounts and at what time; who decided to wipe or to wait, with the reason and the time; what the employee said existed only on the device; Kari Nordmann's decision whether it was a personal data breach, with her name, the reason and the time, whether the answer was yes or no; the police case number where there was a theft; whether the insurer was told; when the replacement was given and the access restored; and, where the device was found again, when and where and whether anyone else could have used it. The record is kept 3 years from the day the case is closed, and 5 years where it was also a personal data breach, so that it lasts as long as the record of that case.
