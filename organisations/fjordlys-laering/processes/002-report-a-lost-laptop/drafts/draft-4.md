# Report a lost laptop

## When this applies

Start this process the moment a device cannot be accounted for. A device that is only missing counts as lost from the first minute: a laptop left on a train, a laptop that may be in the other jacket at home and a laptop taken in a burglary all start the process the same way. Do not wait to see whether it turns up. A device nobody has reported missing is treated exactly like one an employee reported.

Whoever notices tells head of IT Marius Tangen the same hour. That can be the person who had the device, a colleague who notices it is gone, Marius Tangen finding a device in the equipment register that nobody can account for, or a report from outside the company such as a hotel, a train company, the police or a finder. Outside working hours telephone the on-call developer, Erik Moen, on the on-call telephone number, and do not send an email. The number is on the card you were given when you started and in the staff handbook in the office suite.

The process covers every device that held company data or could sign in to a company system: the company laptops, work phones and tablets in the equipment register, and any memory stick or external disk with company data on it.

A private device the person used for work is covered only for what the company can do in its own systems: end the person's sessions, change their password and revoke their tokens, exactly as for a company device. The company does not locate, lock or wipe a device it does not own, does not claim on its insurer for it and does not replace it. The person claims on their own insurance and reports a theft to the police themselves, and Marius Tangen helps them with what the company knows. Where they need a device to work, Marius Tangen gives them a company laptop within 2 working days; that is the company equipping them and not a replacement of their own device.

A company device that device management cannot reach, such as a memory stick or an external disk, is covered too, and the steps on the device fall away because there is nothing to reach. Treat the data on it as exposed from the first minute, unless the person who had it says the disk was encrypted and Marius Tangen can confirm from the equipment register or the purchase that it was an encrypted device. Where that cannot be confirmed, it counts as unencrypted. Where any password, key or token was stored on the device, change or revoke it in every system it could reach, beyond the person's own sessions and tokens.

This process covers getting the person working again: a replacement laptop and their access back. It ends when they are working again, the record is complete and the decision whether the loss was a personal data breach stands in the record. Finding the device again does not end it.

This process does not cover the fact page, Datatilsynet, the school owners or the people affected: those belong to `Handle a personal data breach`. Where the loss is a personal data breach the two processes run alongside each other, with one record and one timeline, and neither waits for the other. This process keeps the device, the accounts, the police, the insurer and the replacement.

Every time in this process counts from the moment the loss reached the company. Every time runs around the clock, through every evening, every weekend and every public holiday, except the two counted in working days: the insurer told within 3 working days, and the replacement laptop and the access back within 2 working days.

## Steps

1. Whoever notices that the device cannot be accounted for: tell head of IT Marius Tangen the same hour you notice it. Outside working hours telephone the on-call developer, Erik Moen, on the on-call telephone number from your card or the staff handbook, and do not send an email. Where he cannot be reached, telephone Marius Tangen on his mobile, and managing director Ingrid Solheim where he cannot be reached either, with head of finance Tomas Berg in her place.
2. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): open the record of the case and write into it the time the report reached the company, who reported it and what they said.
3. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): locate and lock the device in device management, which is Microsoft Intune, within 1 hour of the report. Do this for every lost device and ask nobody first. Do not do it where the device is the person's own. Where device management cannot reach the device, this step, the block in step 7 and the wipe fall away: write into the record that the device cannot be reached from device management and why.
4. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): end the person's sessions in the office suite, which is Microsoft 365, in Nordsky Cloud and in the support desk, within 2 hours of the report.
5. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): change the person's password in those three systems, within 2 hours of the report.
6. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): revoke the person's access tokens in those three systems, within 2 hours of the report. Where the person has left the company and their account was already closed, write into the record that it was closed and when.
7. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): block the device itself in device management, within 2 hours of the report, so that it cannot sign in again even with a new password. Not where the device is the person's own.
8. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): where any password, key or token was stored on a device device management cannot reach, change or revoke it in every system it could reach, within 2 hours of the report.
9. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): tell data protection officer Kari Nordmann about the lost device at the same time as you start establishing the facts, even when you are sure what her answer will be. Tell Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold when she is away.
10. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): establish the facts of the loss within 4 hours of the report and write each of them into the record, with `not established` and the reason where one cannot be established:
    - from device management: the model and serial number, whether the disk was encrypted, whether the screen was locked, and when and from where the device last contacted the company;
    - from the person who had the device: where and when it went missing, whether it was taken or mislaid, whether anyone else could have seen the screen or the password, and what was stored on it that exists nowhere else;
    - from the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud: which systems the device was signed in to, with which account, and whether anything was reached from it after the time it went missing.
11. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): in reading device management and those logs, read only the device's own entries and the entries of that one account, and only for the window from when the device went missing until its access was blocked. Do not use what you read to see how the person works, what they worked on or where they were at other times, and draw no conclusion about their performance or conduct from it. Where you need more than that window, Kari Nordmann decides what is looked at and why, and that goes into the record.
12. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): where there is no person behind the device, for example a laptop that someone who has left the company never handed back, answer for the device in their place. Head of people (Nora Lind) gives what the company knows instead of the facts only that person could give: who had the device, when they left, what they handed back and what they said about it then. Where the person has not left the company but cannot be reached, handle the device the same way, ask them for the facts as soon as they can be reached and add what they say to the record.
13. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): tell the person who had the device, within 4 hours of the report and in the same answer in which you ask them for the facts, in plain language:
    - that you look at where and when the device last contacted the company and at the entries of their account in the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud, for the window from when the device went missing until its access was blocked;
    - that this is to find out what could have been reached, and nothing else;
    - that it is not used to judge how they work;
    - that what you find goes into the record of the case, which is kept 3 years, or 5 years where the loss was also a personal data breach;
    - that Kari Nordmann is the data protection officer they can ask about it.

    Where the person is still at the company but cannot be reached in time, send them the same thing at their work email within those 4 hours so that it is waiting for them, and say it again when you first speak to them.
14. Head of people (Nora Lind): where the person who had the device has left the company, send them the same thing in writing within 24 hours of the report, at the private email address or private postal address the company holds for them in the employment files. Where the company holds no address that reaches them, write that into the record with what was tried. Do not drop the telling: send it at the first address the company later has.
15. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): decide within 4 hours of the report whether the device is wiped. Where the device held personal data, ask Kari Nordmann first, or Henrik Aas when she is away; her answer is advice on the wipe and not permission for it, and you decide. Wipe the device where any of these is true: it cannot be located; it was not encrypted; the screen was not locked when it went missing; it was signed in to the reading app, Nordsky Cloud or the support desk; or it was taken rather than mislaid. Where none of them is true, wait with the wipe, and write into the record who decided to wait and why.
16. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): before the wipe, reach the person who had the device, tell them that the device will be wiped, ask them what exists only on it and write their answer into the record. Do not hold the wipe back longer than 1 hour because they cannot be reached. Where there is no person behind the device, there is nobody to tell: wipe it at once rather than after any waiting time.
17. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): wipe the device in device management. Wipe a device that has not been recovered within 72 hours of the report whether or not a ground for an earlier wipe was met, and do it without asking again; that is an outer limit and not a question to be reopened. Write into the record what work was lost because it existed only on the device.
18. Data protection officer (Kari Nordmann): decide within 4 hours of the report whether the loss is a personal data breach, from the established facts; Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold decides when she is away. Where it is a personal data breach, start `Handle a personal data breach` in the same hour you decide it, by telling Marius Tangen and Ingrid Solheim that it is a personal data breach and that the breach process now runs; Marius Tangen then holds both processes and keeps one record and one timeline for the two, and the record says both the moment the loss reached the company and the moment of the decision. Where it is not a personal data breach, write that line into the record with your name, the reason and the time, and this process runs on to its end on its own.
19. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): where neither Kari Nordmann nor Henrik Aas can be reached within the 4 hours, decide the breach question yourself within those same 4 hours; Amina Farah decides where you are away. Decide it one way: where you cannot establish that the loss is not a personal data breach, decide that it is one, and `Handle a personal data breach` starts at once. Write into the record that you decided it yourself, whom you tried to reach and when, and what you based it on, and tell Kari Nordmann, or Henrik Aas, at the first hour one of them is reachable. She looks at it again then and may decide that it is not a personal data breach after all; she writes that line into the record with her name, her reason and the time, the breach process stops with that line in its own record, and she does not undo what was already done.
20. The person who had the device: where the device was taken rather than mislaid, report the theft to the police within 24 hours of the report, with Marius Tangen's help, and the case number is written into the record. Where there is no person behind the device, Marius Tangen reports it himself with the serial number from the equipment register. Where the device was the person's own, they report it themselves.
21. Head of finance (Tomas Berg): tell the insurer within 3 working days of the report where the lost equipment was worth more than 10,000 kroner. Do not do this where the device was the person's own.
22. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): give the person a replacement laptop and their access back within 2 working days of the report, and tell Nora Lind so that she knows the person could not work and for how long. Where the device was the person's own, give them a company laptop only where they need one to work. Give no replacement where there is no person behind the device.
23. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): put the replacement device into the equipment register and mark the lost one there as lost, with the date.
24. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): where the device is found again, write into the record when and where it was found and whether anyone else could have used it in the meantime. Do not wipe a device recovered within the 72 hours. Undo nothing that was already done until you have checked the device and said that it is safe to use again, and the person does not use it before you have.
25. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): write into the record that the case is closed, once the person is working again, the record is complete and the decision whether the loss was a personal data breach stands in the record.

## What to record

One record per lost device, kept for every loss including the harmless ones. Head of IT Marius Tangen keeps it in the office suite, in the folder he makes for the case. Where the loss is also a personal data breach, `Handle a personal data breach` and this process share this one record and one timeline. The record holds:

- who reported the loss and when they noticed it, and the time the report reached the company;
- the device, its model and its serial number from the equipment register, and, where the device was the person's own, that it was private, what they say was on it and that the company could not reach it;
- that the device cannot be reached from device management and why, where it cannot;
- where and when the device went missing, and whether it was taken or mislaid;
- the established facts, with `not established` and the reason where one could not be established;
- what Kari Nordmann decided to look at beyond the window from when the device went missing until its access was blocked, and why;
- what was done to the device and to the accounts and at what time, among it what the on-call developer did and the time of the call, and that an account was already closed and when;
- who decided to wipe or to wait, with the reason and the time; what the person said existed only on the device; and what work was lost;
- the decision whether the loss was a personal data breach, with the name of the one who decided, the reason and the time, whether the answer was yes or no, and, where Marius Tangen decided it himself, whom he tried to reach and when and what he based it on;
- the attempts to reach someone with access to the systems and their times, and which deadline was missed and why;
- where no address reaches a person who has left the company, that the telling could not be sent and what was tried;
- the police case number where there was a theft, and whether the insurer was told;
- when the replacement was given and the access restored;
- where the device was found again, when and where it was found, whether anyone else could have used it in the meantime, and that Marius Tangen has said it is safe to use again;
- the line that closes the case.

The record is kept 3 years from the day the case is closed, and 5 years where the loss was also a personal data breach.

The entry in the equipment register that says which person had the lost device, which replacement they were given and the date the lost device was marked as lost is kept as long as the record: 3 years from the day the case is closed, and 5 years where the loss was also a personal data breach. Marius Tangen then deletes the personal part of the entry, which is which person had the device, and keeps the device, its serial number and the dates without a name for as long as the company needs them for its accounts and its insurance. The entry for a device that was returned rather than lost is kept 3 years after the return.
