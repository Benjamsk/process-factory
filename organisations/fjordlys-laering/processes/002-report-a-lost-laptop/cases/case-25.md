# Case 25: The colleague who reports a missing work phone asks not to be named

Kind: Conflicting rules

## Situation

Thursday 26 November 2026. At 09:15 developer Lise Berntsen telephones head of IT Marius Tangen and says that support adviser Sondre Lie's work phone has been gone since an after-work gathering on Tuesday evening, that he told her he thinks it was taken from his jacket in the cloakroom, and that he has reported nothing because he hopes it will turn up. She asks that her name not appear anywhere in the case, because she does not want Sondre Lie to know that she was the one who told the company. The report reaches the company at 09:15. Kari Nordmann works Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday.

## Who acts

Data protection officer, Kari Nordmann.

## What the person knows

She knows that she is told about every lost device at the same time as Marius Tangen starts establishing the facts. She knows that the record must hold who reported the loss, how they can be reached and what they said, and that the basis for keeping it is the company's legitimate interest in being able to show who told it about the loss and when, because the whole timeline of the case counts from that moment, and that nothing else about the reporter is kept and it is kept no longer than the record of the case. She knows that where the reporter asks not to be named, she decides what of their identity the record holds and that decision is written into the record, and that her decision, her reason and the time are part of what the record holds about the reporter. She knows that the reporter must be told within 1 hour of the report what the record holds about them, why, how long it is kept and whom they can ask, and that a reporter who is an employee is told the same. She works on Thursdays. She does not know whether the phone was taken or mislaid, where and when it went missing or what was on it: those are facts for Marius Tangen to establish, and only Sondre Lie can give some of them. She knows that the specification does not say who may read the record of a case, and so does not say whether Sondre Lie would learn who reported the loss.

## What should happen

Every time counts from 09:15, the moment the loss reached the company, and not from Tuesday evening.

- At 09:15 Marius Tangen opens the record and writes into it who reported the loss, how she can be reached, what she said, when she says the phone went missing and the time the report reached the company. He tells Kari Nordmann about the lost device at the same time as he starts establishing the facts, and she is the one who decides what of Lise Berntsen's identity the record holds.
- Kari Nordmann decides what of Lise Berntsen's identity the record holds and writes that decision into the record with her name, her reason and the time. What she decides is hers to decide: the specification sets no answer and no time for it, and it says only that the decision and the reason stand in the record.
- By 10:15, within 1 hour of the report, the reporter is told, in plain language, that her name, how she can be reached and what she said are written in the record of the case; that this is so that the company can show how it handled the loss and from what moment; that the record is kept 3 years from the day the case is closed, or 5 years where the loss was also a personal data breach, and is then deleted; and that Kari Nordmann is the data protection officer she can ask, with Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold when she is away. She is an employee and is told the same as anyone else. Where Kari Nordmann's decision has by then changed what the record holds of her identity, the telling says what the record holds.
- The two rules pull in different directions: the record must hold who reported the loss and how they can be reached, and the reporter has asked not to be named. The specification settles that only so far as to put the question to Kari Nordmann and to have her decision and her reason written down. It does not say that the 1-hour telling waits for her decision, and it does not say what the telling says where the decision has not yet been taken. That gap is not filled here. The deadline keeps running, and where the telling is late the record says which deadline was missed and why.
- Lise Berntsen asked that Sondre Lie should not learn that she reported the loss. The specification says what the record may hold of her identity and who decides that; it says nothing about who may read the record of a case, and so nothing about whether her request can be met beyond Kari Nordmann's decision. That gap is not filled here either, and nothing in the record is left out that the specification requires.
- By 10:15 Marius Tangen locates and locks the work phone in device management. By 11:15 he ends the sessions of Sondre Lie's account, changes its password and revokes its access tokens in the office suite, in Nordsky Cloud and in the support desk, blocks the device in device management, and tells Nora Lind that the accounts and access are blocked.
- By 13:15 the six facts stand in the record, each established or written as `not established` with its reason, among them whether the phone was taken or mislaid and whether anything was reached from it after Tuesday evening. By 13:15 Sondre Lie is told what is read about him and why, in the same answer in which he is asked for the facts only he can give.
- By 13:15 Marius Tangen decides whether the phone is wiped, and asks Kari Nordmann first because it held personal data in the office suite and the support desk. It was signed in to the support desk, which is a ground, so it is wiped. Before the wipe he reaches Sondre Lie, tells him the phone will be wiped and asks him what exists only on it, writes the time of that first attempt into the record, and writes his answer and what work was lost into the record.
- By 13:15 Kari Nordmann decides from the established facts whether the loss is a personal data breach, and her decision, her name, her reason and the time go into the record whether the answer is yes or no.
- Where the facts show the phone was taken, Sondre Lie reports the theft to the police with Marius Tangen's help within 24 hours of 09:15 and the police case number goes into the record.
- The equipment register shows the work phone was bought for 9,200 kroner, which is not more than 10,000 kroner, so the insurer is not told.
- Within 2 working days of 09:15 Marius Tangen gives Sondre Lie a replacement device and his access back, and tells Nora Lind that he could not work and for how long. The replacement goes into the equipment register and the lost phone is marked there as lost, with the date.

## Pass

The record holds Kari Nordmann's decision on what of the reporter's identity the record holds, written in her name with her reason and the time, and of Lise Berntsen it holds what that decision says and nothing more. It holds an entry timed no later than 10:15 saying that the reporter was told what the record holds about her, why, that it is kept 3 years from the day the case is closed or 5 years where the loss was also a personal data breach, and that Kari Nordmann is the data protection officer she can ask, with Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold when she is away; where that telling came later, the record names the missed deadline and says why. The record says plainly that the specification does not say whether the 1-hour telling waits for her decision, and that it does not say who may read the record of a case and so does not say whether Sondre Lie learns who reported the loss; neither gap is filled and nothing the specification requires is left out of the record. It also shows 09:15 on Thursday 26 November 2026 as the moment the loss reached the company with every deadline counted from it, the lock by 10:15, the account measures and the block by 11:15 with Nora Lind told, the six facts and the telling to Sondre Lie by 13:15, a wipe decision by 13:15 with Kari Nordmann asked beforehand and the time of the first attempt to reach him, Kari Nordmann's breach decision with her name, her reason and the time, a police case number within 24 hours where the phone was taken, no entry saying the insurer was told, and a replacement device and the access back within 2 working days with Nora Lind told.
