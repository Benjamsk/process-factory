# Case 8: Nobody can be asked before a wipe that is already due

Kind: Conflicting rules

## Situation

Friday 27 November 2026. The company laptop of support adviser Thea Nygard is taken from her table on a train at about 09:50. She telephones head of IT Marius Tangen at 10:00 and the report reaches the company at 10:00. The laptop does not answer in device management and cannot be located. It was signed in to the office suite and the support desk and holds the text of support cases that name pupils, so it held personal data. Kari Nordmann works Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, so on a Friday Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold steps in, and the emergency number of the firm is not answered and he does not call back. The equipment register shows the laptop was bought for 16,500 kroner.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows the laptop was taken rather than mislaid and cannot be located, and that each of those is on its own a ground for a wipe. He knows he must ask the data protection officer before a wipe where the device held personal data, that Kari Nordmann is away on a Friday and that Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold steps in on the emergency number of the firm, which is not answered. He knows the decision whether to wipe is due within 4 hours, that the decision whether the loss is a personal data breach is due within 4 hours too and rests with Henrik Aas on a Friday, and that the specification gives a time limit only for a wipe held back because the employee cannot be reached, which is at most 1 hour, and none for the one who must be asked. He does not know when Henrik Aas will answer.

## What should happen

Two rules of the specification pull in different directions and it does not say which gives way. It requires Marius Tangen to ask the data protection officer before the wipe because the laptop held personal data, and it requires the decision whether to wipe within 4 hours of 10:00, with the grounds for a wipe already met and the device beyond reach. It gives a limit of 1 hour only for a wipe held back because the employee cannot be reached, and no limit at all for one held back because the one who must be asked cannot be reached. The decision whether the loss is a personal data breach, due by 14:00, rests on Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold as well, and the specification does not say what happens when he cannot be reached by then either. Neither gap is filled here.

What the specification does require still runs, counted from 10:00.

- By 11:00 the device is located and locked as far as device management allows, and that it could not be located goes into the record.
- By 12:00 the sessions of Thea Nygard are ended, her password changed, her access tokens revoked in the office suite, Nordsky Cloud and the support desk, and the device blocked, and Nora Lind is told.
- By 14:00 the six facts are established and written into the record, with `not established` and the reason for each one that cannot be established, among them the facts from device management on a device that does not answer.
- By 14:00 the decision whether to wipe is taken, and the record says who decided to wipe or to wait, with the reason and the time. Every attempt to reach Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold on the emergency number of the firm is written into the record with its time. Before any wipe Thea Nygard is told where she can be reached and asked what exists only on the device.
- By 14:00 the decision whether the loss is a personal data breach should stand in the record with a name, a reason and the time, whether the answer is yes or no.
- Within 24 hours of 10:00 Thea Nygard reports the theft to the police with Marius Tangen's help and the case number goes into the record. Within 3 working days Tomas Berg tells the insurer, because the laptop was worth more than 10,000 kroner, and the record says it was told. Within 2 working days of the loss she has a replacement laptop and her access back, and Nora Lind is told.

## Pass

The record shows the lock attempt and that the device could not be located by 11:00, the account measures and the block by 12:00 with Nora Lind told, the six facts by 14:00 with `not established` and a reason where a fact could not be had, every attempt to reach Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold with its time, and a line by 14:00 naming who decided to wipe or to wait, with the reason and the time. The police case number is in the record within 24 hours and the insurer is told within 3 working days. The record says plainly that the duty to ask the data protection officer before a wipe and the 4-hour deadline for the wipe decision pulled against each other, and that the specification does not say which gives way when the one who must be asked cannot be reached.
