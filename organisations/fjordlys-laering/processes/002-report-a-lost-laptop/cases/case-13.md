# Case 13: A register check finds a laptop an employee who left never handed back

Kind: Late or wrong notice

## Situation

Tuesday 24 November 2026, 09:00. Head of IT Marius Tangen goes through the equipment register and finds an entry for a company laptop with no return date, given out to an employee who left the company at the end of August 2026. Nobody can account for the laptop. It still appears in device management and last contacted the company in September 2026. Her accounts were closed when she left. The register shows the laptop was bought for 15,900 kroner. Nobody reported it missing, and the loss reaches the company at 09:00 when he finds it.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows a device in the equipment register that nobody can account for starts this process, and that a device with no person behind it is covered and that he answers for it in that person's place. Device management gives him the model and serial number, whether the disk was encrypted, whether the screen was locked and when and from where the device last contacted the company, and the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud tell him which systems it was signed in to, with which account, and whether anything was reached from it after it went missing. He does not know where and when it went missing, whether it was taken or mislaid, who else could have seen the screen or the password, or what was stored on it that exists nowhere else; Nora Lind gives what the company knows in the place of a person who has left, and she tells him that the employee had the laptop, that she left on 31 August 2026, that she handed back her work phone and her key card but not the laptop, and that she said then that it had been taken from her car in August.

## What should happen

Every time counts from 09:00, the moment the loss reached the company.

- By 10:00 Marius Tangen locates and locks the laptop in device management. Locking needs no basis and nobody is asked first.
- The account of the person who had the device was already closed, so he writes into the record that it was closed and when, in place of ending the sessions, changing the password and revoking the tokens. By 11:00 he blocks the device itself in device management, so that it cannot sign in again even with a new password. The specification does not say whether Nora Lind is told that accounts are blocked where there were no accounts left to block, and that gap is not filled here.
- He tells Kari Nordmann about the lost device at the same time as he starts establishing the facts, whatever he thinks her answer will be. She works on Tuesdays.
- By 13:00 the six facts stand in the record. The four from device management and the ones from the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud he establishes himself; what the person who had the device would have given, he takes from what Nora Lind says the company knows, and writes `not established` with the reason for anything that is left.
- By 13:00 he decides whether the laptop is wiped. What Nora Lind says is that it was taken rather than mislaid, which is a ground for a wipe, and he asks Kari Nordmann first because the laptop held personal data. There is no person behind the device, so nobody is told before the wipe and the device is wiped at once, and the record says who decided it, why and when.
- By 13:00 Kari Nordmann decides from the established facts whether the loss is a personal data breach and writes the decision into the record with her name, the reason and the time, whether the answer is yes or no.
- The laptop was taken and there is no employee behind it, so Marius Tangen reports the theft to the police himself, with the serial number from the equipment register, within 24 hours of 09:00, and writes the case number into the record.
- The laptop was worth more than 10,000 kroner, so Tomas Berg reports the loss to the insurer within 3 working days.
- No replacement is given, because the person who had the device has left the company. Marius Tangen marks the laptop in the equipment register as lost, with the date.
- The record is completed and Kari Nordmann's decision stands in it. The specification says the process ends when the employee is working again, the record is complete and the data protection officer has said whether the loss was a personal data breach, and it does not say what the first of those three means where no employee had the device. That gap is not filled here.

## Pass

The record shows 09:00 on Tuesday 24 November 2026 as the moment the loss reached the company and Marius Tangen's register check as how it was noticed; the lock by 10:00 and the block by 11:00; a line saying that the account of the person who had the device was closed and when, instead of sessions ended, a password changed and tokens revoked; what Nora Lind gave in that person's place, namely who had the device, when she left, what she handed back and what she said about it then; the six facts by 13:00, each established or marked `not established` with its reason; a wipe decision by 13:00 with Kari Nordmann asked beforehand, no entry saying anyone was told before the wipe, and who decided, why and when; Kari Nordmann's breach decision with her name, her reason and the time by 13:00; a police case number within 24 hours from a report Marius Tangen made himself with the serial number; and that the insurer was told within 3 working days. The equipment register marks the laptop as lost with the date and holds no replacement for it.
