# Case 22: The head of IT decides the breach question himself, and it is reversed the next morning

Kind: Person away

## Situation

Monday 30 November 2026. Support adviser Sondre Lie works for an hour in a cafe and at 08:50 sees that his company laptop is gone from the table he left while he paid at the counter; he last had it at 08:15. He telephones head of IT Marius Tangen at 09:00, and the report reaches the company at 09:00. The laptop answers in device management: the disk is encrypted, and the last contact with the company was at 08:05, before it went missing, so whether the screen was locked when it was taken cannot be established. It was signed in to the office suite and the support desk, which hold the text of support cases that name pupils, so it held personal data. Kari Nordmann works Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday. Marius Tangen telephones the emergency number of Aas & Wold at 09:30, 10:30, 11:30 and 12:30; nobody answers and Henrik Aas does not call back that day. The equipment register shows the laptop was bought for 15,800 kroner.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows the decision whether the loss is a personal data breach is due within 4 hours of 09:00 and rests with Kari Nordmann, or with Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold on a Monday. He knows that where neither of them can be reached within those 4 hours he decides the question himself within the same 4 hours, that he decides it one way, so that where he cannot establish that the loss is not a personal data breach he decides that it is one and `Handle a personal data breach` starts at once, and that he then writes into the record that he decided it himself, whom he tried to reach and when, and what he based it on, and tells one of them at the first hour one is reachable. He knows the laptop was taken rather than mislaid, which is a ground for a wipe, and that he decides the wipe alone where neither of them can be reached and a ground is met. He knows the laptop was signed in to systems that hold personal data and that whether the screen was locked cannot be established, so he cannot establish that the loss is not a personal data breach. He does not know when either of them will answer; Kari Nordmann is reachable again from Tuesday.

## What should happen

Every time counts from 09:00.

- By 10:00 the laptop is located and locked in device management, and by 11:00 Sondre Lie's sessions are ended, his password changed and his access tokens revoked in the office suite, in Nordsky Cloud and in the support desk, the device is blocked, and Nora Lind is told.
- Marius Tangen tries to reach the data protection officer when he starts establishing the facts, and writes each attempt and its time into the record: 09:30, 10:30, 11:30 and 12:30, none of them answered.
- By 13:00 the six facts stand in the record, each established or marked `not established` with its reason, among them that whether the screen was locked cannot be established and why. By 13:00 Sondre Lie has been told in plain language what the company reads about him and why, in the same answer in which he is asked for the facts.
- By 13:00 Marius Tangen decides the wipe. Neither Kari Nordmann nor Henrik Aas can be reached and the laptop was taken rather than mislaid, so he decides it alone and writes into the record what he tried and when, who decided, why and at what time. Before the wipe he reaches Sondre Lie, tells him the laptop will be wiped, asks him what exists only on it and writes his answer and what was lost into the record.
- By 13:00, within the same 4 hours, Marius Tangen decides the breach question himself, because neither of them could be reached. He cannot establish that the loss is not a personal data breach, so he decides that it is one, and `Handle a personal data breach` starts at once. He writes into the record that he decided it himself, whom he tried to reach and when, and what he based it on: that the laptop was taken, that it was signed in to the office suite and the support desk and that whether the screen was locked could not be established. Ingrid Solheim is told that the loss is a personal data breach, because she is told in every case where it is; the specification does not say who tells her where Marius Tangen decided it himself, and that gap is not filled here.
- He holds both processes and keeps one record and one timeline for the two, and the record says both the moment the loss reached the company, 09:00 on Monday 30 November 2026, and the moment of his decision. The 72 hours of `Handle a personal data breach` count from 09:00, because that is the moment the company became aware, and not from his decision. Neither process waits for the other.
- At 08:30 on Tuesday 1 December 2026, the first hour one of them is reachable, he tells Kari Nordmann that he decided the wipe and the breach question himself, whom he tried to reach and when, and what he based it on.
- She looks at the breach question again and decides that the loss was not a personal data breach after all, and writes that line into the record with her name, her reason and the time. The breach process stops with that line in its own record. She does not undo what was already done: the wipe, the lock, the block and the measures on the accounts stand. Where she says she would have advised otherwise on the wipe, that stands in the record as a finding for the next version of this process rather than as something to undo.
- This process runs on. The laptop was taken, so within 24 hours of 09:00 Sondre Lie reports the theft to the police with Marius Tangen's help and the case number goes into the record. The laptop was worth more than 10,000 kroner, so Tomas Berg tells the insurer within 3 working days. Within 2 working days of the loss Sondre Lie has a replacement laptop and his access back, and Nora Lind is told that he could not work and for how long. The replacement goes into the equipment register and the lost laptop is marked there as lost, with the date.
- Marius Tangen writes into the record that the case is closed, once Sondre Lie is working again, the record is complete and the decision whether the loss was a personal data breach stands in it.

## Pass

The record shows every attempt to reach Kari Nordmann and Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold with its time, and that none was answered by 13:00. It holds, timed no later than 13:00, a decision on the breach question in Marius Tangen's own name, deciding that the loss is a personal data breach, with a line saying that he decided it himself, whom he tried to reach and when, and what he based it on, and no entry waiting past 13:00 for a data protection officer to answer. It shows that `Handle a personal data breach` started at once, that Ingrid Solheim was told, that there is one record and one timeline for the two processes, and that the 72 hours of the breach process count from 09:00 on Monday 30 November 2026 and not from his decision, with both times in the record. It shows that at 08:30 on Tuesday 1 December 2026 he told Kari Nordmann what he had decided, what he tried and what he based it on, and then her line that the loss was not a personal data breach after all, with her name, her reason and the time, with nothing that was done undone after it: the wipe, the lock, the block and the measures on the accounts all still stand in the record, and any advice to the contrary on the wipe stands as a finding. It also shows the lock by 10:00, the account measures and the block by 11:00 with Nora Lind told, the six facts and the message to Sondre Lie about what is read about him by 13:00, a wipe decision by 13:00 in Marius Tangen's name with Sondre Lie told beforehand and what was lost, a police case number within 24 hours, the insurer told within 3 working days, the replacement and the access within 2 working days with Nora Lind told, and the line that closes the case.
