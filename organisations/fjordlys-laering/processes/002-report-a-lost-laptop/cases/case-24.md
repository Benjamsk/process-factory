# Case 24: A train company's lost property office reports a laptop nobody missed

Kind: Late or wrong notice

## Situation

Wednesday 25 November 2026. At 10:05 a woman at the lost property office of the train company telephones the main number of the company and the call is put through to head of IT Marius Tangen. She says that a laptop was handed in at Oslo S on Monday evening by a cleaner who found it on a seat, that it stood on the shelf of the lost property office, and that it was collected on Tuesday afternoon by someone who signed for it with a name nobody there can read. She reads out the serial number from the note the office wrote when the laptop came in, gives her own name and the direct telephone number of the lost property office, and asks whom she should write to. The serial number is the laptop the equipment register gives to support adviser Thea Nygård, who took Monday and Tuesday off, believed the laptop was in the office and has reported nothing. The report reaches the company at 10:05.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows that the loss reached the company at 10:05 and that every time counts from that moment, that the loss was reported by someone outside the company, and that a device nobody has reported missing is treated exactly like one an employee reported. He knows what the reporter said, her name and the telephone number she can be reached on, and that the record holds those three things about her and nothing else, kept no longer than the record of the case, because the company must be able to show who told it about the loss and when. He knows that he must answer her within 1 hour of the report and tell her what the record holds about her, why, how long it is kept and whom she can ask. Device management gives him the model and serial number, that the disk was encrypted and the screen locked, and that the laptop last contacted the company at 17:40 on Monday 23 November from central Oslo and does not answer now, so it cannot be located. The sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud tell him which systems the laptop was signed in to, with which account, and whether anything was reached from it after Monday evening. The equipment register shows the laptop was bought for 15,200 kroner. He does not know where and when Thea Nygård last had the laptop, whether it was taken or mislaid, whether anyone else could have seen the screen or the password, or what was on it that exists nowhere else: those are the facts only she can give, and she is at work and reachable.

## What should happen

Every time counts from 10:05, and nothing counts from Monday evening.

- By 11:05 Marius Tangen answers the woman at the lost property office and tells her, in plain language, that her name, how she can be reached and what she said are written in the record of the case; that this is so that the company can show how it handled the loss and from what moment; that the record is kept 3 years from the day the case is closed, or 5 years where the loss was also a personal data breach, and is then deleted; and that Kari Nordmann is the data protection officer she can ask, with Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold when she is away. She is not an employee, and she is told the same as an employee who reported the loss would be told.
- At 10:05 he opens the record of the case and writes into it who reported the loss, how she can be reached, what she said, when she says the laptop was found and handed in, and the time the report reached the company. Nothing about her beyond her name, how she can be reached and what she said is kept.
- By 11:05 he locates and locks the laptop in device management. It does not answer and cannot be located, and he writes that into the record.
- By 12:05 he ends the sessions of the account the laptop last used, which is Thea Nygård's, changes its password and revokes its access tokens in the office suite, in Nordsky Cloud and in the support desk, blocks the device in device management, and tells Nora Lind that the accounts and access are blocked.
- He tells Kari Nordmann about the lost device at the same time as he starts establishing the facts. She works on Wednesdays.
- By 14:05 the six facts stand in the record, each established or written as `not established` with its reason: the model and serial number, the encryption, the screen lock and the last contact from device management; where and when it went missing, whether it was taken or mislaid, who could have seen the screen or the password and what existed only on the laptop, from Thea Nygård; and which systems it was signed in to, with which account, and whether anything was reached from it after Monday evening, from the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud. He keeps the reading to the laptop's own entries in device management and the entries of that one account in the window from when the laptop went missing until its access was blocked.
- By 14:05 he tells Thea Nygård, in the same answer in which he asks her for those facts, what he reads about her and why: that he looks at where and when the laptop last contacted the company and at the entries of her account in the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud for that window; that this is to find out what could have been reached and nothing else; that it is not used to judge how she works; that what he finds goes into the record, which is kept 3 years from the day the case is closed or 5 years where the loss was also a personal data breach; that the entry in the equipment register naming her as the person who had the laptop is kept as long as the record and the name is then removed; and that Kari Nordmann is the data protection officer she can ask.
- By 14:05 he decides whether the laptop is wiped, and asks Kari Nordmann first because it held personal data in the office suite and the support desk. It cannot be located, and it was signed in to the support desk, each of which is a ground, so it is wiped. Before the wipe he reaches Thea Nygård, tells her the laptop will be wiped and asks her what exists only on it, writes the time of that first attempt into the record, and writes her answer and what work was lost into the record.
- By 14:05 Kari Nordmann decides from the established facts whether the loss is a personal data breach, and her decision, her name, her reason and the time go into the record whether the answer is yes or no.
- Where the facts show the laptop was taken rather than mislaid, Thea Nygård reports the theft to the police with Marius Tangen's help within 24 hours of 10:05 and the police case number goes into the record. Where it cannot be established whether the laptop was taken, that stands in the record as `not established` with its reason.
- The laptop was worth more than 10,000 kroner, so Tomas Berg tells the insurer within 3 working days, and the record says the insurer was told.
- Within 2 working days of 10:05 Marius Tangen gives Thea Nygård a replacement laptop and her access back, and tells Nora Lind that she could not work and for how long. The replacement goes into the equipment register and the lost laptop is marked there as lost, with the date.
- Marius Tangen writes into the record that the case is closed, once she is working again, the record is complete and the breach question has been decided.

## Pass

The record holds an entry timed no later than 11:05 saying that the woman at the lost property office was told, naming the telephone number she was reached on, and holding all four things: that her name, how she can be reached and what she said stand in the record; that this is so the company can show how it handled the loss and from what moment; that the record is kept 3 years from the day the case is closed, or 5 years where the loss was also a personal data breach, and is then deleted; and that Kari Nordmann is the data protection officer she can ask, with Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold when she is away. The record holds of her only her name, that telephone number and what she said, and nothing else. It holds 10:05 on Wednesday 25 November 2026 as the moment the loss reached the company, with every deadline counted from it and none counted from Monday evening: the attempt to locate and the lock by 11:05 with a line saying the laptop could not be located, the sessions ended, the password changed, the tokens revoked and the device blocked by 12:05 with Nora Lind told, Kari Nordmann told when the facts were started, the six facts by 14:05 each established or marked `not established` with a reason, the telling to Thea Nygård by 14:05 in the same answer that asked her for the facts, a wipe decision by 14:05 with Kari Nordmann asked beforehand and the time of the first attempt to reach Thea Nygård, what existed only on the laptop and what work was lost, Kari Nordmann's breach decision with her name, her reason and the time, the insurer told within 3 working days, and the replacement laptop and the access given within 2 working days with Nora Lind told. The equipment register shows the replacement and the lost laptop marked as lost with the date.
