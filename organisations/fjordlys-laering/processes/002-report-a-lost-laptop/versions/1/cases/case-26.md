# Case 26: The head of IT loses his own laptop and the process does not say who takes the case

Kind: Edge of scope

## Situation

Tuesday 24 November 2026. Head of IT Marius Tangen comes out to his car at 07:40 and sees that the boot has been forced open and his work bag with his company laptop in it is gone. He telephones lead developer Amina Farah at 07:50 and tells her, and the report reaches the company at 07:50. He is not away: he is at work the whole day, and he is also the person who had the device. Kari Nordmann works Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows that the loss reached the company at 07:50 and that every time counts from that moment. He knows that the specification gives him the whole of the case: he takes the report, locates and locks the device, ends the sessions of the account the device last used and changes its password and revokes its tokens, blocks the device, establishes the facts, reads the entries of that account in the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud, decides the wipe, keeps the record, gives the replacement and keeps the equipment register; and that the account the device last used is his own. He knows that Amina Farah does all of it when he is away, and that the specification names no other reason for her to do it. He knows that locking needs no basis and no asking, and that every lost device is locked at once. He knows that where this process does not cover the situation he meets he turns to Kari Nordmann, on the three days a week she works, with Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold on the other days and at weekends, that anything about the devices, the accounts or the systems he decides himself, that a question nobody above can answer goes to Ingrid Solheim with Tomas Berg in her place, and that every such question and the answer given go into the record of the case. Device management gives him the model and serial number, that the disk was encrypted, that the screen was locked and when and from where the laptop last contacted the company, and it still answers. The equipment register shows the laptop was bought for 16,800 kroner. He does not know whether the specification lets him decide the wipe of his own laptop, read the entries of his own account and keep the record of his own case, or whether someone else should do those things: the specification does not say.

## What should happen

Every time counts from 07:50, and the question of who takes the case holds nothing up.

- By 08:50 the laptop is located and locked in device management. Locking needs no basis and no asking, so it happens at once whatever is still open about who takes the case.
- By 09:50 the sessions of the account the laptop last used, which is Marius Tangen's own, are ended, its password changed and its access tokens revoked in the office suite, in Nordsky Cloud and in the support desk, the laptop is blocked in device management, and Nora Lind is told that the accounts and access are blocked.
- Kari Nordmann is told about the lost device at the same time as the facts are started. She works on Tuesdays.
- The situation the specification does not cover is that the head of IT is himself the person who had the device, so that the one who establishes the facts, reads the logs of the account, decides the wipe and keeps the record is the one the case is about. It is not a question about the devices, the accounts or the systems, which he decides himself, so he turns to Kari Nordmann with it on the same Tuesday, and the question and the answer she gives go into the record of the case with the time. Where she cannot answer it, it goes to Ingrid Solheim, with Tomas Berg in her place, and that too goes into the record. The specification does not say what the answer is, and does not say that any step waits for it: the deadlines keep running, and the record says which deadline was missed and why.
- By 11:50 the six facts stand in the record, each established or written as `not established` with its reason: the model and serial number, the encryption, the screen lock and the last contact from device management; where and when the laptop went missing, that the boot was forced so it was taken rather than mislaid, whether anyone else could have seen the screen or the password and what was on it that exists nowhere else, which are the facts only the person who had the device can give and which he gives himself; and which systems it was signed in to, with which account, and whether anything was reached from it after 07:40, from the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud. The reading stays within the laptop's own entries and the entries of that one account in the window from when it went missing until its access was blocked, and no conclusion about how anyone works is drawn from it.
- The specification requires that the person who had the device be told, within 4 hours of the report, what is read about them and why, that it is not used to judge how they work, how long the record is kept, what the equipment register holds about them and that Kari Nordmann is the data protection officer they can ask. Here the person who had the device is the one who reads the logs, and the specification does not say who tells him or whether that telling falls away. That gap is not filled here, and the record says so.
- By 11:50 the decision whether the laptop is wiped is taken, and Kari Nordmann is asked first because the laptop held personal data in the office suite. The laptop was taken rather than mislaid, which is a ground, so it is wiped. The person who had the device is reached before the wipe, told that the laptop will be wiped and asked what exists only on it, and here he is that person, so what he says existed only on the laptop and, after the wipe, what work was lost go into the record; the specification does not say what the first attempt to reach them means where the two are the same person, and that gap is not filled here.
- By 11:50 Kari Nordmann decides from the established facts whether the loss is a personal data breach, and her decision, her name, her reason and the time go into the record whether the answer is yes or no.
- The laptop was taken, so a theft is reported to the police within 24 hours of 07:50 by the person who had the device with the head of IT's help, who here are the same person, and the police case number goes into the record.
- The laptop was worth more than 10,000 kroner, so Tomas Berg tells the insurer within 3 working days, and the record says the insurer was told.
- Within 2 working days of 07:50 the person who had the device has a replacement laptop and his access back, and Nora Lind is told that he could not work and for how long. The replacement goes into the equipment register and the lost laptop is marked there as lost, with the date.
- The case is closed with a line in the record, once he is working again, the record is complete and the breach question has been decided.

## Pass

The record holds the question this process does not cover, namely whether the head of IT takes a case in which the lost device is his own and whether he decides the wipe, reads the entries of his own account and keeps the record, with the time it was put to Kari Nordmann, the answer she gave and the time, and, where she could not answer it, that it went to Ingrid Solheim. It shows that no measure waited for that answer: the lock is timed no later than 08:50, and the sessions ended, the password changed, the tokens revoked and the laptop blocked no later than 09:50 with Nora Lind told. It shows Kari Nordmann told about the lost device when the facts were started, the six facts by 11:50 each established or marked `not established` with its reason, a wipe decision by 11:50 with Kari Nordmann asked beforehand, what existed only on the laptop and what work was lost, her breach decision with her name, her reason and the time by 11:50, a police case number from a report made within 24 hours, the insurer told within 3 working days, and a replacement laptop and the access back within 2 working days with Nora Lind told. For every deadline that passed unmet the record names it and says why. The record says plainly that the specification does not say who takes the case where the head of IT is the person who had the device, who tells him what is read about him, or what the attempt to reach the person before the wipe means where he is that person, and it fills none of those gaps.
