# Case 1: Laptop left in a train luggage rack

Kind: Ordinary path

## Situation

Tuesday 24 November 2026. Support adviser Hanne Riise gets off the Oslo train at 10:20 and sees on the platform that her company laptop is still in the luggage rack of a train that has left. She telephones head of IT Marius Tangen at 10:40, in the same hour she noticed it, and the report reaches the company at 10:40.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows the time the report reached the company, that the laptop was mislaid and not taken, and where and when it went missing. Device management shows him the model and serial number, that the disk is encrypted, that the screen locks after five minutes, and that the device last contacted the company at 09:55 from Oslo Central Station, and it still answers, so he can locate and lock it. The sign-in logs of the office suite and the Nordsky Cloud access logs will tell him which systems it was signed in to and whether anything was reached from it after 10:20. The equipment register shows the laptop was bought for 14,200 kroner. He does not yet know whether anyone else could have seen the screen or the password, or what was stored on it that exists nowhere else; only Hanne Riise can tell him that.

## What should happen

Every time counts from 10:40.

- By 11:40 he locates and locks the device in device management, without asking anyone.
- By 12:40 he ends Hanne Riise's sessions, changes her password and revokes her access tokens in the office suite, Nordsky Cloud and the support desk, blocks the device in device management, and tells Nora Lind that the accounts and access are blocked.
- When he starts establishing the facts he tells Kari Nordmann about the lost device, whatever he thinks the answer will be. She works on Tuesdays.
- By 14:40 the six facts are established and written into the record: the model and serial number, the encryption, the screen lock and the last contact from device management; where and when it went missing, taken or mislaid, who could have seen the screen or the password and what existed only on the device from Hanne Riise; and which systems it was signed in to, with which account, and whether anything was reached from it after 10:20, from the sign-in logs of the office suite and the Nordsky Cloud access logs. Anything that cannot be established is written as `not established` with the reason.
- By 14:40 he decides whether the device is wiped. The sign-in logs show it was signed in to the support desk, which is one of the grounds, so it is wiped. Because the laptop held personal data in the office suite and the support desk, he asks Kari Nordmann before the wipe.
- Before the wipe Hanne Riise is told where she can be reached and is asked what exists only on the device, and that goes into the record, and work that existed only on the device is recorded as lost.
- By 14:40 Kari Nordmann decides from the six facts whether the loss is a personal data breach, and her decision, her name, her reason and the time go into the record whether the answer is yes or no.
- The device was mislaid, not taken, so there is no report to the police.
- The equipment was worth more than 10,000 kroner, so Tomas Berg tells the insurer within 3 working days and the record says it was told.
- Within 2 working days of the loss Marius Tangen gives Hanne Riise a replacement laptop and her access back, and tells Nora Lind.
- The replacement goes into the equipment register and the lost laptop is marked there as lost, with the date.
- The process ends when she is working again, the record is complete and Kari Nordmann has said whether the loss was a personal data breach.

## Pass

The record shows: the lock in device management timed no later than 11:40; the sessions ended, the password changed, the tokens revoked and the device blocked no later than 12:40, with Nora Lind told; Kari Nordmann told when the facts were started; all six facts written by 14:40, each either established or marked `not established` with a reason; a wipe decision by 14:40 naming who decided, with Kari Nordmann asked before the wipe; what Hanne Riise said existed only on the device and what was lost; Kari Nordmann's decision with her name, her reason and the time; that the insurer was told within 3 working days; and that the replacement laptop and the access were given within 2 working days with Nora Lind told. The equipment register shows the replacement and the lost laptop marked as lost with the date.
