# Case 20: No address reaches the former employee who must be told

Kind: Missing knowledge

## Situation

Wednesday 25 November 2026. Head of people Nora Lind telephones an employee who left the company in October 2026 and never handed back her company laptop. In that call, at 14:40, the former employee says the laptop was taken in a burglary at her flat on 18 November and that she told nobody. Nora Lind telephones head of IT Marius Tangen at 15:00 and the report reaches the company at 15:00. The laptop still appears in device management and answers. The former employee's account was closed when she left. The equipment register shows the laptop was bought for 13,900 kroner. The same evening Nora Lind writes to her at the private email address in the employment files and it bounces, posts the same letter to the private postal address the files hold, which comes back on 2 December marked that she has moved, and finds that the private telephone number in the files no longer answers.

## Who acts

Head of people, Nora Lind.

## What the person knows

She knows that the person who had the device has left the company, so she is the one who sends her in writing, within 24 hours, the same thing Marius Tangen would tell an employee about what the company reads about them and why, at the private email address or the private postal address the company holds for her in the employment files she keeps. She knows what the former employee said in the call at 14:40: that the laptop was taken in a burglary on 18 November. She knows she gives what the company knows in place of the facts only that person could give: who had the device, when she left, what she handed back and what she said about it then. She does not know any address that reaches the former employee: the private email address bounces, the letter to the private postal address comes back and the private telephone number no longer answers, and the employment files hold no other address.

## What should happen

Every time counts from 15:00, the moment the loss reached the company, and runs around the clock.

- By 16:00 Marius Tangen locates and locks the laptop in device management, and by 17:00 he blocks the device there. The account of the person who had the device was already closed, so he writes into the record that it was closed and when, in place of ending the sessions, changing the password and revoking the tokens.
- He tells Kari Nordmann about the lost device at the same time as he starts establishing the facts. She works on Wednesdays.
- By 19:00 the six facts stand in the record. The four from device management and the ones from the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud he establishes himself; in place of the facts only the person who had the device could give, Nora Lind gives what the company knows, namely that this former employee had the laptop, that she left in October 2026, what she handed back and that she said in the call of 14:40 that it was taken in a burglary on 18 November. Anything that is left is written as `not established` with its reason.
- By 19:00 Marius Tangen decides whether the laptop is wiped, having asked Kari Nordmann first because it held personal data. It was taken rather than mislaid, which is a ground for a wipe, so it is wiped. There is no person behind the device, so there is nobody to tell beforehand and it is wiped at once rather than after any waiting time, and the record says who decided it, why and when.
- By 19:00 Kari Nordmann decides whether the loss is a personal data breach and writes the decision into the record with her name, the reason and the time, whether the answer is yes or no.
- By 15:00 on Thursday 26 November 2026, which is 24 hours from the report and not a working-day count, Nora Lind sends the former employee in writing, at the private email address or the private postal address in the employment files, in plain language: that the company looks at where and when the device last contacted it and at the entries of her account in the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud, for the window from when the laptop went missing until its access was blocked; that this is to find out what could have been reached, and nothing else; that it is not used to judge how she worked; that what is found goes into the record of the case, which is kept 3 years, or 5 years where the loss was also a personal data breach; and that Kari Nordmann is the data protection officer she can ask about it.
- The private email bounces and the letter comes back, so no address the company holds reaches her. Nora Lind writes that into the record together with what was tried and when, and the telling is not dropped: it is sent at the first address the company later has.
- The laptop was taken and there is no person behind the device, so Marius Tangen reports the theft to the police himself, with the serial number from the equipment register, within 24 hours of 15:00, and writes the case number into the record.
- The laptop was worth more than 10,000 kroner, so Tomas Berg tells the insurer within 3 working days. No replacement is given, because there is no person behind the device. Marius Tangen marks the laptop in the equipment register as lost, with the date.
- The specification says the process ends when the person is working again, the record is complete and the decision whether the loss was a personal data breach stands in the record, and it does not say what the first of those three means where no employee had the device. That gap is not filled here.

## Pass

The record holds an entry timed no later than 15:00 on Thursday 26 November 2026 saying that Nora Lind sent the former employee what the company reads about her and why, naming the address it was sent to, and holding all five things: the device's last contact and the entries of her account in the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud for the window from when the laptop went missing until its access was blocked, that it is only to find what could have been reached, that it is not used to judge how she worked, that it goes into the record kept 3 years or 5, and that Kari Nordmann is the data protection officer she can ask. It then holds a line saying that no address the company holds reached her, with what was tried and when: the email that bounced, the letter that came back on 2 December and the telephone number that no longer answers, and that the telling is still to be sent at the first address the company later has. The telling is nowhere written off as impossible. The record also shows the lock by 16:00 and the block by 17:00, a line saying the account was already closed and when, what Nora Lind gave in place of the facts only the person who had the device could give, the six facts by 19:00 each established or marked `not established` with its reason, a wipe decision by 19:00 with Kari Nordmann asked beforehand and no entry saying anyone was told before the wipe, Kari Nordmann's breach decision with her name, her reason and the time by 19:00, a police case number from a report Marius Tangen made himself with the serial number within 24 hours, and that the insurer was told within 3 working days. The equipment register marks the laptop as lost with the date and holds no replacement for it.
