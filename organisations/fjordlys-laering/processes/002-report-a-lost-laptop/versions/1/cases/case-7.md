# Case 7: A hotel reports the laptop while its user is on a flight

Kind: Late or wrong notice

## Situation

Wednesday 25 November 2026, 11:00. Support lead Petter Dahl telephones head of IT Marius Tangen to say that a hotel in Bergen has written to the support desk that a company laptop was handed in at the reception that morning. The equipment register shows the laptop is the one given to head of customer success Silje Haugland, who is on a flight to a conference and cannot be reached until 15:00. She has reported nothing. The report reaches the company at 11:00.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows the laptop is at the hotel reception and that the equipment register says it is Silje Haugland's. Device management gives him the model and serial number, the encryption, the screen lock and when and from where the device last contacted the company, and the sign-in logs of the office suite and the Nordsky Cloud access logs tell him which systems it was signed in to and whether anything was reached from it. He does not know where and when it went missing, whether it was taken or mislaid, whether anyone else could have seen the screen or the password, or what was stored on it that exists nowhere else: those are the facts only the employee can give, and she cannot be reached until 15:00. He knows the specification has the employee who lost the device notice and report it, and that it does not say whether the trigger changes when someone else reports the loss instead; what it does fix is that the clock starts when the report reaches the company, which was 11:00.

## What should happen

Every time counts from 11:00.

- By 12:00 the device is located and locked in device management. Locking needs no basis and no asking, so it does not wait for Silje Haugland.
- By 13:00 her sessions are ended, her password changed, her access tokens revoked in the office suite, Nordsky Cloud and the support desk, and the device blocked, and Nora Lind is told.
- Kari Nordmann is told about the lost device when the facts are started. She works on Wednesdays.
- By 15:00 the six facts are established and written into the record. The facts that only the employee can give are written as `not established` with the reason that she could not be reached, rather than left blank, and are filled in when she lands.
- By 15:00 Marius Tangen decides whether the device is wiped, and asks Kari Nordmann first because the laptop held personal data. Before any wipe Silje Haugland is told where she can be reached and asked what exists only on the device, and that goes into the record; the wipe is not held back more than an hour because she cannot be reached.
- By 15:00 Kari Nordmann decides from the established facts whether the loss is a personal data breach, and her decision, her name, her reason and the time go into the record whether the answer is yes or no.
- The device has been found, so the case is closed with a line in the record saying that it was found, when, where and whether anyone else could have used it in the meantime. Nothing already done is undone until Marius Tangen says the device is safe to use again.
- Within 2 working days of the loss Silje Haugland has a laptop and her access back, and Nora Lind is told.

## Pass

The record shows 11:00 as the time the loss reached the company and that Petter Dahl, not the employee, reported it. It shows the lock by 12:00 and the account measures and the block by 13:00 with Nora Lind told, neither of them waiting for Silje Haugland. It shows the six facts by 15:00, with each fact only she could give marked `not established` and the reason, and filled in afterwards. It shows a wipe decision by 15:00 with Kari Nordmann asked first, her breach decision with her name, her reason and the time by 15:00, and a closing line saying the laptop was found at the hotel, when and whether anyone else could have used it. The block and the revocations stand until Marius Tangen has said in the record that the device is safe to use again. The record says plainly that the specification does not say whether a loss reported by someone other than the employee changes the trigger.
