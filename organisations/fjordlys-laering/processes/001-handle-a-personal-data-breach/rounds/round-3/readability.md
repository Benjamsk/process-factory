# readability review of draft-3.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Step 7: the step asks Kari Nordmann to decide that a case Marius Tangen judged not to be a breach is closed, and says she must agree before it is closed, but it never says what she does, or what happens to the case, when she does not agree. The draft must say what happens then, for example that the case goes on as a confirmed breach from step 8. spec.md does not say it either.
- [medium] Steps 8 and 10: "the AI support assistant goes into hand-over" and "switch the AI support assistant into hand-over" use "hand-over" before the draft explains it; the only explanation, that it hands the case over to a human, comes at step 12. The draft must explain hand-over where the reader first meets it, in step 8.
- [medium] Step 11, last sentence, and "Who must be told", the last bullet inside the company: "Silje Haugland and Petter Dahl are telephoned at home at once" names nobody who telephones them, and the sentence says "whenever support is closed on the subject of the breach" while it stands inside a step that applies only outside working hours. The draft must name the person who telephones them and say that this also holds in working hours. spec.md (step 9) does not name that person either.
- [medium] Steps 11 and 13: for working hours no one is told to put anything into the support desk. Step 11 gives the standing holding message to the on-call developer outside working hours only, and step 13 has Ingrid Solheim decide the wording while requiring that the sentences "exist as actual sentences in the support desk within 4 hours of the report". The draft must name the person who puts the holding message and the agreed sentences into the support desk in working hours. spec.md (step 10) does not name that person either.
- [medium] Steps 1, 3, 10, 11, 12 and 16 make what the reader does depend on "working hours" or "outside working hours", and the draft never says which hours those are; the only hours it gives are the hours Silje Haugland and Petter Dahl are reachable, Monday to Friday 08:00 to 16:00, under "Who does what". The draft must say which hours count as working hours. Neither spec.md nor the organisation files define them.
- [medium] "Who does what", the data protection officer bullet, step 5 and "When to hand over": the reader is told to telephone Henrik Aas on "the firm's emergency number" but not where to find that number, while the on-call telephone number is said to be in the staff handbook. The draft must say where the number is. Neither spec.md nor the organisation files give the number or say where it is kept.
- [medium] "When this applies", the bullet on pupil data in the reading app, where Datatilsynet is first named: the draft never says what Datatilsynet is, although the reader has to act on deadlines towards it. The draft must explain it where the reader first meets it; the organisation files call it "Datatilsynet, the Norwegian data protection authority".
- [medium] "When this applies", the bullet on the processors' reporting times: Hjelpedesk AS, Lønn Norge AS, Anthropic, Rekrutt AS and Microsoft are given reporting times without the reader being told what each of them supplies, while Nordsky Cloud is explained in the paragraph above. The draft must say which system each one supplies: Hjelpedesk AS the support desk, Lønn Norge AS the payroll system, Anthropic the AI support assistant, Rekrutt AS the hiring system and Microsoft the office suite.
- [medium] Step 17: "the pupil side and the controller side" are new names for the two situations that "When this applies" calls pupil data in the reading app, where the company is the processor, and teacher, support desk, employee and applicant data, where the company is the controller. The draft must use one name for each of the two situations everywhere.
- [medium] Step 16 calls it "the facts on one page" and "the page", while steps 20 and 26 and "When to hand over" call it "the fact page": the same page has two names. The draft must give the page its name in step 16 and use that one name everywhere.
- [medium] Steps 25 and 27: step 25 says each measure gets one named person and a date, and step 27 says the dates on the measures are set within 2 weeks of the case being closed, so the reader cannot tell when the date on a measure is set. The draft must say it in one place only. spec.md holds the same two statements (steps 18 and 20) and does not resolve it.
- [medium] Step 27 asks for more than one action: set the dates on the measures, and close the measures. It also carries Kari Nordmann's six-monthly check of the list and what happens to an overdue measure. Each of these must be its own step.
- [medium] Step 18 asks for three actions by three people: Ingrid Solheim decides what the message says, Kari Nordmann writes the part about the data, and Silje Haugland sends the message. Only the sending has a time, so the reader who must send within 24 hours cannot tell when the two things the sending waits for have to be done. Each must be its own step with its own time.
- [low] Step 3, last sentence: "head of product Jonas Vikane and head of customer success Silje Haugland are told the moment it happens" does not read as an instruction to the reader. It must say that Marius Tangen tells Jonas Vikane and Silje Haugland the moment it happens.
- [low] Step 21, second sentence: "This is one decision together with your decision on Datatilsynet, not a second one ... and it does not move to another person depending on whose data leaked" is a reason the reader does not need in order to act. It is enough to say that Ingrid Solheim takes both decisions at the same time, at the latest 48 hours after the report.
