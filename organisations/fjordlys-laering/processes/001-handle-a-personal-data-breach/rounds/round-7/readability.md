# readability review of draft-8.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] `Steps`, steps 1 to 3: the reader who meets the document in a running incident starts at step 1 and is told to keep a standing configuration and a standing holding message ready and to review them every six months, none of which can be done now, so they have to jump to step 4 before they can do steps 1 to 3. The draft must say, before step 1, that steps 1 to 3 are done in advance and before any breach, and that a report that has just arrived starts at step 4.
- [high] `Steps`, step 17: the reader decides whether pupils, teachers or school owners are affected or the breach is likely to become publicly known, and the draft only says what follows where the answer is yes (step 18). It never says what to do where the answer is no: whether steps 18 to 24 fall away and the case goes on at step 25, or whether the holding message and the sentences support may use are still put in. The draft must say what the reader does where the answer is no.
- [medium] `Steps`, step 5: "take the case within 1 hour of the report" names no action the reader can do, only a result; the reader does not know what taking the case consists of. The draft must say what the head of IT actually does within that hour. `spec.md` does not say either: step 5 and the deadline list only say that the head of IT has the case within 1 hour.
- [medium] `Steps`, step 7: the step asks for four actions at once - make a folder for the case in the office suite, put the secured copy there, give the right to open it to the five named people and no others, and give nobody else access without the agreement of the data protection officer - besides the ban on email, print, private device, private account and memory stick. Each action must be its own step.
- [medium] `Steps`, step 18: the one act has two names in the same step: the support lead is told to "switch the AI support assistant into hand-over" and the on-call developer to "switch on the standing configuration". The draft must use one name for it in step 18, in step 19, in step 20 and in `What to record`, where it is called "switched into hand-over" again.
- [medium] `Steps`, step 12: the step tells the head of IT to tell the data protection officer (Kari Nordmann) within 4 hours, but not what to do when the report comes on a day she does not work or at a weekend. That Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold stands in for her then stands only under `Who must be told` and `When to hand over`. The step must say it where the reader acts on it.
- [medium] `Steps`, step 21: the step tells the on-call developer to telephone the head of customer success and the support lead "at once" but does not say what sets it off. That the call is made when a breach is confirmed outside working hours stands only under `Who must be told`. The step must carry its condition.
- [medium] `Steps`, step 22: the step gives the managing director no time by which the sentences support may use must be decided, while step 23 makes the support lead put them into the support desk within 4 hours of the report. The reader of step 23 cannot tell whether the 4 hours can be met. The draft must say by when the decision in step 22 is taken. `spec.md` gives no time for the decision either; it puts the 4 hours on the sentences standing in the support desk.
- [medium] `Steps`, step 26: "the fact page" is used here for the first time, and the reader is told to own it and sign it off without being told what it is or where it is kept; only step 28 shows what goes on it. The draft must say at step 26 what the fact page is and where it is written. `spec.md` does not explain it either; it only lists what the page holds.
- [medium] `Steps`, step 27: the person responsible for each affected system must deliver the facts "within 12 hours of being asked", but no step tells anyone to ask them, so the reader waiting for the request and the reader who has to meet the 24 hours of step 31 both have no starting point. The draft must say who asks them and when. `spec.md` does not say who asks either.
- [medium] `When this applies`, and steps 7, 11 and 16: "the record" is used from the first paragraph on - the case is closed "with a line in the record", the folder is made "beside the record", times are written down - without the reader being told what the record is or where it is kept, which stands only under `What to record` at the end of the document. The draft must say what the record is where the reader first meets it.
- [medium] `When this applies`: Datatilsynet is named from the first section on and is never explained, so a reader who is not the data protection officer does not know who is being notified. The draft must say at the first mention that Datatilsynet is the Norwegian data protection authority, which is what `organisation/roles.md` calls it.
- [low] `Steps`, steps 4, 6, 8, 18, 20, 21, 26 and `When to hand over`: "the on-call developer (Erik Moen this week)" can be read as the developer on call in the week of the incident or as Erik Moen, who holds the week beginning 23 November 2026 named under `Who does what`; a reader in another week may telephone the wrong person before reaching the rota. The steps would be clearer pointing to the weekly rota in the staff handbook, with the name as the holder of that one week.
- [low] `Steps`, steps 9, 18, 23, 57 and 58: "he" in "The lead developer (Amina Farah) decides when he is away" and "The head of customer success (Silje Haugland) does both when he is away" points back over the named stand-in to the person being stood in for, and the reader has to read the sentence again to see it. Naming the person who is away would say it at once.
