# Answers 2

## 1. Who decides that a reported case is not a personal data breach and may be closed with a line in the record, and must Kari Nordmann agree before it is closed?

Kari Nordmann decides, and yes, she must agree before it is closed. Marius Tangen can say "this is not a breach" as a technical judgement, but he cannot close it. The reason is that the one who found the problem and the one who would be blamed for it should not be the one who decides it does not count. Kari writes the line in the record herself, with her name and the reason, so that if Datatilsynet ever asks why a case was closed there is a name on it. When Kari is away it is Henrik Aas at Aas & Wold. Marius may not close a case even if it looks obvious to him.

## 2. Who establishes the facts of the breach and writes the one page that Kari Nordmann must have within 24 hours?

Marius Tangen owns the page and signs it off, and Amina Farah does the work in the systems that fills it: the Nordsky logs, which pupils and which school owners are affected, the time window. When Marius is away Amina owns it and Jonas Vikane signs it off. Kari may ask for anything more she needs for her advice, and the page is not finished until she says she has what she needs. Outside working hours the on-call developer starts the page with what is known in the first hours, and Marius or Amina takes it over.

## 3. The times run around the clock, but Silje Haugland and Petter Dahl are reachable only Monday to Friday 08:00 to 16:00. Who puts the sentences support may use into the support desk, and who makes the AI support assistant hand over the breach cases, when a breach is reported in the evening or at the weekend?

This is the hole in our setup and the process has to plug it, not describe it. Two things. First, the support desk and the AI support assistant are closed to the subject: at the moment Marius confirms there is a real breach, the on-call developer switches the AI support assistant into hand-over for breach cases, using a standing configuration that Petter Dahl prepares in advance so that nobody has to write it at midnight, and puts a standing holding message into the support desk, which Kari and I approve in advance as part of this process and not during the incident. Second, Silje Haugland and Petter Dahl are called at home at once anyway, and if neither answers it is Silje who gets escalated to me. Petter owns the standing configuration and the standing holding message and must review both every six months; the on-call developer only has to switch them on. When the real wording from me and Kari is ready, in working hours, it replaces the standing message.

## 4. When Fjordlys Læring AS is the controller itself, so for teacher data, support desk data, employee data or applicant data, who decides whether the people affected are told about the breach, and who tells them?

I decide, after advice from Kari, exactly as for the notification to Datatilsynet - the decision is the same decision and should not sit with a different person depending on whose data leaked. Who tells them depends on who they are: Silje Haugland tells teachers and school contacts, Nora Lind tells employees and job applicants, and Kari writes the part of the message that describes the data and what the person can do, in every case. When I am away it is Tomas Berg. And one rule: the message goes out within 24 hours of my decision, the same promise we give the school owners in the contract, because it would be indefensible to be slower to our own people than to our customers.
