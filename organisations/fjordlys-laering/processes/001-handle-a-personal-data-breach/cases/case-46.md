# Case 46: A sharing link left open on the contract archive

Kind: Edge of scope

## Situation

On a Thursday at 09:00 Marius Tangen goes through the sharing settings of the office suite and finds that the folder that is the contract archive has had a link open to anyone who has it since June, five months. The folder holds the subscription contracts with the 310 school owners, with the names and work contact details of the school contacts who signed them, the data processor agreements, and the employment contracts of all 48 employees. The sharing log does not show whether anyone outside the company opened the link. No pupil data of the reading app is in the folder.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows what the folder holds and that he is responsible for the office suite and for the contract archive, which is a folder in it, and that he is therefore also the person responsible for establishing the facts in both. He knows that Thursday is one of Kari Nordmann's three working days. He does not know whether anyone outside the company opened the link, how long each contract has been in the folder or which of the school contacts and employees whose data it holds are still at their posts; the sharing log does not say.

## What should happen

The process covers a personal data breach of all personal data in all the company's systems, and the contract archive is one of them, so this is a case like any other, with one record and one timeline, and the process also starts on a suspicion that personal data may have been exposed. The ways the specification lists for a report to reach Marius Tangen do not name the head of IT finding it himself; what it does require is that every time counts from the moment the company became aware, which is 09:00 on the Thursday, and that the case is opened and run from there. He opens the record and writes in it the time, what he found and that he has taken the case, decides whether the breach may still be running and closes the open link, and writes in the record which systems may be touched, which are the office suite and the contract archive, all within 1 hour, and he writes what was done to stop the breach, what was not done and why. He gives his technical judgement whether this is a real personal data breach, and he may not close the case even where it looks obvious to him: Kari Nordmann decides whether a reported case is not a personal data breach, must agree before it is closed, and writes that line in the record herself with her name, the reason and the time. The step that secures the logs names the Nordsky Cloud access logs, and the specification does not say what is secured where the breach is in the office suite and the logs are the sharing log of that suite; the gap is not filled here. He asks the person responsible for every system his record names as possibly touched within 2 hours of the report, which for the office suite and the contract archive is himself, and the facts are the categories of data touched, whose data and how many people, the time window and what the logs of that system show, given within 12 hours of being asked, so that the fact page is finished within 24 hours of the report; he owns the fact page and signs it off, and it is not finished until Kari Nordmann says she has what she needs. Within 4 hours of the report he tells Kari Nordmann and Ingrid Solheim, and at the same time Nora Lind, because employee data in the office suite is touched, and Silje Haugland, because school contact data is touched. School contacts are among the people affected, so support is closed on the subject: Petter Dahl switches on the standing configuration and puts the standing holding message into the support desk and writes down when, the sentences support may use are decided by Ingrid Solheim within 3 hours after Kari Nordmann's check and stand in the support desk within 4 hours, and Nora Lind prepares what managers may say to staff who ask. No pupil data of the reading app is in the folder, so there is no message to the school owners under the data processing annex; the company is the controller for the employee data and the school contact data, so Ingrid Solheim decides at the latest 48 hours after the report whether Datatilsynet is notified, after advice from Kari Nordmann and after asking Marius Tangen what happened in the systems, and where the fact page cannot establish the risk the breach is notified, at the latest 72 hours after the company became aware. Where the fact page shows a high risk for the school contacts or the employees, those people are told: Silje Haugland tells the school contacts and Nora Lind the employees, with the part about the data and what the person can do written by Kari Nordmann.

## Pass

The record names 09:00 on the Thursday as the moment the company became aware, says what was found and that Marius Tangen took the case, and names the office suite and the contract archive as the systems that may be touched. The open link was closed and the record says so by 10:00. Kari Nordmann, Ingrid Solheim, Nora Lind and Silje Haugland had the case by 13:00. A fact page owned and signed off by Marius Tangen exists by 09:00 on the Friday, names the categories of data, whose and how many people, the window and what the sharing log shows, says that whether anyone outside the company opened the link is not established and why, and states for each kind of data whether the company is the controller or the processor. The record says when the standing configuration was switched on and the standing holding message put in, and the sentences support may use stood in the support desk by 13:00. No message went to a school owner about its pupils, because no pupil data of the reading app was in the folder. Ingrid Solheim's decision on Datatilsynet stands in the record with its reason, her name and the time by 09:00 on the Saturday, and the case was not closed without Kari Nordmann's agreement.
