# Case 47: The fix is ready before the logs are secured

Kind: Conflicting rules

## Situation

On a Monday at 11:00 a developer telephones Marius Tangen: since a release on the Friday, a page of the reading app has returned the read-aloud recordings of pupils at any school to anyone who knows the address, without logging in. The developer says he has a fix ready that he can put in production in ten minutes, that it rewrites the storage configuration the exposed addresses point to, and that he can delete the exposed copies of the recordings at the same time. He asks whether to push it now. The Nordsky Cloud access logs of the past days have not been exported.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows what the developer told him and that the exposure has been running since the Friday. He knows the recordings are pupil data of the reading app, so the company is the processor and the school owners are the controllers, and he knows the Nordsky Cloud access logs are kept 90 days and roll over, and that evidence has been lost that way before. He does not know how many recordings were fetched, by whom, or which school owners the pupils belong to; the logs have to be read for that.

## What should happen

Two rules of the specification pull in different directions. The breach is stopped within 4 hours of the report, by closing the access, revoking the token, locking the account or taking the reading app or a part of it offline. At the same time the Nordsky Cloud access logs are exported and copied out of the normal 90-day retention within 4 hours of the report, before anything is fixed in production and before anything is deleted, and nobody deletes anything, nobody fixes the bug in production before the logs are secured, and nobody emails the school owners before Kari Nordmann has looked at the case. The specification settles the order: the logs go first and the fix waits for them, because both have the same 4 hours and the logs cannot be got back once they are gone, and the exposed copies of the recordings are not deleted at all. So Marius Tangen tells the developer to hold the fix, exports and copies the logs, makes the folder for the case in the office suite, puts the copy in it, gives the right to open it to himself, Amina Farah, Kari Nordmann, Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold while he stands in for her, and Ingrid Solheim and nobody else without Kari Nordmann's agreement, and writes in the record that he made the folder and set those rights, where the logs are kept and that they hold personal data; the copy is never sent by email, never printed and never put on a private device, a private account or a memory stick. Then the fix goes in, inside the 4 hours, and he decides which of closing the access, revoking the token and locking the account fit this breach, and takes the reading app or a part of it offline only where there is no narrower way that stops the breach within those 4 hours. He writes in the record what was done to stop the breach, what was not done and why. The clock starts at 11:00. Within 1 hour he has answered the person who reported it, opened the record with the time of the report, who reported it, what they said and that he has taken the case, decided whether the breach may still be running and written which systems may be touched. Within 2 hours he asks Amina Farah, as the person responsible for the reading app and Nordsky Cloud, for the facts in them, and she gives them within 12 hours of being asked. Within 4 hours Kari Nordmann and Ingrid Solheim have the case, Monday not being one of her three working days, so Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold has it instead on the firm's emergency number, and at the same time Silje Haugland is told because pupil data is touched. Pupils and school owners are affected, so support is closed on the subject. The fact page is finished within 24 hours of the report and says, among the rest, whether anyone actually accessed the recordings, which means what the Nordsky Cloud logs show, and where the logs cannot tell, that it cannot be ruled out and that it is treated as accessed. Every affected school owner is told within 24 hours of 11:00 and before Datatilsynet is notified.

## Pass

The record shows that the Nordsky Cloud access logs were exported and copied out of the 90-day retention before any fix went to production and before anything was deleted, with the time, and that the fix went to production after that and the breach was stopped by 15:00 on the Monday. No copy of the exposed recordings was deleted, and the record says what was done to stop the breach, what was not done and why. The secured copy lies in the folder Marius Tangen made for the case, whose rights are the five people named in the process, and the record says that he made the folder and set those rights, where the logs are kept and that they hold personal data; the copy was not sent by email, not printed and not put on a private device, a private account or a memory stick. Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold, Ingrid Solheim and Silje Haugland had the case by 15:00, and Amina Farah was asked for the facts by 13:00. The fact page exists by 11:00 on the Tuesday and says what the logs show about whether the recordings were fetched, or that it cannot be ruled out and is treated as accessed. Every affected school owner received its own message by 11:00 on the Tuesday, and nothing went to a school owner before Kari Nordmann or Henrik Aas had looked at the case.
