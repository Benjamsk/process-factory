# Handle a personal data breach

## When this applies

A confirmed leak or exposure of personal data starts this process, and so does a suspicion that personal data may have been exposed. An unexplained access in the Nordsky Cloud logs, a bug report that one teacher could see another school's pupils, a lost laptop and a phishing click by an employee all start it. Run the process ten times too often rather than once too late: the 72 hours start when the company became aware, and aware is earlier than certain. The process decides early whether the case is a real breach; where it is not, it is closed with a line in the record and that is the end of it.

Every report ends up with head of IT Marius Tangen. The clock starts when the report reaches the company, not when someone gets round to reading it. Where Marius Tangen cannot be reached, the on-call developer (Erik Moen) takes the report and wakes him.

A report reaches him in one of these ways:

- An employee reports a suspected security incident. Every employee must report one to the head of IT the same day.
- A processor reports a breach of its own:
    - Nordsky AS (Nordsky Cloud), to Marius Tangen by email and telephone within 24 hours.
    - Hjelpedesk AS (the support desk), within 48 hours.
    - Lønn Norge AS (the payroll system), to head of finance Tomas Berg within 24 hours.
    - Aas & Wold (the data protection officer stand-in service), within 24 hours.
    - Anthropic (the AI support assistant), without undue delay, with no fixed number of hours.
    - Rekrutt AS (the hiring system), without undue delay, with no fixed number of hours.
    - Microsoft (the office suite, through the tenant administrator Marius Tangen), without undue delay, with no fixed number of hours.
- A teacher or a school owner writes to support. The support person who sees it telephones Marius Tangen the same hour and does not only tag the case.
- An outsider, such as a researcher or a parent, writes to the company's general address or to managing director Ingrid Solheim. Whoever receives it forwards it to Marius Tangen and telephones him.

The process covers all personal data in all the company's systems: the reading app, Nordsky Cloud, the support desk, the AI support assistant, the payroll system, the hiring system, the office suite and the contract archive. One process, not one per system.

Decide in the first minutes which of these two you are in, because the rest differs:

- Pupil data of the reading app. The pupils are children, the company is the processor and the 310 school owners are the controllers. The company tells the affected school owner, and the school owner notifies Datatilsynet.
- Teacher data, support desk data, employee data and applicant data. The company is the controller itself and notifies Datatilsynet directly.

One breach that touches both, as a breach in Nordsky Cloud would, is one case, one record and one timeline, with both duties running at the same time. The deadline that binds the case is the earliest one on the list of duties.

The process does not cover what the school owner decides for its own pupils: whether Datatilsynet and the parents are told about them.

Every time in this process runs around the clock, every day of the week, and not in working hours only. Working hours are Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 16:00, the hours in which Silje Haugland and Petter Dahl are reachable; every other hour, including every evening, every weekend and every public holiday, is outside working hours. A step that falls partly on each side belongs to whoever holds it at the moment the step has to be done. Every time counts from the moment the report reached the company, unless another moment is named.

## Steps

Do not work through these steps one after another to the end: several of them run at the same time, and what binds each one is the deadline written in it. The earliest come first: the case taken within 1 hour of the report (steps 6 to 9); the people responsible for the systems that may be touched asked within 2 hours (step 30); the sentences support may use decided within 3 hours (step 26); and within 4 hours the logs secured (step 10), the breach stopped (step 15), Kari Nordmann and Ingrid Solheim told (step 18) and the sentences support may use standing in the support desk (step 27).

## What to record

One breach, one document, written while it happens and not reconstructed a month later. Keep a record for every reported case, including the ones that turn out not to be a breach and the ones that are not notified.

The record holds:

- When the report reached the company and who reported it, what they said and that Marius Tangen took the case.
- Which systems the record names as possibly touched, whom Marius Tangen asked for facts and when.
- The timeline with real timestamps.
- What data was affected and whose, and how many people.
- What was done to stop the breach, what was not done and why.
- What the Nordsky Cloud logs showed.
- Who was told and when, and the message that went to the school owners.
- The decision whether the case is a personal data breach, with the reason, the name of the person who took it and the time, written by Kari Nordmann herself.
- The decision on Datatilsynet, with the reason, the name of the person who took it and the time, whether the answer was yes or no, and where it was yes the sent notification, the time it was sent and the receipt from Datatilsynet.
- The decision whether the people affected are told, with the reason, the name of the person who took it and the time, whether the answer was yes or no.
- That Marius Tangen made the folder for the case and set the rights on it, where the secured copy of the logs is kept and that it holds personal data, any access given to anyone else with Kari Nordmann's agreement, and why the copy was kept longer than 12 months where it was.
