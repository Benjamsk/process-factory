# Case 13: Rekrutt AS reports to the head of people

Kind: Late or wrong notice

## Situation

On a Monday at 15:30 Rekrutt AS sends an email to Nora Lind: a fault in the hiring system let applicants open the CVs, references and interview notes of other applicants for two days, and the fault was closed that morning. The email goes to her alone, because she is the one Rekrutt AS deals with. No pupil, teacher, support desk or employee data is involved. Nora Lind reads the email at 15:40.

## Who acts

Head of people, Nora Lind.

## What the person knows

She knows what Rekrutt AS wrote and that the hiring system holds the name, email address, telephone number, CV, references and interview notes of about 700 applicants a year. She knows that the agreement with Rekrutt AS obliges it to report a breach without undue delay with no fixed number of hours, so the agreement does not tell her how long ago Rekrutt AS became aware. She does not know how many applicants could open the documents of other applicants, or whether any of them did; only Rekrutt AS can tell her that.

## What should happen

Every report, from inside or outside the company, ends up with Marius Tangen, and whoever in the company receives a report from a processor forwards it to him and telephones him. Nora Lind does both rather than only forwarding the email. The clock of the process starts when the report reached the company at 15:30, not when she got round to reading it, and Marius Tangen has the case within 1 hour of 15:30. He stops the breach within 4 hours and writes in the record the time of the report and who reported it. He tells Kari Nordmann and Ingrid Solheim within 4 hours, and in the same step and at the same time he tells Nora Lind, because the breach touches the hiring system, so that she starts her part without waiting for anyone to think of her. Support is not closed on the subject, because the breach affects only job applicants and a holding message would tell 6,000 teachers about something that has nothing to do with them. The company is the controller for applicant data, so Ingrid Solheim decides at the latest 48 hours after the report whether Datatilsynet is notified, after advice from Kari Nordmann and after asking Marius Tangen what happened in the systems, and the decision goes into the record with its reason, her name and the time whether the answer is yes or no; where the fact page cannot establish the risk the breach is notified, and the notification goes at the latest 72 hours after the company became aware. Nora Lind prepares what managers may say to staff who ask, as actual sentences and not as improvisation. Where the fact page shows a high risk for the applicants, they are told: Ingrid Solheim decides the wording and the way they are reached after advice from Kari Nordmann, Nora Lind tells them, and Kari Nordmann writes the part that describes the data and what the person can do. No school owner is told, because no pupil data of the reading app is involved. The step that secures the logs is about the Nordsky Cloud access logs, and the specification does not say what is secured when the breach is in a system a processor runs and the company has no logs of its own. The gap is not filled here.

## Pass

The record names 15:30 on the Monday as the time of the report and Rekrutt AS as the one who reported it. Nora Lind forwarded the email to Marius Tangen and telephoned him, and he had the case by 16:30. The record shows that Marius Tangen told Kari Nordmann, Ingrid Solheim and Nora Lind by 19:30, so Nora Lind was told in the same step and not afterwards. No holding message went into the support desk and the AI support assistant was not switched into hand-over. Ingrid Solheim's decision on Datatilsynet is in the record with its reason, her name and the time by 15:30 on the Wednesday, and if the answer was yes the notification was sent within 72 hours of 15:30 on the Monday. Nora Lind's sentences for managers exist in writing before any manager answers a member of staff. If the applicants were told, Nora Lind told them within 24 hours of Ingrid Solheim's decision, with the part about the data and what the person can do written by Kari Nordmann.
