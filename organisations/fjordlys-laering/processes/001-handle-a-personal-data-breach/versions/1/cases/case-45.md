# Case 45: A stolen laptop and nobody knows what was on it

Kind: Missing knowledge

## Situation

On a Wednesday at 07:40 an employee telephones Marius Tangen: her work laptop was taken from her bag on the train the evening before and she found the bag open that morning. She uses the laptop to make exports from the reading app and from the support desk for the schools she works with, and she cannot say which of those exports were still on it. She was signed in to the reading app and to the office suite on it when it was taken.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He has what she told him on the telephone. He knows that the information security policy requires every employee to report a suspected security incident to the head of IT the same day, and that she is reporting the morning she found out. He knows that he is the tenant administrator of the office suite and that an access token can be revoked and an account locked in Nordsky Cloud, and that he is the person responsible for the office suite and the contract archive. He does not know what was stored on the laptop, whether anyone has opened it, whether the exports held pupil or teacher data or whose, and he cannot find that out from the laptop, which is gone; the Nordsky Cloud access logs show only what was fetched through the app.

## What should happen

The process starts on a suspicion that personal data may have been exposed, and a lost laptop is one of the things that starts it, because the 72 hours start when the company became aware and aware is earlier than certain. The clock starts at 07:40, when the report reached the company. Marius Tangen answers her so that she knows the case is held and by whom, opens the record of the case and writes in it the time the report reached the company, who reported it, what they said and that he has taken the case, decides from what he has whether the breach may still be running, and writes in the record which systems may be touched, all within 1 hour; here those systems are the reading app, Nordsky Cloud, the office suite and the support desk. The Nordsky Cloud access logs are exported and copied out of the normal 90-day retention within 4 hours, put in the folder he makes for the case in the office suite, and the right to open that folder is given to himself, Amina Farah, Kari Nordmann, Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold while he stands in for her, and Ingrid Solheim and nobody else without Kari Nordmann's agreement. He decides which of closing the access, revoking the token and locking the account fit this breach, and every one that fits is done within 4 hours, and he writes in the record what was done to stop the breach, what was not done and why. He asks the person responsible for every system his record names as possibly touched within 2 hours of the report, so Amina Farah for the reading app and Nordsky Cloud, Silje Haugland for the support desk and himself for the office suite, and each gives what they found within 12 hours of being asked. He owns the fact page and signs it off and it is finished within 24 hours of the report, so by 07:40 on the Thursday, and it is not finished until Kari Nordmann says she has what she needs for her advice; Wednesday is one of her three working days, so she has the case within 4 hours. Where something cannot be established, including the consequence for a kind of person affected or the level of risk, the fact page says that it is not established and why rather than leaving it blank, and the case is handled as a high risk until it is established, so what was on the laptop stands on the page as not established and why. Kari Nordmann judges the risk for every kind of person affected separately and writes it on the fact page with the reason. Where the fact page cannot establish the risk, the breach is notified, so Ingrid Solheim's decision at the latest 48 hours after the report is taken on a fact page that cannot establish it, and the notification goes at the latest 72 hours after the company became aware and says what is not yet established. Where the fact page shows a high risk for a kind of person affected, those people are told. The specification does not say which school owners are told where it cannot be established whose pupil data was on the device: it requires every affected school owner to be told within 24 hours of the company becoming aware. The gap is not filled here.

## Pass

The record names 07:40 on the Wednesday as the time the report reached the company and the employee as the one who reported it, and Marius Tangen had the case and had written which systems may be touched by 08:40. The Nordsky Cloud access logs were secured by 11:40 and lie in the folder for the case, whose rights are the five people named in the process, and the record says that he made the folder and set those rights and that the logs hold personal data. Of closing the access, revoking the token and locking the account, every one that fitted was done by 11:40, and the record says which were done, which were not and why. The record shows that Amina Farah, Silje Haugland and Marius Tangen himself were asked for the facts in their systems by 09:40. A fact page owned and signed off by Marius Tangen exists by 07:40 on the Thursday, says that what was on the laptop is not established and why, with no blank field, and carries Kari Nordmann's risk judgement for each kind of person affected with its reason. The case was handled as a high risk while that was not established, and Ingrid Solheim's decision on Datatilsynet stands in the record with its reason, her name and the time by 07:40 on the Friday.
