# Case 38: The head of people has to tell the employees and may not open the folder

Kind: Conflicting rules

## Situation

On a Monday at 10:00 Lønn Norge AS reports to Tomas Berg that an export from the payroll system was sent to the wrong recipient, and it holds the names, national identity numbers, bank account numbers and salaries of all 48 employees. Tomas Berg forwards it to Marius Tangen and telephones him, and Marius Tangen tells Kari Nordmann, Ingrid Solheim and Nora Lind within 4 hours. By the Tuesday afternoon the fact page is final and shows a high risk for the employees, and Ingrid Solheim has decided that they are told. Nora Lind is the one who tells them, and she asks Marius Tangen to give her the right to open the folder of the case in the office suite so that she can read the fact page.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He made the folder for the case in the office suite beside the record, put the secured copy there and set the rights to it, and he knows that the five who may open it are himself, Amina Farah, Kari Nordmann, Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold while he stands in, and Ingrid Solheim. He owns the fact page and signed it off, so he knows what it says about the employees and how many are affected. He does not know what wording Ingrid Solheim decided for the message to the employees, because she decides that after advice from Kari Nordmann.

## What should happen

Two rules of the specification pull in different directions. Nora Lind tells the employees, so she needs what the fact page says about the people she must tell. At the same time only those five may open the folder, and nobody else is given access without Kari Nordmann's agreement, which goes into the record. The specification settles it: anyone else who needs the fact page, such as Nora Lind or Silje Haugland for the part about the people they must tell, is given what they need from it rather than access to the folder, and the record says what was given to whom. So Nora Lind is given that part and not the right to open the folder, and the secured copy of the logs is never sent by email, never printed and never put on a private device, a private account or a memory stick. Tomas Berg gave the categories of data touched, the number of employees affected and what the logs of the payroll system show, and nothing about any individual's health, diagnosis, reason for absence or dates of absence, and the same holds for whoever writes the fact page. Nora Lind prepares what managers may say to staff who ask, as sentences and not as improvisation. She tells the employees within 24 hours of Ingrid Solheim's decision and at the latest 72 hours after the fact page showed the high risk; Kari Nordmann writes the part of the message that describes the data and what the person can do and checks before it goes out that it says what happened, names the data protection officer with her contact details, gives the likely consequences for that person and the measures the company has taken or proposes to take; and the message holds no personal data of anyone other than the person it is sent to, carries the date it was sent and goes to each person separately. Support is not closed on the subject, because only employees are affected.

## Pass

Nora Lind was not given the right to open the folder of the case, and the five people who could open it are still Marius Tangen, Amina Farah, Kari Nordmann, Henrik Aas and Ingrid Solheim, unless the record holds Kari Nordmann's agreement for someone else, naming who was given access, by whom and when. Nora Lind received the part of the fact page she needed about the employees she had to tell, and the record says what was given to whom. The secured copy of the logs was not sent by email, not printed and not put on a private device, a private account or a memory stick, and the record says that Marius Tangen made the folder and set the rights on it. The sentences for managers existed in writing before any manager answered a member of staff. Each affected employee received a message of their own within 24 hours of Ingrid Solheim's decision, carrying the date it was sent and holding no personal data of any other employee, with the part about the data and what the person can do written by Kari Nordmann and checked by her before it went out, and nothing in it about any individual's health, diagnosis or reason for absence. No holding message went into the support desk and the AI support assistant was not switched into hand-over.
