# Case 5: Payroll breach that a newspaper is already asking about

Kind: Conflicting rules

## Situation

On a Tuesday at 08:00 Tomas Berg receives the report from Lønn Norge AS: a fault in the payroll system let payslips be sent to the wrong recipients, and the names, national identity numbers, bank account numbers and salaries of the 48 employees are in them. No pupil, teacher, support desk or applicant data is involved. At 09:00 a journalist telephones Ingrid Solheim and says she has seen one of the payslips and is writing about it today.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He has the report from Lønn Norge AS, forwarded by Tomas Berg, and he knows from Ingrid Solheim that a journalist has one of the payslips. He knows the payroll system holds the data of the 48 employees and that it is run by Lønn Norge AS, so the company is the controller for it. He does not know how many payslips went to the wrong recipients or who received them; Lønn Norge AS has to tell him that.

## What should happen

Two rules of the specification pull in different directions when Marius Tangen decides whether support is closed to the subject. It is switched on when the breach is likely to become publicly known, and the journalist makes that certain. It is not switched on for a breach of employee or applicant data alone, because that would tell 6,000 teachers about something that has nothing to do with them. The specification settles the case where he is unsure: it is switched on, because the cost of an unnecessary holding message is far lower than the cost of a support person improvising. Whatever he decides, Nora Lind prepares what managers may say to staff who ask, as sentences and not as improvisation, and she is the one who tells the employees once Ingrid Solheim has decided that the affected people are told. The company is the controller here, so Ingrid Solheim decides whether Datatilsynet is notified, at the latest 48 hours in, after advice from Kari Nordmann and after asking Marius Tangen what happened in the systems, and the notification goes within 72 hours of becoming aware. The decision, its reason and the name of the person who took it go into the record whether the answer is yes or no. No school owner is told, because no pupil data of the reading app is involved.

## Pass

The record says whether support was closed to the subject, who decided it and when, and if Marius Tangen was unsure it was switched on. Nora Lind's sentences for managers exist in writing before any manager answers a member of staff. Marius Tangen had the case within 1 hour of the report at 08:00, and Kari Nordmann and Ingrid Solheim had it by 12:00. Ingrid Solheim's decision on Datatilsynet is in the record with its reason and her name by 08:00 on the Thursday, and if the answer was yes, Kari Nordmann sent the notification through Datatilsynet's reporting form within 72 hours of 08:00 on the Tuesday, after Marius Tangen checked the technical description and Ingrid Solheim approved it. The employees were told within 24 hours of Ingrid Solheim's decision if she decided they are told, by Nora Lind, with the part describing the data and what the person can do written by Kari Nordmann.
