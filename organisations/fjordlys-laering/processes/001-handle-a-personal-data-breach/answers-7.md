# Answers 7

## 1. What does the head of IT do in the first hour after a report, so that a reader can tell what taking the case consists of?

Four things, and he has done all four within 1 hour of the report. He answers the person who reported it, so that they know the case is held and by whom. He opens the record of the case and writes in it the time the report reached the company, who reported it, what they said and that he has taken the case. He decides from what he has whether the breach may still be running, and if it may be he goes at once to securing the logs and stopping it. And he says in the record which systems may be touched, so that the people responsible for them can be asked. Where he cannot do this within the hour, the person who steps in for him does it: Amina Farah, or outside working hours the on-call developer, who does what the role can do and reaches him or Amina Farah for the rest.

## 2. Who asks the person responsible for each affected system for the facts for the fact page, and by when must they be asked?

Marius Tangen asks, because he owns the fact page, and he asks within 2 hours of the report, so that the 12 hours they have to answer and the 24 hours for the fact page both fit. He asks the person responsible for every system his record names as possibly touched, and asks a further one as soon as a new system turns out to be touched. Amina Farah asks when he is away, and outside working hours the on-call developer asks for him. Whom he asked and when goes into the record.

## 3. Who judges, for each kind of person affected, whether the breach is unlikely to be a risk for them or is likely to be a high risk, and writes that judgement on the fact page, and on whose advice?

Kari Nordmann judges it and writes it on the fact page, for every kind of person affected separately, after asking Marius Tangen what the systems show and Amina Farah what the data is; Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold judges it when she is away. The fact page carries, for every kind of person affected, one of three words written out as a sentence: that the breach is unlikely to result in a risk for them, that it is likely to result in a risk for them, or that it is likely to result in a high risk for them, each with the reason in one or two sentences. Where she cannot establish it, she writes that it is not established, and the case is then handled as a high risk until she can. Her judgement is what steps on Datatilsynet and on telling the people affected read, and Ingrid Solheim does not change it: she decides what is done about it.

## 4. When the head of finance gives the head of IT what he found in the payroll system, may he give more about the absence and the sick leave of an employee than the categories and the number of employees affected?

No. Tomas Berg gives the categories of data touched, the number of employees affected and what the logs of the payroll system show, and nothing about any individual's health, diagnosis, reason for absence or dates of absence. The same holds for everyone who gives facts about a system and for whoever writes the fact page. Where Kari Nordmann needs more than that to judge the risk or to write a notification, she asks Tomas Berg for exactly what she needs, says in the record why she needed it, and it goes no further than the people who must see it.

## 5. By when, counted from the report, must the managing director have decided the sentences support may use, so that they can replace the standing holding message?

Within 3 hours of the report, with Kari Nordmann's check done inside that time, so that Petter Dahl has them in the support desk within 4 hours of the report. Where Ingrid Solheim is not reachable in that time, Tomas Berg decides them in her place, and where neither is reachable the standing holding message stays and the record says why.

## 6. What must the notification to Datatilsynet say, beyond what the fact page already holds, and does it give the name and the contact details of the data protection officer, and of Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold when she is away?

Yes, it gives them. The notification says: what happened, as the nature of the breach, with the categories of personal data and the categories and approximate number of the people affected and the approximate number of records concerned; the name and contact details of Kari Nordmann as the data protection officer, and of Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold where he is standing in and is the one who sends it; the likely consequences of the breach; and the measures the company has taken or proposes to take, including what it has done to limit the harm. Where the notification is sent later than 72 hours after the company became aware, it says why. Where something is not yet established it says so and a supplementary notification follows when it is.

## 7. Where is the fact page written and kept: is it part of the record of the case, or a document of its own, and where?

It is a document of its own and it is part of the record: Marius Tangen writes it in the office suite, in the folder he makes for the case, the same folder the secured copy of the Nordsky Cloud access logs is kept in, and the record names it and lies beside it. The five people who may open that folder are the five already named for the logs, and anyone else who needs the fact page, such as Nora Lind or Silje Haugland for the part about the people they must tell, is given what they need from it rather than access to the folder, and the record says what was given to whom.
