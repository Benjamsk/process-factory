# Answers 3

## 1. When one breach affects both pupil data, where the company is the processor, and teacher data, where the company is the controller, as a breach in Nordsky Cloud would, is it handled as one case that runs both situations at once?

One case, one record, one timeline, both duties running at the same time. Splitting it into two cases would mean two people believing the other one had told Datatilsynet. So: Kari keeps one list of duties for the case, and on it the pupil side says "tell the 310 affected school owners within 24 hours, they notify Datatilsynet" and the teacher side says "we notify Datatilsynet ourselves within 72 hours and decide whether to tell the teachers". The deadline that binds the case is the earliest one on the list. And the fact page must say for each kind of data which of the two we are, because the mistake we would make is to see "pupil data" first, go into processor mode, and forget that the teachers are ours.

## 2. Who is responsible for carrying out the measures that stop the breach happening again, which the record of the breach must hold, and by when must those measures be in place?

Marius Tangen owns the list of measures and is answerable for it to me; each single measure gets one named person, usually Amina Farah for anything in the reading app or Nordsky Cloud, Petter Dahl for anything in support or the AI support assistant, Nora Lind for anything about employees, and a date. The dates are set within two weeks of the case being closed, not left open. Anything that could let the same breach happen again must be in place within 30 days; anything else within 90 days. Kari checks the list at her six-monthly review of the record of processing activities and tells me what is overdue, and an overdue measure goes on the agenda of our Monday leadership meeting until it is done. The honest truth is that this is where we have failed before: we fixed the bug within a day and never did the four other things we wrote down.

## 3. Are the standing holding message in the support desk and the hand-over of breach cases by the AI support assistant switched on for every breach, including one that affects no teacher and no school owner, such as a breach of the payroll system or the hiring system?

No, only when teachers, school owners or pupils are affected, or when the breach is likely to become publicly known. A payroll or hiring breach concerns our own people, and the people who have to hear about it are the employees and the applicants, not the support queue; turning on a holding message about it would tell 6,000 teachers about something that has nothing to do with them. Marius Tangen makes that call when he confirms the breach, and when he is unsure the answer is to switch it on - the cost of an unnecessary holding message is far lower than the cost of a support person improvising. For a breach of employee or applicant data the equivalent step is Nora Lind's: she prepares what managers may say to staff who ask, and the same rule applies to her that applies to support, that it exists as sentences and not as improvisation.
