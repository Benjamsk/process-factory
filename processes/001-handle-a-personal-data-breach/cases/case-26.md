# Case 26: The 48 hours and the 72 hours both fall on days the data protection officer does not work

Kind: Outside working hours

## Situation

On a Friday at 10:00 Nordsky AS reports that an export with the names, work email addresses and schools of all 6,000 teachers of the reading app lay open on the internet for a week. No pupil, employee or applicant data is affected. Marius Tangen stops it the same morning and the fact page is ready on the Saturday evening. Kari Nordmann works Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, so she is away from the Friday until the Tuesday. The 48 hours for the decision on Datatilsynet run out at 10:00 on the Sunday and the 72 hours for the notification at 10:00 on the Monday.

## Who acts

Data protection officer, Kari Nordmann. Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold steps in on the days she does not work and at weekends, on the firm's emergency number.

## What the person knows

Henrik Aas has the case from Marius Tangen, the fact page and what the Nordsky Cloud access logs show about the week the export lay open. He may receive the record and the fact page with the personal data they hold, advise on them, decide whether a case is a breach and write and send the notification to Datatilsynet on behalf of the company. He does not know whether the export was downloaded by anyone outside the company, and where the logs cannot tell, the fact page says that access cannot be ruled out and it is treated as accessed.

## What should happen

Every time in the process runs around the clock, including every evening, every weekend and every public holiday, so neither deadline waits for the Tuesday. The data is teacher data, which the company is the controller for. Ingrid Solheim decides whether Datatilsynet is notified at the latest 48 hours after the report, which is 10:00 on the Sunday, after advice from Henrik Aas in place of Kari Nordmann and after asking Marius Tangen what happened in the systems, and Tomas Berg decides when she is away. Datatilsynet is notified in every case, and the only ground for not notifying is that the fact page shows the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of the people affected; where the fact page cannot establish the risk, the breach is notified. Where the answer is yes, Henrik Aas writes the notification from the fact page and sends it through Datatilsynet's own reporting form, Marius Tangen reads it first and says whether the technical description is actually true, and Ingrid Solheim still approves it and nobody else needs to. The notification goes the moment the fact page carries enough to send it and at the latest 10:00 on the Monday, and where something is still unestablished it goes with that said in it. The decision goes into the record with its reason, the name of the person who took it and the time, whether the answer was yes or no, and where it was yes the record holds the sent notification, the time it was sent and the receipt from Datatilsynet. Where the fact page shows a high risk for the teachers, they are told, Silje Haugland tells them, and the part of the message that describes the data and what the person can do is written by Henrik Aas in place of Kari Nordmann, who is also named in the message with his contact details while he stands in. When the stand-in on this case ends, Aas & Wold deletes or returns the case data.

## Pass

The record holds Ingrid Solheim's decision on Datatilsynet by 10:00 on the Sunday, with its reason, her name and the time, taken after advice from Henrik Aas and after Marius Tangen was asked what happened in the systems, and it was not left waiting for Kari Nordmann's Tuesday. Where the answer was yes, Henrik Aas wrote the notification from the fact page, Marius Tangen read it for technical truth, Ingrid Solheim approved it and Henrik Aas sent it through Datatilsynet's own reporting form by 10:00 on the Monday, and the record holds the sent notification, the time it was sent and the receipt. Where the teachers were told, the message names Henrik Aas with his contact details in place of Kari Nordmann and carries the part about the data and what the person can do that he wrote, and Silje Haugland sent it. The record says when the stand-in on the case ended and that Aas & Wold then deleted or returned the case data.
