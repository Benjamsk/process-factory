# Case 23: The sign that hands over nearly everything

Kind: Conflicting rules

## Situation

A breach of the support desk data is confirmed on a Tuesday at 09:00 and support is closed on the subject at 09:30. By 13:00 the five support people have 180 cases in front of them that the AI support assistant has handed over, and almost none of them is about the breach: teachers write that their pupils' data will not load, that the data on the class page is wrong, that they cannot see last week's data. The word data is one of the signs the assistant hands a case over on. Two support people ask Petter Dahl to take data off the list for the rest of the day so that the queue can be worked down.

## Who acts

Support lead, Petter Dahl.

## What the person knows

He knows that the breach is confirmed, that he switched the hand-over on at 09:30 and that data is one of the signs on the list, in Norwegian and in English. He can see the 180 handed-over cases and how few of them touch the breach. He does not know how many teachers already know about the breach or what the fact page will say, and he may say nothing about the breach beyond the sentences support is given.

## What should happen

Two rules of the specification pull in different directions. The assistant keeps answering the ordinary cases that carry no sign of the breach, because switching it off would bury the support team on the worst day of the year, and here the sign data is burying the team anyway. At the same time the support lead may take nothing off the list of signs while the case runs; he may only add a word or a sign, and must write down what he added and when. The specification settles it on the second rule: data stays on the list for as long as the case runs, however many ordinary cases it catches, because a sign taken off is a case about the breach answered by a machine. The assistant answers nothing in a handed-over case and says nothing about the breach, not even that there is one. The 180 cases go to human support people, who answer within the sentences Ingrid Solheim decided and Kari Nordmann checked, which say that the company knows about it, that it is looking into it, who is handling it and when the person will hear more, and nothing else. Petter Dahl collects every question support cannot answer and brings it to Kari Nordmann once a day, so that the answers get added rather than five support people each inventing something. A question the process does not answer goes to Kari Nordmann on the three days she works, and Tuesday is one of them.

## Pass

Data was still on the list of signs for the whole time the case ran, and the record shows that nothing was taken off the list while the case ran. The record names every word or sign Petter Dahl added while the case ran, with what he added and when. No answer the AI support assistant sent in a handed-over case says anything about the breach, and the assistant is still answering ordinary cases that carry no sign. Every answer support sent stood within the sentences Ingrid Solheim decided and Kari Nordmann checked. The questions support could not answer were brought to Kari Nordmann that day.
