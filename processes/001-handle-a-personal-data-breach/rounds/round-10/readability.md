# readability review of draft-13.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Step 20: Kari Nordmann cannot decide that a reported case is not a personal data breach before she has been told about it, and the step itself says that Marius Tangen tells her under step 21, so the reader who works down the list has to do step 21 before step 20; put the decision after the step that tells her.
- [high] Step 23: the step tells Marius Tangen to telephone Petter Dahl with the decision whether support is closed on the subject and then says "Take that decision, which is yours under step 25, before you telephone", so the reader has to do step 25 before step 23; put the decision whether support is closed on the subject before the step that tells Petter Dahl the result.
- [high] Step 47: the step makes Ingrid Solheim decide whether the affected school owners are told, and nothing says what to do where she decides they are not: step 48 sends a message to every affected school owner and step 49 words it with no condition, and step 62 requires that every affected school owner has been told before the case can be closed. Say what the decision covers, as step 55 does for the people affected; spec.md says that the affected school owners are told before the company notifies Datatilsynet and that this is not a choice, because the school owner is the controller for its own pupils and its own 72 hours start when the company tells it.
- [medium] Steps 23, 25 and 32 and the third point of `Who must be told`: the reader is told to act "at the same moment as you decide that this is a real breach", "when you confirm the breach" and "where the breach is confirmed outside working hours", but no step says who confirms that a case is a real breach and when, so the reader cannot tell when these steps start; step 19 gives Marius Tangen only his technical judgement and step 20 covers only the decision that a case is not a breach. spec.md does not say who confirms the breach or when either.
- [medium] Step 13: the step holds two actions at different times, giving the five named people the right to open the folder now and taking Henrik Aas's right away when his stand-in on the case ends, which is days or weeks later; make the second one a step of its own so that the reader does not lose it inside the first.
- [medium] Step 33: the step asks Nora Lind to prepare what managers may say to staff and only then says that it is for a breach where support is not closed on the subject, so a reader whose support is closed does the step before learning that it does not apply to them; put that condition before the action.
- [medium] Steps 52 to 54 and steps 56 to 59: these steps write, send and word the notification to Datatilsynet and the messages to the people affected without saying that they are done only where the decision in step 50 or in step 55 was yes, so a reader who decided not to notify or not to tell goes on and does them; the condition stands only in step 62, which says that the notification is sent where the answer was yes and that the people told are the ones the company decided to tell. Say the condition at the first step of each of the two blocks, as steps 47 and 50 do for their blocks.
- [medium] Step 59: the content of the message to the people affected stands under Kari Nordmann, while step 57 gives that message to Silje Haugland and Nora Lind and gives Kari Nordmann only the part that describes the data and what the person can do, so Silje Haugland and Nora Lind, who have to send it within 24 hours under step 58, cannot tell who writes the other four things the message must say. Say who puts those things in the message; spec.md gives Kari Nordmann the check that they are in it before it goes out.
- [low] Step 41: the clause that names the GDPR, Article 9(2)(b) together with Article 6(1)(c), as the ground on which the company handles absence and sick leave at all is background that Tomas Berg does not need in order to give the facts; the limit on what he may name is enough.
- [low] Step 51: "Notify Datatilsynet in every case" is followed by the only ground for not notifying, so the reader has to read the step again to see that the first sentence is not absolute; say in one sentence that Datatilsynet is notified unless the fact page shows that the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of the people affected.
