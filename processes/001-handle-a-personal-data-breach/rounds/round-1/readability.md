# readability review of draft-1.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Step 9: the step first tells the on-call developer to switch the AI support assistant into hand-over "using the standing configuration support lead Petter Dahl prepared in advance" and then tells Petter Dahl to "change the configuration yourself", so the reader cannot tell whether the configuration already exists and is only switched on or has to be built now, and which of the two people acts; the step must say that outside working hours the on-call developer only switches on the standing configuration Petter Dahl prepared in advance, and that in working hours Petter Dahl changes the configuration himself and writes down when he did it, as the `AI steps` section of `spec.md` states.
- [high] Step 7: the step makes Kari Nordmann decide whether the case is closed and says she "must agree before it is closed", but says nothing about what happens when she does not agree, so the reader is left without an action in that outcome; the step must say that the case then continues as a real breach from step 8. `spec.md` does not say this either.
- [high] Step 17: "It is the same decision as the one on Datatilsynet" can be read as meaning that Ingrid Solheim already took this decision in step 15 and has nothing to decide here, or that she takes a second decision now, and in the second reading the step gives no time by which she must take it, while "Within 24 hours of your decision" then counts from a decision the reader cannot identify; the step must say plainly whether one decision covers both and, if it is a second decision, by when it is taken.
- [medium] Step 9: the step asks for five actions from two different roles in one step - switch the AI support assistant into hand-over, put the standing holding message into the support desk, change the configuration of the AI support assistant, write down when it was changed, and telephone Silje Haugland and Petter Dahl at home - and so does not begin with one role and one action; it must be split into separate steps.
- [medium] Step 9, last sentence, and `Who must be told`, inside the company, last bullet: "Silje Haugland and Petter Dahl are telephoned at home at once in any case" does not say who telephones them, so no reader knows it is their job; the sentence must name the person who makes the call. `spec.md` does not say who makes it either.
- [medium] Step 8, and `Who must be told` inside the company and `When to hand over`: "support is closed to the subject" is used as the name of a state without being explained where the reader first meets it, and "Switch it on" in the same step has no named thing to switch on, while `When to hand over` calls the same state "the standing holding message and the hand-over", so the same thing has two names; the draft must explain at step 8 what is switched on and use one name for it everywhere. `spec.md` uses the same phrase without explaining it.
- [medium] `When this applies`, and steps 2, 3 and 12: "Nordsky Cloud" is used as the place where the reader exports the access logs, revokes tokens and locks accounts without being explained where the reader first meets it, so a reader who has not worked in it does not know what system is meant; the draft must say, as `organisation/systems.md` does, that Nordsky Cloud is the cloud platform the reading app runs on, supplied by Nordsky AS, which holds the app's data and the access logs of everyone who logs in.
- [medium] Steps 1, 5 and 9, and `When to hand over`, first and third bullets: the reader is told to telephone the on-call developer, Henrik Aas "on the firm's emergency number" and Silje Haugland and Petter Dahl "at home", but no telephone number or other way of reaching any of them is given anywhere in the draft, so the reader cannot carry out the instruction; the draft must say where the numbers are. Neither `spec.md` nor the organisation files give the numbers themselves - `organisation/roles.md` says only that the on-call telephone number and the rota are in the staff handbook.
- [medium] Steps 1, 3, 9 and 12, and `When to hand over`, first bullet: the steps name Erik Moen as the on-call developer, while `Who does what` says he holds the role only in the week beginning 23 November 2026 and that the rota is in the staff handbook, which the reader does not have, so a reader in any other week cannot tell whom to call; the steps must say "the on-call developer on the rota in the staff handbook" instead of fixing one name.
- [medium] Steps 14 and 16, and `Who must be told`, first two bullets outside the company: these deadlines run from "the company becoming aware", while `When this applies` and steps 1, 2, 3, 5, 10 and 19 run from "the report", and step 4 says the 72 hours start at the time of the report, so the reader cannot tell whether the two are the same moment; the draft must use one name for that moment, which the `Deadlines` section of `spec.md` says is the moment the report reaches the company.
- [medium] Steps 18 and 20: step 18 says each measure gets "a date" and gives no time by which the list of measures must exist, while step 20 says the dates are set "within 2 weeks of the case being closed", so the reader does not know whether to put a date on each measure now or after the case is closed; the draft must say which, and by when the list itself must exist. `spec.md` holds the same two statements and does not resolve them.
- [medium] `When to hand over`, sixth bullet: "Neither Silje Haugland nor Petter Dahl answers at home: Silje Haugland is escalated to Ingrid Solheim" does not say who telephones Ingrid Solheim, and the reader cannot tell what being escalated means here or what Ingrid Solheim then does; the bullet must name the person who makes the call and say what she is asked to do. `spec.md` says no more than this.
- [low] Step 2 ("they roll over after 90 days"), step 12 ("since a read-aloud recording of a child's voice is worse than a reading level", "because that decides who must be called"), step 14 ("as the data processing annex to the subscription contract requires"), step 15 ("so that 24 hours are left to write a decent notification") and step 16 ("as the GDPR requires"): these give the reason for a rule that the reader does not need in order to act, and the data processing annex is a document the reader does not have; the reasons can be dropped.
- [low] Step 12, step 19 and step 9: each is one very long sentence chain holding many separate requirements, so the reader has to read it more than once to find the one they are on; the content of the fact page, the six things that must be in place and the duties in step 9 can each be a list.
- [low] `When to hand over`, seventh bullet ("it is switched on") and ninth bullet ("the case is not closed"): these tell the reader what happens rather than what to do; they can be written as instructions, such as "switch it on" and "do not close the case".
