# gdpr review of draft-4.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Step 23, and "Who must be told", the people affected: the draft makes telling the affected people a free decision and states the duty as "the GDPR requires the controller to consider telling the people affected". Article 34(1) requires the controller to tell the affected people without undue delay whenever the breach is likely to result in a high risk to their rights and freedoms; it is not a matter of discretion. The step must say that the affected people are told when the risk for them is high, and that Ingrid Solheim's decision is only whether the risk is high. The high-risk rule is not in `spec.md` either: its step 17, its `Duties to notify` and its `Legal basis` also say only that the controller "must consider telling the people affected".
- [medium] Step 16, the fact page: the only risk item is "the likely consequence for the children", so where the company is the controller for teacher, support desk, employee or applicant data the decisions in steps 21 and 23 are taken without the risk for those people ever being judged. Articles 33(1) and 34(1) make the risk for the people the test for both notifications, so the fact page must hold the likely consequence for every group of people the breach affects, not only the children. `spec.md` step 12 also names only the children.
- [medium] "What to record", the list of what the record of a breach holds: it records the decision on Datatilsynet with its reason and the name of the person who took it, but not the decision of step 23 on whether the affected people are told. Article 33(5) requires a decision not to tell the people to be documented with its reason and its author as well, and step 23 is a decision of its own that can end in no. The list must hold the decision on telling the affected people, whether the answer was yes or no, with its reason and the name of the person who took it. `spec.md`'s `Records` section also names only the decision on Datatilsynet.
- [medium] "When this applies", the paragraph listing the systems in scope, and steps 16, 22 and 26: a breach of the payroll system is in scope, and `organisation/systems.md` says that system holds the absence and sick leave of the 48 employees, which is health data and a special category under Article 9(1). The fact page, the notification to Datatilsynet and the message to the affected employees therefore handle health data, and Article 9(1) forbids that without a ground of Article 9(2). The `Legal basis` section of `spec.md` gives no Article 9 ground, so the ground is missing from the specification and not only from the draft.
- [medium] "What to record", the last paragraph, the secured logs: "The logs secured at the start of the case are stored where Kari Nordmann can find them again" gives neither a place nor a retention time, although `organisation/systems.md` says the Nordsky Cloud access logs hold the log-in data of everyone who uses the reading app and the platform, and step 2 takes them out of the 90-day retention. Article 5(1)(e) and (f) require a storage period and a place for a record of personal data, as the five years and the folder of the data protection officer give for the record itself. The draft must say where the secured logs are kept and for how long. `spec.md`'s `Records` section says the same sentence and gives neither.
- [medium] Step 5, and "When to hand over", Henrik Aas: on the days Kari Nordmann does not work the case is given to Henrik Aas of the external firm Aas & Wold, which means the fact page and the record, with pupil, teacher and employee data, go to someone outside the organisation who is named neither in `organisation/processors.md` nor in the `Duties to notify` section of `spec.md`. Article 28 requires an agreement before personal data is handled on the company's behalf by someone outside it. Name Aas & Wold as a recipient of the case in the draft only once the organisation context has an agreement that covers it; the agreement is missing from `organisation/processors.md`, not from the draft.
- [low] Steps 24 to 26, the message to the affected people: it holds only "the part of the message to the affected people that describes the data and what the person can do". Article 34(2) requires the message to describe the nature of the breach and to give the name and contact details of the data protection officer, the likely consequences of the breach and the measures the company has taken or proposes. The message should hold those four things as well. `spec.md` step 17 and its `Duties to notify` describe the same two parts only.
