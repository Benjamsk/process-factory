# gdpr review of draft-3.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Step 19 and "Who must be told": the draft makes notifying Datatilsynet a free decision ("decide whether Datatilsynet is notified") with no test to decide it by, so a reader may decide not to notify a breach that is a risk for the people; Article 33(1) makes the notification a duty, and the draft must say that Datatilsynet is notified unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the people, which is also how the section `Legal basis` of `spec.md` puts it ("the controller must notify the data protection authority").
- [high] Step 21 and "Who must be told": the draft makes telling the affected people a free decision with no test, so a reader may decide not to tell people whose data leaked; Article 34(1) requires that the people are told when the breach is likely to result in a high risk for them, and the draft must say that they are told in that case; `spec.md` does not say this either, as its section `Legal basis` only says the controller "must consider telling the people affected".
- [medium] Step 16: the fact page weighs the risk only for the children ("the likely consequence for the children"), although the company is the controller for teacher, support desk, employee and applicant data, and the decisions of Articles 33(1) and 34(1) in steps 19 and 21 rest on the risk for those people too; the page must hold the likely consequence for every kind of person whose data is affected, and `spec.md` names only the children in its step 12.
- [medium] "When this applies", third-to-last paragraph, and steps 16, 20 and 24: the process covers a breach in the payroll system, which `organisation/systems.md` says holds the absence and sick leave of the 48 employees, so these steps handle data concerning health, a special category of Article 9, and neither the draft nor the section `Legal basis` of `spec.md` gives a ground of Article 9 for handling it; the ground is missing from `spec.md`, not only from the draft.
- [medium] "Who does what", "When to hand over" and steps 7 and 20: Henrik Aas of the external firm Aas & Wold receives the whole case, writes the line in the record and writes and sends the notification, which gives pupil, teacher, employee and applicant personal data to someone outside the organisation; `organisation/processors.md` has no section for Aas & Wold and the section `Duties to notify` of `spec.md` does not name the firm either, so the agreement of Article 28 is missing from the organisation context, not from the draft.
- [medium] "What to record", last paragraph, and step 2: the record is kept for five years, but the logs secured in step 2 and taken out of the normal 90-day retention are only "stored where Kari Nordmann can find them again", with no time they are kept and no system they are kept in, although they hold the access data of everyone who logs in to the reading app or the platform; Article 5(1)(e) and (f) require a retention time and a named place, and `spec.md` gives neither in its section `Records`.
- [medium] "What to record": the list holds "The decision on Datatilsynet with its reason and the name of the person who took it", but nothing on the decision of step 21 on whether the affected people are told, so a decision not to tell them is recorded nowhere with its reason; the list must hold that decision with its reason and the name of the person who took it, whether the answer is yes or no (Articles 34 and 33(5)), and `spec.md` leaves it out of its section `Records` as well.
- [medium] Step 18 and "Who must be told", first bullet: the message goes "to all affected school owners at the same time", which a reader can carry out as one message addressed to all of them, so that each school owner sees the names and work addresses of the other school owners' contacts and the pupil numbers of their schools, none of which it needs; the step must say that each affected school owner is sent its own message, with the figures for its own pupils, so that no recipient sees the others (Article 5(1)(c) and (f)); `spec.md` does not say this either.
