# gdpr review of draft-2.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Step 17, and the Datatilsynet bullet of "Who must be told": notifying Datatilsynet is left as a free decision for Ingrid Solheim with no test for the answer, so an employee who follows the draft may answer no for any reason; Article 33 requires the notification unless the breach is unlikely to be a risk for the people, and the step must say that the answer is yes unless the advice from Kari Nordmann is that the breach is unlikely to be a risk for the people, and that this is the only ground for a no. `spec.md` gives no such test either: its step 15 and its section `Legal basis` only say that she decides.
- [high] Step 19: whether the affected people are told is also left as a free decision with no test, so a breach with a high risk for the people can go untold; Article 34 requires that the people are told when the risk for them is high, and the step must say that the answer is yes whenever the risk for the people is high. `spec.md` step 17 and its section `Duties to notify` give no such test either.
- [medium] Step 14, the fact page: the page judges the risk only for the children ("the likely consequence for the children"), while the decisions in steps 17 and 19 rest on the risk for every person affected, which under Articles 33 and 34 also means teachers, school contacts, employees and job applicants; the page must hold the likely consequence for each group of people whose data is affected. `spec.md` step 12 names only the children.
- [medium] "What to record", the list of what the record of a breach holds: the record holds the decision on Datatilsynet with its reason and the name of the person who took it, but nothing about the decision in step 19 on whether the affected people are told; Articles 33(5) and 34 require that this decision, and above all a decision not to tell the people, is recorded with its reason and the name of the person who took it. `spec.md` lists the same records and does not name it either.
- [medium] "What to record", last paragraph, and step 2: the secured Nordsky Cloud access logs hold personal data about everyone who logged in, and the draft says only that they are "stored where Kari Nordmann can find them again": neither a place nor a time, while the record itself is kept for five years; Article 5(1)(e) requires a storage time, so the draft must name where the secured logs are kept and how long. `spec.md` names neither in its section `Records`.
- [medium] Step 5 and the Kari Nordmann bullet of "When to hand over": the whole case, including the fact page with the data of pupils between 6 and 13, goes to Henrik Aas of the external firm Aas & Wold, who advises, decides that a case is not a breach and writes and sends the notification; Article 28 requires an agreement for that, and Aas & Wold has no section in `organisation/processors.md` and is named neither there nor in the section `Duties to notify` of `spec.md`. The agreement is missing from the organisation context, not from the draft.
- [medium] "When this applies", the scope paragraph that names the payroll system, and the bullet on Lønn Norge AS: the payroll system holds the absence and sick leave of the 48 employees, which is health data and a special category of Article 9, and the process establishes, records and notifies that data for a breach there; Article 9 needs its own ground besides the basis of Article 6, and `spec.md` gives none in its section `Legal basis`, so the ground is missing from `spec.md` and the draft writer cannot state it.
