# Answers 1

## 1. What starts the process: only a confirmed leak of pupil data, or also a suspicion that pupil data may have been exposed, such as an unexplained access in the logs?

Both. A suspicion starts it. We would rather run the process ten times too often than once too late, because the 72 hours start when we became aware, and "aware" is earlier than "certain". So an unexplained access in the Nordsky logs, a bug report that one teacher could see another school's pupils, a lost laptop, a phishing click by an employee - all of those start it. The process itself decides early whether it is a real breach or not, and if it is not, it is closed with a line in the record and that is the end of it.

## 2. How does the process start when the leak is noticed outside the company, for example by Nordsky AS, by a teacher or school owner who writes to support, or by an outsider who reports it to us?

All outside reports have to end up in the same place, with Marius Tangen, and the clock starts when the report reaches us, not when someone gets round to reading it. Nordsky AS is obliged to report to Marius within 24 hours and does so by email and phone. Hjelpedesk AS has 48 hours. If a teacher or school owner writes to support, the support person who sees it must call Marius the same hour and not just tag the case - that is the weak point today, support has reclassified this kind of case as a normal bug before. An outsider who reports it, a researcher or a parent, usually writes to our general address or to me; whoever receives it forwards it to Marius and calls him. If Marius cannot be reached, the on-call developer takes it and wakes him.

## 3. Does the process cover only pupil data from the reading app, or also a breach of teacher data, of the support desk, or of another system such as the office suite or the payroll system?

All personal data in all our systems. One process, not five. But pupil data is special in two ways and the process must show it: the pupils are children, and for them we are the processor and the 310 school owners are the controllers, so we notify the school owner and the school owner notifies Datatilsynet. For teacher data, the support desk, payroll, the hiring system and the office suite we are the controller ourselves, so there we notify Datatilsynet directly. The process must make the person reading it see which of the two situations they are in within the first minutes, because the whole rest of it differs.

## 4. What must be done at once when a leak is reported, before anyone knows how large it is, and in which order: closing the access, taking the affected part of the app offline, securing the logs, something else?

In this order. First secure the logs - Marius exports and copies the Nordsky access logs out of the normal retention, because they roll over after 90 days and we have lost evidence that way before. Second stop the leak: close the access, revoke the token, lock the account, and only take the app or a part of it offline if there is no narrower way, because that stops teaching in 310 schools and Jonas Vikane and Silje Haugland must be told the moment it happens. Third write down the time of the report and who reported it, because that is where the 72 hours start and nobody remembers it afterwards. Fourth tell Kari Nordmann and me. Do not delete anything, do not fix the bug in production before the logs are secured, and do not email the school owners before Kari has looked at it.

## 5. What must be established about the breach before the decision on notifying Datatilsynet is taken: which pupils and which data are affected, how long the data was exposed, whether it was actually accessed by anyone?

All of those, and we have to be honest about what we cannot establish. What data, which categories, how sensitive - a read-aloud recording of a child's voice is worse than a reading level. Which pupils and how many, and which school owners they belong to, because that decides who we have to call. The window: from when to when was it exposed. Whether anyone actually accessed it, which for us means: what do the Nordsky logs show, and if the logs cannot tell us, the answer is "we cannot rule it out" and we treat it as accessed. What the likely consequence for the children is. And what we have already done to stop it. Kari needs these on one page, not in a thread.

## 6. How quickly must each step inside the 72 hours happen, for example how soon after the report the data protection officer and the managing director must have the case, and are those times counted in working hours or around the clock including evenings and weekends?

Around the clock. A leak on Friday evening is the common case, not the rare one, and the 72 hours of the GDPR run on the clock, not on our opening hours. Marius must have it within one hour of the report. The logs secured and the leak stopped within four hours. Kari and I must have it within four hours - and here is the real problem: Kari works Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, so on the other days it is Henrik Aas at Aas & Wold on the emergency number, and the process has to say that out loud instead of letting the case wait for Tuesday. The facts in question 5 on one page within 24 hours. The school owners told within 24 hours of us becoming aware, because the contract says so. My decision on Datatilsynet at the latest 48 hours in, so there are 24 hours left to write a decent notification if the answer is yes.

## 7. At what point are the affected school owners told: before the notification to Datatilsynet, at the same time, or after it?

Before, and that is not a choice. The contract gives us 24 hours to the school owner, and for pupil data the school owner is the controller and has its own 72 hours to Datatilsynet that start when we tell it. If we notify first and the school owner hears about it from Datatilsynet or from a newspaper, we have broken the contract and lost the customer. Silje Haugland sends the message, I decide what it says, Kari writes the part about the data. One message to all affected school owners at the same time, not a phone call to the big ones first - we did that once with an outage and the small schools were furious.

## 8. What may the support team say to a teacher or a school owner who asks about the leak while the process is still running, and who decides that wording?

They may say that we know about it, that we are looking into it, who is handling it and when the person will hear more, and nothing else. They may not say how many pupils, whether data was accessed, whose fault it is, or that it is "probably nothing". I decide the wording, Kari checks it, and it has to exist as actual sentences in the support desk within four hours of the report, not as a rule telling support to improvise. One person owns the question list: Petter Dahl collects every question support cannot answer and brings it to Kari once a day so the answers get added, rather than five support people each inventing something.

## 9. Must the AI support assistant stop answering on its own while the process is running, or may it keep answering ordinary cases and hand over only the cases that mention the leak?

It keeps answering ordinary cases. Switching it off would bury the support team on the worst day of the year. But Petter Dahl must make it hand over every case that touches the breach, and it must hand over on the ordinary signs - words like leak, hacked, data, privacy, Datatilsynet, parent complaining - rather than on someone guessing the right keyword, and it may not say anything about the breach itself even to say it is happening. The AI use policy already says a human must decide anything about personal data or a complaint, so this is that rule applied, and the thing that must happen is that Petter actually changes the configuration and writes down when he did it.

## 10. What is recorded about each breach, including the ones that are not notified, where is that record kept and for how long?

Everything, including the ones we decide not to notify, and the reason for not notifying - that is the part Datatilsynet will ask for if they ever come, and we have nothing like it today. The record holds: when it was reported and by whom, the timeline with real timestamps, what data and whose, how many, what we did to stop it, what the logs showed, who was told and when, the decision on Datatilsynet with the reason and who took it, the message that went to the school owners, and what we changed afterwards so it does not happen again. Kari keeps it with the record of processing activities in her folder in the office suite. We keep it five years. One breach, one document, written while it happens and not reconstructed a month later.
