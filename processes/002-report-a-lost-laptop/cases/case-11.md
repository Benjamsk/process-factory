# Case 11: A private laptop used for work is stolen

Kind: Edge of scope

## Situation

Thursday 26 November 2026, 13:00. The private laptop of support lead Petter Dahl, which he sometimes used for his work email in the office suite and for the support desk, is taken from a cafe table while he pays at the counter. He telephones head of IT Marius Tangen at 13:10, and the report reaches the company at 13:10. The laptop belongs to Petter Dahl, not to the company.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows this is a private device the employee used for work, so it is covered only for what the company can do to its own systems. He knows the company does not lock or wipe a device it does not own, and that the laptop is not in device management or in the equipment register, so he cannot see its model, its serial number, its encryption, its screen lock or when it last contacted the company. He knows the sign-in logs of the office suite and the Nordsky Cloud access logs will tell him which systems were reached from it, with which account, and whether anything was reached after 13:00. What was stored on the laptop he knows only from what Petter Dahl says.

## What should happen

The device is private, so step 3 is done and steps 2, 4 and 7 are not: the device is neither located, locked, blocked nor wiped, and the record says that the device was private and what the employee says was on it.

Every time counts from 13:10.

- By 15:10 the sessions of Petter Dahl are ended, his password changed and his access tokens revoked in the office suite, Nordsky Cloud and the support desk, and Nora Lind is told that his accounts and access are blocked.
- Kari Nordmann is told about the lost device when the facts are started. She works on Thursdays.
- By 17:10 the six facts are established and written into the record. The four from device management are written as `not established` with the reason that the device is not a company device; where and when it went missing, that it was taken, who else could have seen the screen or the password and what existed only on it come from Petter Dahl; which systems were signed in to, with which account, and whether anything was reached after 13:00 come from the sign-in logs of the office suite and the Nordsky Cloud access logs.
- By 17:10 Kari Nordmann decides from the six facts whether the loss is a personal data breach, and her decision, her name, her reason and the time go into the record whether the answer is yes or no.
- The laptop was taken rather than mislaid, so within 24 hours of 13:10 Petter Dahl reports the theft to the police with Marius Tangen's help, and the police case number goes into the record.
- Within 2 working days of the loss he has his access back, and Nora Lind is told.
- The specification does not say whether the insurer is told for a device the company does not own, and it does not say whether the company gives a replacement for a private laptop. Neither gap is filled here; the record says that the device was private.

## Pass

The record shows that the device was private and what Petter Dahl says was on it; the sessions ended, the password changed and the tokens revoked in the office suite, Nordsky Cloud and the support desk by 15:10, with Nora Lind told; no entry locking, blocking or wiping the device; the six facts by 17:10 with the four from device management marked `not established` and the reason; the decision of Kari Nordmann with her name, her reason and the time by 17:10; a police case number within 24 hours; and his access back within 2 working days with Nora Lind told. The record says plainly that the specification does not settle the insurer or a replacement for a device the company does not own.
