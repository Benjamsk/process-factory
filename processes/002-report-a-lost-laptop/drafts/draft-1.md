# Report a lost laptop

## When this applies

Start this process the moment an employee cannot account for a device that held company data or could sign in to a company system. A device that is only missing counts as lost from the first minute. Do not wait to see whether it turns up. A laptop left on a train, a laptop that may be in the other jacket at home and a laptop taken in a burglary all start the process in the same way.

The clock starts when the report reaches the company. Every deadline below counts from that moment. All times run around the clock, including every evening, every weekend and every public holiday. Working hours are Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 16:00; every other hour is outside working hours, and then the loss goes to the on-call developer.

This covers every device that held company data or could sign in to a company system: the company laptops, work phones and tablets in the equipment register, and any memory stick or external disk with company data on it. It is one process for all of them, not one per kind of device. It runs from the report until the employee is working again.

A private device the employee used for work is covered only for what the company can do to its own systems: end the employee's sessions, change their password and revoke their tokens. Do not lock or wipe a device the company does not own. Record that the device was private and what the employee says was on it.

Finding the device again does not end the process by itself. Undo nothing already done until Marius Tangen (head of IT) says the device is safe to use again.

This process does not cover what `Handle a personal data breach` covers. Where Kari Nordmann (data protection officer) decides the loss is a personal data breach, that process takes the fact page, Datatilsynet, the school owners and the people affected. This one keeps the device, the accounts, the police, the insurer and the replacement.

## Steps

1. The employee who lost the device: telephone Marius Tangen (head of IT) the same hour you notice the device is missing. Outside working hours telephone the on-call developer, Erik Moen, on the on-call telephone number in the staff handbook. Do not wait for Monday and do not send an email. Where the on-call developer cannot be reached, telephone Marius Tangen on his mobile, and Ingrid Solheim (managing director) where he cannot be reached either.
2. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): locate and lock the device in device management, within 1 hour of the report. Lock every lost device at once: this needs no basis and no asking. Outside working hours the on-call developer (Erik Moen) does this at once, without asking anyone. Do not do this for a private device the company does not own.
3. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): within 2 hours of the report, end the employee's sessions, change their password and revoke their access tokens in the office suite, Nordsky Cloud and the support desk, and tell Nora Lind (head of people). Do this also where the lost device was a private one. Outside working hours the on-call developer (Erik Moen) revokes at once what the role can revoke in Nordsky Cloud, writes the time of the call and what was done into the record, and telephones Marius Tangen at once on his mobile. Marius Tangen then does the rest himself or decides what waits until working hours, and says so in the record.
4. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): block the device itself in device management, within 2 hours of the report, so that it cannot sign in again even with a new password. Do not do this for a private device the company does not own.
5. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): tell Kari Nordmann (data protection officer) about the lost device at the same time you start establishing the facts, whatever you think the answer will be. On the days she does not work and at weekends, tell Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold on the firm's emergency number.
6. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): establish six things and write all six into the record, within 4 hours of the report. Where something cannot be established, write `not established` and the reason, never a blank.
   - from device management: the model and serial number, whether the disk was encrypted, whether the screen was locked, and when and from where the device last contacted the company;
   - from the employee: where and when it went missing, whether it was taken or mislaid, whether anyone else could have seen the screen or the password, and what was stored on it that exists nowhere else;
   - from the sign-in logs of the office suite and the Nordsky Cloud access logs: which systems the device was signed in to, with which account, and whether anything was reached from it after the time it went missing.
7. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): decide within 4 hours of the report whether the device is wiped. Ask Kari Nordmann (data protection officer) first where the device held personal data, and Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold in her place on the days she does not work and at weekends. Wipe the device where any one of these is true: it cannot be located; it was not encrypted; the screen was not locked when it went missing; it was signed in to the reading app, Nordsky Cloud or the support desk; or it was taken rather than mislaid. Where none of them is true and the device is locked, encrypted and being looked for, the wipe can wait, at the latest until 72 hours from the report; write into the record who decided to wait and why. Do not wipe a private device the company does not own.
8. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): before the wipe, tell the employee where they can be reached and ask what exists only on the device, and write that into the record. Do not hold the wipe back more than 1 hour because they cannot be reached. Work that existed only on the device is lost: write into the record what it was.
9. Data protection officer (Kari Nordmann): decide from the six established facts, within 4 hours of the report, whether the loss is a personal data breach. Write the decision, your name, your reason and the time into the record whether the answer is yes or no. On the days she does not work and at weekends, Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold decides, on the firm's emergency number. Where the answer is yes, `Handle a personal data breach` starts and the two processes run alongside each other, with one record and one timeline: its 72 hours count from the moment the loss reached the company, not from this decision, and neither process waits for the other. Where the answer is no, this process runs on to its end on its own.
10. The employee who lost the device: where the device was taken rather than mislaid, report the theft to the police within 24 hours of the report, with the help of Marius Tangen (head of IT). The police case number goes into the record.
11. Head of finance (Tomas Berg): where the lost equipment was worth more than 10,000 kroner, tell the insurer within 3 working days of the report, and write into the record that it was told.
12. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): give the employee a replacement laptop and their access back, within 2 working days of the loss, and tell Nora Lind (head of people).
13. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): put the replacement device into the equipment register and mark the lost one there as lost, with the date.
14. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): where the device is found again, close the case with a line in the record saying that it was found, when, where and whether anyone else could have used it in the meantime. Undo nothing already done until you say the device is safe to use again.
15. Head of IT (Marius Tangen): the case is finished when the employee is working again, the record is complete and Kari Nordmann (data protection officer), or Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold in her place, has said whether the loss was a personal data breach.

## What to record

Keep one record per lost device, for every loss, including the ones that turn out to be harmless. Marius Tangen (head of IT) keeps it in the office suite, in the folder he makes for the case. Where the loss is also a personal data breach, it is the one record of both processes, with one timeline.

The record holds:

- who reported the loss and when they noticed it, and the time it reached the company;
- the device, its model and its serial number from the equipment register, and, for a private device, that it was private and what the employee says was on it;
- where and when it went missing, and whether it was taken or mislaid;
- the six established facts, with `not established` and the reason where one could not be established;
- what was done to the device and to the accounts, and at what time, including the time of the call and what was done where the on-call developer took it;
- who decided to wipe or to wait, with the reason and the time, and what Marius Tangen (head of IT) decided should wait until working hours;
- what the employee said existed only on the device, and what was lost;
- the decision of Kari Nordmann (data protection officer) whether the loss was a personal data breach, with her name, her reason and the time, whether the answer was yes or no;
- the police case number where there was a theft;
- whether the insurer was told;
- when the replacement was given and the access restored;
- where the device was found again, when and where, and whether anyone else could have used it in the meantime.

The equipment register holds the replacement device and marks the lost one as lost, with the date.

Keep the record 3 years from the day the case is closed, and 5 years where the loss was also a personal data breach.
