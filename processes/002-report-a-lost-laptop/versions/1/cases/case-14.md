# Case 14: An external disk with a cloud key on it, and nobody can say it was encrypted

Kind: Missing knowledge

## Situation

Wednesday 25 November 2026, 08:45. Developer Lise Berntsen telephones head of IT Marius Tangen: her rucksack was taken from her at a bus stop at about 08:20 on her way to the office, and in it was the external disk she used to move database exports out of Nordsky Cloud. A key file for a Nordsky Cloud service account was saved on the disk. The report reaches the company at 08:45. The disk is in the equipment register with its model and serial number, but device management does not hold it and nothing can reach it. She says she thinks the disk was encrypted but is not sure, and neither the entry in the equipment register nor the purchase invoice in the contract archive says whether it was an encrypted device.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows the process covers an external disk with company data on it, and that for a device device management cannot reach the steps on the device fall away because there is nothing to reach. He knows the data on it is treated as exposed from the first minute unless the person who had it says the disk was encrypted and he can confirm from the equipment register or the purchase that it was an encrypted device, and that where that cannot be confirmed it counts as unencrypted. He knows a key for a Nordsky Cloud service account was stored on the disk. He does not know whether the disk was in fact encrypted: she cannot say and neither the register nor the invoice answers it, and the disk cannot be asked. He also cannot know whether the screen was locked or when the device last contacted the company, because it is not a managed device, and the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud cannot answer for the disk itself, which signs in to no system.

## What should happen

Every time counts from 08:45.

- The steps on the device fall away, because device management cannot reach the disk: it is not located, not locked, not blocked and not wiped. The record says that the device cannot be reached from device management and why. Everything else is done.
- By 10:45 Marius Tangen ends Lise Berntsen's sessions, changes her password and revokes her access tokens in the office suite, in Nordsky Cloud and in the support desk, and tells Nora Lind that her accounts and access are blocked.
- By 10:45 he also changes or revokes the Nordsky Cloud service account key that was stored on the disk, in every system it could reach, beyond her own sessions and tokens.
- She says she thinks the disk was encrypted, but he cannot confirm from the equipment register or from the purchase that it was an encrypted device, so it counts as unencrypted and the data on it is treated as exposed from the first minute. The record says that the disk cannot be reached, why, and that it counts as unencrypted.
- He tells Kari Nordmann about the lost device at the same time as he starts establishing the facts. She works on Wednesdays.
- By 12:45 the six facts stand in the record, each established or marked `not established` with its reason: the four that come from device management because the disk is not a managed device, the ones from the sign-in logs and the access logs because the disk signs in to no system, and from Lise Berntsen where and when it went missing, that it was taken, who else could have seen what was on it and what was on it that exists nowhere else.
- In the same answer in which he asks her for those facts, and in any case by 12:45, he tells her in plain language what the company reads about her and why.
- By 12:45 Kari Nordmann decides whether the loss is a personal data breach, knowing that the data on the disk is treated as exposed, and writes the decision into the record with her name, the reason and the time, whether the answer is yes or no.
- The disk was taken, so within 24 hours of 08:45 Lise Berntsen reports the theft to the police with Marius Tangen's help, and the case number goes into the record.
- The register shows the disk was bought for 1,400 kroner, which is not more than 10,000 kroner, so the insurer is not told.
- Within 2 working days of the loss she has her access back, and Marius Tangen tells Nora Lind that she is working again and for how long she could not work.

## Pass

The record shows that the disk cannot be reached from device management and why, and that it counts as unencrypted and its data is treated as exposed, because she could not say it was encrypted and the equipment register and the purchase did not confirm it. It shows the sessions ended, the password changed and the tokens revoked no later than 10:45, with Nora Lind told, and the Nordsky Cloud service account key that was stored on the disk changed or revoked no later than 10:45 in every system it could reach. It holds no entry locating, locking, blocking or wiping the disk. It shows the six facts by 12:45 with every fact that could not be had written as `not established` with its reason and no blanks, what Lise Berntsen was told about what the company reads about her by 12:45, Kari Nordmann's decision with her name, her reason and the time by 12:45 taken on data treated as exposed, a police case number within 24 hours, no entry saying the insurer was told, and her access back within 2 working days with Nora Lind told how long she could not work.
