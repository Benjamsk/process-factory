# Case 23: The managing director holds the case for five hours on a Sunday

Kind: Outside working hours

## Situation

Sunday 29 November 2026. Support adviser Maja Eriksen is at a hotel in Tromso and at 11:00 sees that her company laptop is gone from the breakfast room, where she had it at 10:30. She telephones the on-call telephone number from the card in her wallet at 11:10 and nobody answers; the week beginning 23 November 2026 is held by Erik Moen. At 11:15 she telephones head of IT Marius Tangen on his mobile and gets no answer. At 11:20 she telephones managing director Ingrid Solheim, who answers and takes the report. The report reaches the company at 11:20. Ingrid Solheim has no access to device management, to Nordsky Cloud or to the support desk. Nobody with access to the systems answers until lead developer Amina Farah calls back at 16:00.

## Who acts

Managing director, Ingrid Solheim.

## What the person knows

She knows she takes the report where neither the on-call developer nor Marius Tangen can be reached, and that she holds the case until someone with access to the systems takes it. She knows she does four things and nothing in the systems: opens the record and writes into it the time the report reached the company, who reported it, what they said and that she is holding it; telephones, in this order and not by email, the on-call developer on the on-call number, then Marius Tangen on his mobile, then Amina Farah, and keeps trying every 30 minutes until one of them answers, writing each attempt and its time into the record; tells Maja Eriksen that the case is held and that someone will come back to her; and asks Maja Eriksen for the facts only insofar as she is on the telephone anyway, writing what she says into the record. She knows the deadlines keep running while nobody with access to the systems can be reached, and that the record says which deadline was missed and why. She knows that where four hours pass with nobody who has access to the systems reachable, she telephones Kari Nordmann, or Henrik Aas, so that the breach question is in someone's hands, and writes that into the record, and that on a Sunday it is Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold on the firm's emergency number. She cannot locate, lock, block or wipe the laptop or touch the accounts herself, and she does not know when one of the three will answer.

## What should happen

Every time counts from 11:20 and runs around the clock, through the Sunday.

- At 11:20 Ingrid Solheim opens the record and writes into it that the report reached the company at 11:20, that Maja Eriksen reported it, that she noticed at 11:00 and had the laptop at 10:30 in the hotel breakfast room, and that Ingrid Solheim is holding the case.
- She telephones the on-call number, then Marius Tangen on his mobile, then Amina Farah, in that order and not by email, and keeps trying every 30 minutes until one of them answers, and writes each attempt and its time into the record.
- She tells Maja Eriksen that the case is held and that someone will come back to her.
- She asks Maja Eriksen for the facts only insofar as she is on the telephone anyway: where and when the laptop went missing, whether it was taken or mislaid, whether anyone else could have seen the screen or the password, and what was on it that exists nowhere else, and writes what she says into the record. She does nothing in the systems.
- The deadlines keep running. The laptop is to be located and locked by 12:20, and the sessions ended, the password changed, the tokens revoked and the device blocked by 13:20. Nobody with access to the systems is reached by either time, so those deadlines are missed, and the record says which deadline was missed and why.
- At 15:20, four hours from the report, nobody who has access to the systems has been reachable, so Ingrid Solheim telephones the data protection officer so that the breach question is in someone's hands. It is a Sunday, so it is Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold on the firm's emergency number, and she writes that call and its time into the record.
- At 16:00 Amina Farah answers and writes into the record that she has taken the case. She then carries on from the step the process has reached rather than starting again from the beginning, with the record as it stands: the time of the report, the attempts and their times, what Maja Eriksen said, and what was not done. She locates and locks the laptop, ends Maja Eriksen's sessions, changes her password and revokes her access tokens in the office suite, in Nordsky Cloud and in the support desk, blocks the device, and tells Nora Lind that the accounts and access are blocked, and each of those is written into the record with its time next to the deadline it passed.
- The remaining steps are carried out from the point the process has reached: the facts are established and written into the record with `not established` and the reason where one cannot be established, Maja Eriksen is told what is read about her and why, the decision whether to wipe is taken with the data protection officer asked first where the laptop held personal data, and the decision whether the loss is a personal data breach stands in the record with a name, a reason and a time, whether the answer is yes or no. The specification requires the facts, that telling, the wipe decision and the breach decision within 4 hours of 11:20, so each of those deadlines is missed as well, and the record says so and why.
- Where the facts show the laptop was taken rather than mislaid, Maja Eriksen reports the theft to the police with Marius Tangen's help within 24 hours of 11:20 and the case number goes into the record. Where the laptop was worth more than 10,000 kroner, Tomas Berg tells the insurer within 3 working days. Within 2 working days of the loss Maja Eriksen has a replacement laptop and her access back, and Nora Lind is told that she could not work and for how long.

## Pass

The record holds, from 11:20, an entry by Ingrid Solheim with the time the report reached the company, who reported it, what she said and that Ingrid Solheim is holding the case. It holds every attempt to reach someone with access to the systems with its time, in the order on-call number, Marius Tangen, Amina Farah, repeated about every 30 minutes from 11:20 until 16:00, and no attempt made by email. It holds a line saying Maja Eriksen was told that the case is held and that someone would come back to her, and the facts she gave on the telephone. It holds no entry of anything Ingrid Solheim did in device management, Nordsky Cloud, the office suite or the support desk. It holds a line timed at or soon after 15:20 saying that four hours had passed with nobody who has access to the systems reachable and that she telephoned Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold on the firm's emergency number so that the breach question was in someone's hands. It holds Amina Farah's line at 16:00 saying she has taken the case, and after it the measures carried out from the step the process had reached, with no step repeated that was already done and nothing begun again from the beginning. For each of the deadlines counted from 11:20 that passed unmet, namely the lock by 12:20, the account measures and the block by 13:20 and the four-hour deadlines at 15:20, the record names the deadline and says why it was missed, and no deadline is recounted from 16:00. Maja Eriksen has a replacement laptop and her access back within 2 working days with Nora Lind told.
