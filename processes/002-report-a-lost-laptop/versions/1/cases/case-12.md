# Case 12: A returned laptop is gone from the IT cupboard

Kind: Edge of scope

## Situation

Monday 23 November 2026, 10:00. Head of people Nora Lind tells head of IT Marius Tangen that the laptop an employee handed back when she left the company three weeks ago, which the equipment register marks as returned on that day, is not in the IT cupboard. Nobody can say when it was last seen there or who took it out. The employee who used it has left. The laptop still appears in device management and has not been wiped or reset since it was handed back. The report reaches the company at 10:00.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows the laptop held company data and still appears in device management, which gives him the model and serial number, whether the disk was encrypted, whether the screen was locked and when and from where the device last contacted the company. He knows the equipment register marks it as returned. He does not know where and when it went missing, whether it was taken or mislaid, whether anyone else could have seen the screen or the password, or what was stored on it that exists nowhere else: those are the facts the employee who lost a device gives, and there is no such employee here. He knows the trigger of this process is an employee who cannot account for a device, and that the specification does not say whether a device the company itself cannot account for in its own cupboard starts this process.

## What should happen

The specification does not settle whether this situation starts the process, and that gap is not filled here: its trigger is an employee who cannot account for a device, and here no employee lost it. Nor does it say who gives the facts only the employee can give, who is told before a wipe, who reports a theft to the police or who gets a replacement when the employee has left the company. What the specification does require, where the process is run, counts from 10:00, the moment the loss reached the company.

- By 11:00 the device is located and locked in device management. Locking needs no basis and no asking.
- By 12:00 the device is blocked in device management, so that it cannot sign in again even with a new password. There are no sessions, no password and no tokens of a current employee to act on, and the specification does not say what step 3 means for an employee who has left.
- The data protection officer is told about the lost device when the facts are started. It is a Monday and Kari Nordmann works Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, so Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold is told.
- By 14:00 the six facts are established and written into the record: the four from device management, the ones from the sign-in logs of the office suite and the Nordsky Cloud access logs, and, for each fact only the employee could give, `not established` with the reason that there is no employee who lost the device.
- By 14:00 the decision whether the device is wiped is taken, and the record says who decided and why. The device held personal data, so the data protection officer is asked first, which on a Monday is Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold. The specification says the employee is told before a wipe, and here there is no employee to tell.
- By 14:00 Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold decides from the established facts whether the loss is a personal data breach, and his decision, his name, his reason and the time go into the record whether the answer is yes or no.
- The laptop is marked in the equipment register as lost, with the date.

## Pass

The record shows the lock by 11:00 and the block by 12:00, both counted from 10:00, the six facts by 14:00 with every fact only an employee could give marked `not established` and the reason, a wipe decision by 14:00 naming who decided and why with Henrik Aas of Aas & Wold asked first, and his decision whether the loss was a personal data breach with his name, his reason and the time by 14:00. The equipment register marks the laptop as lost with the date. The record says plainly that the specification does not say whether a device missing from the company's own cupboard starts this process, and that there is no employee to give the facts only an employee can give, to be told before the wipe, to report a theft or to be given a replacement.
