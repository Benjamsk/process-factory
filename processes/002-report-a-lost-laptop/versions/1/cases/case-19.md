# Case 19: A private laptop is stolen and the employee still needs something to work on

Kind: Edge of scope

## Situation

Tuesday 24 November 2026. There was a break-in at the flat of support adviser Maja Eriksen overnight, and her own laptop is gone. She used it at home for her work email in the office suite and for the support desk. She notices at 08:00 and telephones head of IT Marius Tangen at 08:30, and the report reaches the company at 08:30. The laptop belongs to her, not to the company, and she has no company laptop at home. Kari Nordmann works Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday.

## Who acts

Head of IT, Marius Tangen.

## What the person knows

He knows this is a private device the employee used for work, so the process covers it only for what the company can do in its own systems. He knows the company does not locate, lock or wipe a device it does not own, does not claim on its insurer for it and does not replace it, and that she claims on her own insurance and reports the theft to the police herself, with his help on what the company knows. He knows she needs a device to work. The laptop is not in device management and not in the equipment register, so he cannot see its model, its serial number, its encryption, its screen lock or when it last contacted the company. The sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud tell him which systems were reached with her account and whether anything was reached after the break-in. What was on the laptop he knows only from what she says.

## What should happen

Every time counts from 08:30.

- By 10:30 Marius Tangen ends Maja Eriksen's sessions, changes her password and revokes her access tokens in the office suite, in Nordsky Cloud and in the support desk, exactly as for a company device, and tells Nora Lind that the accounts and access are blocked.
- The device is not located, not locked and not wiped, because the company does not own it.
- He tells Kari Nordmann about the lost device at the same time as he starts establishing the facts. She works on Tuesdays.
- By 12:30 the six facts stand in the record. The four that come from device management are written as `not established` with the reason that the laptop is not a company device; where and when it went missing, that it was taken, who else could have seen the screen or the password and what was on it that exists nowhere else come from her; and which systems were signed in to, with which account, and whether anything was reached after the break-in come from the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud.
- By 12:30, and in the same answer in which he asks her for those facts, he tells her in plain language what the company reads about her and why.
- By 12:30 Kari Nordmann decides whether the loss is a personal data breach and writes the decision into the record with her name, the reason and the time, whether the answer is yes or no.
- The laptop was taken, so within 24 hours of 08:30 Maja Eriksen reports the theft to the police herself, and Marius Tangen helps her with what the company knows. She claims on her own insurance, and the company does not claim on its insurer and does not replace her laptop.
- She needs a device to work, so Marius Tangen gives her a company laptop within 2 working days, which is the company equipping her and not a replacement of her own device, and gives her her access back. He tells Nora Lind that she is working again and for how long she could not work, and puts the company laptop into the equipment register.
- The record says that the device was private, what she says was on it, and that the company could not reach it.

## Pass

The record shows the sessions ended, the password changed and the tokens revoked in the office suite, Nordsky Cloud and the support desk no later than 10:30, with Nora Lind told, and no entry locating, locking or wiping the laptop and no entry saying the insurer was told or that the laptop was replaced. It says that the device was private, what Maja Eriksen says was on it, and that the company could not reach it. It shows the six facts by 12:30 with the four from device management marked `not established` and the reason, what she was told about what the company reads about her by 12:30, and Kari Nordmann's decision with her name, her reason and the time by 12:30. It shows that she reported the theft to the police herself within 24 hours of 08:30 with Marius Tangen's help on what the company knows. The equipment register holds a company laptop given to her within 2 working days, entered as the company equipping her and not as a replacement of her own device, her access is back within the same 2 working days, and Nora Lind is told that she is working again and for how long she could not work.
