# readability review of draft-7.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Step 13: the telling must go out "in the same answer in which you ask them for the facts of step 10", so the reader who works through the steps in their order has already asked the person for the facts at step 10 without telling them anything. Step 10 must say, where it names the person who had the device as a source, that what step 13 lists goes in the same message.
- [high] Step 18: the step ends "Wipe the device where any of these is true", so the reader wipes at step 18 — three steps before step 21 tells them to reach the person first, tell them and wait an hour, and before step 23, which is the wipe itself. Step 18 must say only when the wipe is decided, and say that the device is wiped at step 23 after step 21.
- [medium] Step 12: the limit on what may be read — the device's own entries, that one account only, and the window from when the device went missing until its access was blocked — comes two steps after step 10 has already sent the reader into the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud. The window and the ban on using the logs for anything else belong in step 10, with the third source.
- [medium] Steps 5, 8, 21, 22 and 32 each ask for more than one action: step 5 for ending the sessions, changing the password and revoking the tokens, each in three systems; step 8 for writing in the record that the device cannot be reached, for confirming from the equipment register or the purchase whether the disk was encrypted, and for changing or revoking every password, key and token stored on the device within 2 hours; step 21 for reaching the person, telling them the device will be wiped, asking what exists only on it, writing the time of the first attempt and wiping an hour later; step 22 for two writings at two different times, the second only after the wipe of step 23; step 32 for giving the replacement laptop, giving the access back and telling Nora Lind. Make each action its own step, and put the writing that follows the wipe after step 23.
- [medium] Steps 8, 17 and 34 ask for a result or a stance and name no action the reader can do: "Treat the data on it as exposed from the first minute" in step 8, "answer for it in their place" in step 17, where the reader cannot tell what to answer or where to get it, and "check it and say it is safe before the person uses it again" in step 34, where the reader cannot tell what to check. Each must name the action the reader carries out; spec.md says no more than the draft does in all three places, so the draft writer cannot take the action from there.
- [medium] Steps 16, 20 and 30 start with one role and then hold an action by someone else: step 16, under head of people Nora Lind, gives head of IT Marius Tangen the asking for the facts; step 20, under Marius Tangen, gives data protection officer Kari Nordmann the writing in the record; step 30, under the person who had the device, gives Marius Tangen the police report where there is no person behind the device. Each of those actions must be its own step that starts with the role and the name of whoever does it.
- [medium] "When this applies", the paragraph beginning "A company device that device management cannot reach": "device management" is used here, and again in "Who does what", before step 4 explains that it is Microsoft Intune in the office suite subscription. The explanation belongs where the reader first meets the term.
- [medium] The draft uses these names without saying what they are, and the reader has only the draft: the office suite (step 2; the organisation files call it Microsoft 365 and use it for email and documents), Nordsky Cloud ("Who does what" and step 5; the cloud platform the reading app runs on), the support desk (step 5; where support cases from teachers and school owners are answered), the reading app (step 18; spec.md calls it the product the company sells), the equipment register (step 8) and Datatilsynet ("When this applies"; the Norwegian data protection authority). Say what each one is where the reader first meets it, in the words the organisation files use.
- [medium] "Who does what", data protection officer: "decides what is looked at where more than the window is needed" uses "the window" before step 12 says which window that is. Say which window it is here, or leave the duty to step 12.
- [medium] Step 1: "Outside working hours" decides whether the reader tells Marius Tangen or telephones Erik Moen, and the draft never says what the working hours are, so a reader who notices the loss at 16:30 cannot tell which to do. Spec.md does not say what they are either; the organisation files give Monday to Friday 08:00 to 16:00 only for the support people.
- [low] Step 8: "Treat the data on it as exposed from the first minute, not as possibly exposed, unless the person who had it says the disk was encrypted and you can confirm from the equipment register or the purchase that it was an encrypted device; where you cannot confirm that, it counts as unencrypted" is one sentence with two conditions and three different things called "it". Split it into short sentences.
- [low] Step 19: "the wipe waits" tells the reader what happens rather than what to do; write it as an instruction, such as "Do not wipe the device yet".
- [low] Step 28: "where you cannot establish that the loss is not a personal data breach, decide that it is one" negates twice. Write it as one positive instruction.
- [low] Step 20: "tell Kari Nordmann, or Henrik Aas, at the first hour one of them is reachable. She may then write in the record" — "She" can point to either of the two people just named. Write "whoever you reached".
