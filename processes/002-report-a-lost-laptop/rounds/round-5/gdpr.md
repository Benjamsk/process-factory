# gdpr review of draft-7.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] `When this applies`, the paragraph on `Handle a personal data breach`, and the closing line of `Who must be told`: the draft hands the notification of Datatilsynet and of the people affected to `Handle a personal data breach` without saying from when that notification is counted, so a reader of this document can let the breach process start its clock at the moment the breach question was decided, up to five hours after the company learned of the loss; Articles 33 and 34 count the 72 hours and the `without undue delay` from the moment the organisation became aware, and the draft must carry the sentence spec.md gives in `Deadlines`, that the 72 hours of `Handle a personal data breach` count from the moment the loss reached the company, which is the moment the company became aware, and not from the moment the breach question was decided.
- [medium] Steps 15 and 16, step 13 fifth bullet, and step 33 with the second-to-last paragraph of `What to record`: the draft has Nora Lind take the person's private email address or private postal address and what their employment file holds about them, which is when they left, what they handed back and what they said about it then, and has the equipment register keep the name of the person who had the lost device for 3 or 5 years, but the `Legal basis` of spec.md gives an Article 6 basis only for the device's own entries in device management, for the entries of that one account in the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud in the window from when the device went missing until its access was blocked, and for the reporter's name, how they can be reached and what they said; Article 6 needs a basis for the employment-file data and for the named register entry too, and spec.md gives none, so the draft writer cannot take one from spec.md and must tell the user that this finding goes back to the specification.
