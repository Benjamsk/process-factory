# readability review of draft-1.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Step 8 against step 7: step 7 already tells the reader to wipe the device, and only step 8 says that before the wipe the employee must be told and asked what exists only on the device, so a reader who does the steps in their order has wiped before they reach the step that must come first; the telling and the asking must stand in step 7 before the sentence that orders the wipe, or step 8 must stand before step 7.
- [high] Step 7, the case where none of the five grounds is true: the draft says the wipe "can wait" only where the device is besides that "locked, encrypted and being looked for", and says nothing for a device where none of the five grounds is true but nobody is looking for it, so the reader is left with no answer in that outcome; the step must say what to do in that case, and must say it as an instruction ("wait with the wipe until ...") rather than "the wipe can wait".
- [high] Step 8 and `Who must be told`, bullet 6, "tell the employee where they can be reached": this can be read as telling the employee at whatever contact point reaches them that the device will be wiped, and as telling the employee where they can be reached, which are different actions; the draft must say whom to contact, by what means and what to say, for example "reach the employee by telephone, tell them the device will be wiped and ask what exists only on it".
- [high] Step 3, `When to hand over`, bullet 2, and `If something does not fit`: "what the role can revoke in Nordsky Cloud" and "anything the role cannot do or decide" leave the on-call developer to decide in the moment what their role may do, which the draft never states; the draft must name the actions, which `organisation/roles.md` gives as revoking an access token and locking the account in Nordsky Cloud, and name what must go to Marius Tangen (head of IT) or Amina Farah (lead developer).
- [high] Step 12 against `When this applies`, paragraph 2: `When this applies` says every deadline counts from the moment the report reaches the company, while step 12 gives the replacement and the access back "within 2 working days of the loss", so for a device lost days before it was reported the reader gets two different dates; step 12 must say which moment it counts from.
- [high] Step 14 against step 15 and `When this applies`, paragraph 5: step 14 tells the reader to "close the case" when the device is found, while `When this applies` says that finding the device does not end the process and step 15 says the case is finished only when the employee is working again, the record is complete and the data protection officer has decided; step 14 must say that the line goes into the record and that the case still ends as step 15 says.
- [medium] Steps 2, 3, 6, 8, 12 and 13 each ask for more than one action: step 2 locate and lock; step 3 end the sessions, change the password, revoke the access tokens in three systems and tell Nora Lind (head of people); step 6 establish the facts from three sources and write them into the record; step 8 tell the employee, ask what exists only on the device and write it into the record; step 12 give the replacement laptop, give the access back and tell Nora Lind (head of people); step 13 put the replacement into the equipment register and mark the lost device as lost; each action needs its own step.
- [medium] Step 15 asks for no action the reader can do, only for a result ("the case is finished when the employee is working again, the record is complete and ... has said whether the loss was a personal data breach"); it must name the action, such as writing into the record that the case is closed once those three things are true.
- [medium] Step 2 ("device management", also steps 4 and 6) and step 3 ("the office suite", also step 6): the reader meets these systems without being told what they are or where to open them; `organisation/systems.md` says device management is Microsoft Intune and the office suite is Microsoft 365, and the draft must say so where the reader first meets each of them.
- [medium] Step 1 and `Who does what`, bullet 3: the reader is sent to "the on-call telephone number in the staff handbook" without being told where the staff handbook is or being given the number, which costs a search while the loss is running; neither `spec.md` nor the organisation files say where the staff handbook is kept, so the draft must give the number itself or say where the handbook is found.
- [medium] Step 9, and `Who must be told`, bullet 9, and `When to hand over`, bullet 6: "`Handle a personal data breach` starts" does not say who starts it, and the reader does not have that document; `spec.md` does not say who starts it either, so the draft must name the person who starts it and where the reader finds it.
- [low] Step 2 ("this needs no basis and no asking"), step 9 (the 72 hours of the other process and that neither process waits for the other) and `Who must be told`, bullet 5 ("because the employee cannot work until it is undone", "so that she knows the employee could not work and for how long") give reasons the reader does not need in order to act and can be cut.
- [low] `Who must be told`, bullets 6, 7 and 8 ("The employee is told", "The police are told", "The insurer is told") are written as statements of what happens rather than as instructions to the person who must tell, which the other bullets of the same list are.
