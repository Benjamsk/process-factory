# legal review of draft-9.md

Verdict: CHANGES

- [high] Who does what, the lead developer bullet: "Head of product Jonas Vikane steps in when she is away" gives Jonas Vikane everything the head of IT does, which includes deciding to lock, block or wipe the device and blocking the accounts; no rule lets anyone but Marius Tangen and Amina Farah decide those, and `spec.md` names no stand-in for Amina Farah either. The bullet must drop the stand-in for the lead developer, or limit it to what is not a decision to lock, block or wipe a device or to block accounts and access. Rule: `Lock or wipe a lost device remotely` and `Block an employee's accounts and access` in `organisation/authority.md`.
- [high] Step 24: the wipe at 72 hours is done "without asking again", so the person who had the device is not told before the device is wiped in the path where step 19 made the wipe wait and no earlier wipe was decided; the step must say that the telling and the question of step 21 come first, and that the wipe follows one hour after the first attempt to reach them. Rule: `Lock or wipe a lost device remotely` in `organisation/authority.md`, which says the employee who lost the device is told before it is wiped where they can be reached and the wipe is not held back longer than an hour because they cannot be, and `Duties to notify` of `spec.md`, which says Marius Tangen tells the person before the wipe.
- [high] Step 24: unlike steps 4, 6, 18 and 31 the step carries no "does not apply where the device is the person's own", so it tells Marius Tangen to wipe a device the company does not own once 72 hours have passed; the step must say that it does not apply where the device is the person's own. Rule: the section `Legal basis` of `spec.md`, which names no rule for wiping a device the company does not own, and whose company rule `Lock or wipe a lost device remotely` in `organisation/authority.md` covers only a company laptop or work phone.
- [high] Steps 15 and 16, and the telling about them in step 13: Nora Lind reads the private email address, postal address and employment file of the person who had the device and hands what the company knows about them to the case, and no rule covers that reading; the draft must name the basis for it. `spec.md` does not say it either: its section `Legal basis` rests the processing of the person's personal data on GDPR Article 6(1)(f) "namely" for the device's own entries in device management and the entries of that one account in the sign-in logs of the office suite and the access logs of Nordsky Cloud, and names nothing for the employment file. Rule: the section `Legal basis` of `spec.md`, which names no rule for the employment file.
